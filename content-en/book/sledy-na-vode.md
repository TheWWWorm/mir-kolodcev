Traces on the Water is a novel by Roman Surzhikov, the first book set in a new fantasy world the author himself calls the “World of Wells.” It takes place in three countries where steam engines, revolvers, slavery, and wells that grant miracles coexist. The book opens with a preface in the voice of the writer Alvaro da Silva, then shifts to third-person narration and alternates between three storylines: Alvaro in [[kataliya|Catalia]], the slave Red in a Dangarian forest, and Lieutenant Dietrich in [[dangar|Dangar]] and [[ollandiya|Ollandia]]. The protagonists do not meet, but their fates are connected by people they have in common and by the [[kolodets|wells]] themselves.

## Title
The exact phrase “traces on the water” does not occur in the novel: a search across all chapters finds no instance, and the book offers no explanation of its title. The only direct indication comes from the author. In January 2026, a viewer of his livestream asked whether Alvaro was writing a book called Traces on the Water, and the author confirmed that this was exactly the book Alvaro was writing. The novel thus becomes a “book within a book”: the writer-protagonist creates the text we read. The livestream host called this device a “book within a book”; the author did not use that label himself.

### Traces and water in the text (ch. 15, 33)
The words in the title connect with several episodes. In chapter 15, the hunter [[yakka|Yakka]] follows the fugitive [[red|Red’s]] tracks. Chapter 33 opens with a reflection on erasing the traces of death: to remove every trace of blood, one need only strip away a layer of earth as deep as a spade blade; now imagine having to dig an entire well that way. Water, meanwhile, is what people dig both [[kolodets|wells]] and pits for in all three storylines. Neither the novel nor the interviews say whether the author connects these motifs with the title.

## Structure
The novel consists of a preface (chapter 0) and 34 chapters. The preface is written in the first person, followed by third-person narration. Each chapter has its own title. The three main storylines alternate in an almost regular sequence:

- **Alvaro in Catalia**—the preface and chapters 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 16, 18, 20, 22, 24, 26, 28, 30, 32, 34 (18 parts). The settings are [[laerta|Laerta]], [[ortiyar|Ortiyar]] Fortress, and later [[tres-molinos|Tres Molinos]] in [[seleziya|Selezia]].
- **Red in the Dangarian camp**—chapters 4, 8, 12, 15, 19, 23, 27, 33 (8 chapters). This is a forest settlement of slaves, only named [[dunkvald|Dunkwald]] in the text around the middle of the book.
- **Dietrich in Dangar and Ollandia**—chapters 2, 6, 10, 14, 17, 21, 25, 29 (8 chapters). The action moves from [[ollandiya|Ollandia]] to Dangar’s capital, [[khofshtadt|Hofstadt]].

Chapter 31, “The Snake and the Tortoise,” follows Major [[klyajn|Wolfgang von Klein]] of the Kaiser’s Claws and belongs to Dietrich’s storyline: it takes place in [[latsen|Latsen]] during the Ollandian campaign. All chapters are listed in detail in the chapter table on the website.

The storylines take place at different times. According to the author, the events in Red’s storyline precede those in Dietrich’s and Alvaro’s; in his words, the storylines “will fit together in a particular way.” He declined to elaborate, calling it a spoiler. Shared characters make this visible in the text: Fischmeister [[joriksen|Joriksen]] is a young master in Red’s storyline and a renowned fisherman in Dietrich’s, while Klein is a lieutenant in Red’s and a major in Dietrich’s.

The author also discussed form: he introduces each protagonist in a different way. This is visible in the text itself: Alvaro first speaks for himself in the preface; chapter 2 introduces Dietrich through a letter from his commander assessing him; chapter 4 takes the form of a travel guide to Dangar addressed to “you.” According to the author, his priorities are plot first, conveying emotion second, and originality third.

## Plot

### Alvaro: a lost debate and a commission (ch. 0–5)
In spring in Laerta, thirty-three-year-old [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] strolls with his friends [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]] and [[lyusil|Lucille Gereira]], reformers from the Service esfera. In a restaurant near the Grand Opera, the legal master [[vinsente-ramires|Vincente Ramires]] calls his companions “obreros.” The quarrel is settled through a [[duelnyj-spor|dueling debate]] in the [[palata-slova|Chamber of the Word]] rather than a conventional duel: Alvaro loses and owes seven thousand escudos, of which he has only 4600. [[filippe|Don Filippe di Berro]], prim-allen of Art, rescues him by commissioning a book about the heroes of the Solantan expedition, who became famous sixteen years earlier, and promising to pay the debt. Alongside the four living heroes is a fifth who died two days earlier: Dr. [[khuanes|Garcia Juanes]]. Fernan volunteers to become Alvaro’s secretary, and together they inspect the dead man’s house. The butler [[emil|Emil]] and the widow believe the doctor’s heart simply stopped, but Alvaro suspects murder, although killing is forbidden on “white land.”

### Alvaro: the pirate, the general, and the finger (ch. 7–13)
The first hero of the planned book is the smuggler [[karlos|Carlos el Niña]]. In his Old Port fort, the friends endure a cruel joke with a pistol, meet his sickly wife [[izabel|Isabel]], and see an excavated [[kolodets|Well]] in the cellar, with a wilting chrysanthemum entwined in a vine at its bottom. At [[ortiyar|Ortiyar]], General [[dezerej|Gabriel Deserey]] demolishes Alvaro’s battle scenes, demonstrates the power of canister shot, and asks him to make fear of Dangar the book’s central theme. On the way, the friends notice they are being followed, and Alvaro’s pupil [[beatris|Beatriz]] gives them a ride. Back in Laerta, they find themselves at a protest by dismissed obreros, dispersed with blank cannon volleys. At home, Alvaro finds a woman’s severed finger in his notebook and a note reading “Stop this!” The law in the country of the people of the word does not punish threats, and he falls into a creative crisis, but Fernan takes him to the factory owner [[kristobal-maduro|Maduro]], where Alvaro improvises a program of reforms.

### Alvaro: threats, a stranger, and the Scroll (ch. 16–22)
At the [[filosofiya-uzhasa|Philosophy of Horror]] exhibition, Filippe makes Alvaro promise to finish the book. In [[khose-flarbo|Flarbo’s]] shop, he hears the story of the mad philosopher [[liess-van-khalm|Liess van Halm]], meets a witty stranger, and arranges a date with her. The next day, he learns that Carlos’s wife Isabel has disappeared. Returning from the fort, Alvaro is shot through the palm by an unknown man and ordered “not to write.” The Reformists take in the wounded writer at the [[tri-byka|Three Bulls]] tavern. [[mor|Mor]], chief engineer of the [[esperantsa|Esperanza]] factory, asks him to influence [[fatima|Allen Fatima]], but Alvaro believes they want to use him. He reconciles with Beatriz and makes her his assistant. Together, they develop a theory involving two antagonists and search the [[svitok|Great Scroll]] for traces of the sailor [[red|Rodrigo da Vira]].

### Alvaro: the autopsy, van Halm, and Fatima (ch. 24–28)
The stranger takes Alvaro to an autopsy in the anatomical theater. He flees the room, but it is there that he realizes how Juanes died: the doctor accidentally drank someone else’s medicine, then, through the butler’s fault, strong alcohol instead of the antidote. No one intended harm, so the “white land” taboo did not activate. At [[klinika-miloserdiya|Mercy Clinic]], Alvaro and Beatriz see van Halm himself: he has lost the ability to write, but recognizes the names of the heroes of Solanta and fears [[eliga|Eliga]]. That night, in a secret wine cellar, the stranger tells Alvaro about the supremo elections, and they spend the night together. In the morning, Alvaro overhears her conversation with Deserey and discovers that she is [[fatima|Fatima Ir-Reya]], prim-allen of Law, and that Beatriz is her daughter, who concealed their relationship. He quarrels with both women.

### Alvaro: Selezia and Last Winter (ch. 30–34)
Learning that Fernan told Mor about Beatriz, Alvaro strikes his friend and then reconciles with him. He extracts a thousand escudos from Deserey on the pretext of following a spy and presents Fernan with his theory: van Halm found a genuine well in Selezia and saw the heroes of Solanta in it. In [[tres-molinos|Tres Molinos]], they inspect the cursed House Above, learn that six Dangarians stayed with the philosopher, and Alvaro finds Isabel alive. In chapter 34, at [[posledniaya-zima|Last Winter]] Castle, he explains the mystery of the “enchanted” door and tells how da Vira became the expedition’s victim, then is captured with Fernan and Beatriz by [[salvador|Salvador]] and a second killer. In the cellar beside a dark schönbrun, Salvador is killed and the second man wounded and captured; Fatima arrives and gives her version of events. The novel ends with Alvaro and Fatima traveling to Laerta, future troubles left as “tomorrow’s Fatima’s problem.”

### Red: the pit and the well (ch. 4–12)
Red is a Catalian whom a doctor gave a year to live; he has become a slave, a “stone,” in a Dangarian forest. He wins a place in a dugout by force, digs a pit he takes for a grave, and learns from master fisherman [[joriksen|Fritjof Joriksen]] that he must dig a well in exchange for freedom and money for the journey home. The hunter [[yakka|Yakka]] guards him, his fellow work-gang members prove vicious and treacherous, and during a temple service at which free people feed slaves by hand, Red refuses to eat. He deals with the gang and introduces equal division of food in the dugout. Joriksen punishes him with twenty lashes for damaging “guild property.” When water appears at the thirteenth yard, Red receives a “prize”—a night with the enslaved [[greta|Greta]]—and realizes that freedom will not follow. He attacks Yakka and escapes into the forest.

### Red: return and the schönbrun (ch. 15–19)
Yakka follows the fugitive’s tracks and finds him half-frozen. The settlement’s new head, Baron [[landau|Landau]], demands his execution, but Joriksen persuades him that Red needs to dig a [[kolodets|schönbrun]], not an ordinary well. Red learns that a human-made miracle is expected of him and accepts the conditions: a separate dugout, night work by a fire, songs, and a letter home entrusted to Greta. He sees the entire camp digging wells in vain and believes he can succeed where others failed. Reaching water, he suddenly discovers that his cough has vanished, but the [[brigadir|foreman]] buries him under earth in the pit.

### Red: Täuschung (ch. 23–27)
Red is pulled out alive. Baron Landau, who demonstrates an oberfischmeister’s supernatural power to Klein, orders him to restore the well. When water is reached again, the baron declares it a deception. Lieutenant [[klyajn|Klein]] tells Red how Joriksen devised the entire affair: he found a foreigner unaware that a schönbrun cannot be dug and gave him reason to believe. Red escapes, kills his escort, and wrecks a supply train, sparing the young scribe; but Landau’s power stops him beside a gravestone.

### Red: the beast (ch. 33)
Red is chained in a pit near Dunkwald: the baron has imposed the Zustand of a beast on him. He becomes an executioner for condemned prisoners and digs away the blood-soaked earth until he has removed it all. Years pass, Yakka and Klein leave, and Greta confesses that she never sent his letter to Laerta, then finds herself among the condemned. In the morning, she is found dead. Army tax collectors take Red away on the recommendation of the Kaiser’s Claws.

### Dietrich: the railway-halt battle and the interrogation (ch. 2–6)
Twenty-three-year-old Lieutenant [[ditrikh|Dietrich von Dort]], commander of a platoon in an artillery battery stuck in the Ollandian rear, inspects a platoon of enslaved “stones” and speaks with the holy simpleton [[spasenie|Salvation]]. The stones flee in the night after mutilating their NCO, and in the morning Ollandian hussars attack the battery. After looking into a well, Dietrich commands the battle as though he knows its course in advance and defeats the squadron with three volleys of canister. Afterward, he kills Salvation. In [[latsen|Latsen]], he meets [[olkha|Olkha]], while Major [[klyajn|Klein]] suspects him of treason and suspends him from service.

### Dietrich: Hofstadt and fishing (ch. 10–14)
In [[khofshtadt|Hofstadt]], Dietrich encounters distrust from his wife [[mari|Mari]]: the family has no money, he has no posting, and the letter announcing his discharge never arrived. He spends almost all their savings on a ticket to Baroness [[elza|Elsa von Walbrecht’s]] “fishing” event, conducted by Joriksen. In the baroness’s schönbrun, he watches a spectacle unfold in the water. At the reception, he meets Olkha with her uncle, Prince [[lents|Wladyslaw Lenz]], and tells the master about “his own” schönbrun in Ollandia.

### Dietrich: the bargain with Joriksen (ch. 17–21)
Olkha passes Dietrich off as a cousin of her friend [[katrina|Katrina von Eyring]], wife of Count [[lurd|Ludwig von Lurd]]. Lurd explains world politics and Dangar’s centers of power to him. The evening ends with a kiss with Olkha and a quarrel with Mari. The next morning, Dietrich visits Joriksen and is offered harsh terms, including his son [[khajnts|Heinz]] as collateral; but he finds the landowner’s name, Prince Lenz, in the guild’s atlas and negotiates a different agreement.

### Dietrich: Catastrophe (ch. 25–29)
Dietrich conceals the details of the mortgage deed from Mari, pines for Olkha, and visits the baroness uninvited. He is discovered there with Olkha and Katrina, and the prince demands a duel. Count Lurd proposes marriage instead and threatens the prince with the label “unsicher.” At the height of the negotiations, the batman [[milosh|Milosh]] arrives with news from Joriksen: they must leave for Ollandia. On the train, Magnus [[amir-sedoj|Amir the Grey]] announces that everyone in Dunkwald has died.

### Klein in Latsen (ch. 31)
Major Klein attends Field Marshal [[rajniger|Reiniger’s]] conference, where General [[khofmajer|Hofmeyer’s]] army is halved, then hints that Hofmeyer should delay the courier carrying the order. He instructs Joriksen, whom he knows from Dunkwald, to investigate the dark schönbrun near the railway halt and has Dietrich arrested. Under pressure from Katrina and Olkha, the major releases his prisoner, but thinks of him as a snake riding on a tortoise’s back.

### How the storylines connect (ch. 27–33)
Red, Dietrich, and Alvaro do not meet, but encounter the same people and phenomena. Klein is the lieutenant who watched Red in Dunkwald; a year later, he is already a major handling Dietrich’s affairs, while his son [[otto|Otto]] works as Joriksen’s secretary. Joriksen experiments on Red, then takes on Dietrich’s schönbrun. Wells appear in all three storylines: in Red’s settlement, in the baroness’s mansion, in the cellar of Carlos’s fort, and later in van Halm’s castle. The novel does not explain exactly what ultimately connects all three storylines; the author called it a spoiler.

## Themes

### Inequality among the esferas
Catalia is divided into eleven [[esfera|esferas]], each with three [[alleny|allens]] at the [[vysokij-stol|High Table]], although some contain one hundred thousand people and others six million. Fernan discusses this in chapter 1, and it is also debated in the Chamber of the Word. Alvaro initially considers Catalia wisely organized, but the Chamber debate and his acquaintance with the Reformists force him to revisit the question. The theme develops into the factory strike (ch. 11–30) and the struggle for seats at the Table before the supremo elections.

### Creativity
Alvaro is a writer, and the novel shows how an idea is born and dies: lectures on the protagonist and opening premise, a creative crisis after a threat, “kicks” from a mentor and friends, love, and need. The author said that he developed the protagonist from a desire to explore creativity in fantasy and that he shares Alvaro’s philosophy in most respects.

### Miracle and faith
Dangarians expect miracles from wells and believe in the [[seyatel|Sower]]; Catalians are materialists, for whom gods exist, in the author’s words, “like muses.” These outlooks clash in Red’s storyline, where faith in a human-made miracle heals and destroys (ch. 4–27), and in Dietrich’s, where he buys a miracle with money (ch. 14–21).

### Slavery
Dangar is an empire of slaveholders: [[kamni|stones]] work for free people, while noble eagles, according to Dietrich, are equally unfree, merely bound by honor instead. Every slave is assigned a [[tsushtand|Zustand]], a kind of sentence. The author compared Red’s situation to a concentration camp. He promised to show the origins of slavery and the history of Dangar’s system in later books.

### War
From the novel’s beginning, Catalia expects an attack from Dangar; General Deserey insists on this threat (ch. 9, 28). Dietrich’s storyline shows the conquest of [[ollandiya|Ollandia]] itself through Dangarian officers’ eyes. The book does not reach the invasion of Catalia.

## The world
The action takes place on a continent shared by several states. [[kataliya|Catalia]] is a country of professional estates, or esferas, whose law rests on the spoken word, [[duelnyj-spor|dueling debates]], and the records of the [[svitok|Great Scroll]]; its capital is [[laerta|Laerta]]. [[dangar|Dangar]] is a slaveholding empire with a Kaiser, a patriarch, and the [[fishmajster|Fishermen’s Guild]]; Dietrich lives in its capital, [[khofshtadt|Hofstadt]]. [[ollandiya|Ollandia]] is a principality between them, conquered by Dangar. The novel also mentions [[mergana|Mergana]], the [[muaris|Muari Emirates]], and the island of [[solanta|Solanta]]. Magic rests on [[kolodets|wells]], of which the [[kolodets|schönbrun]] is the most precious kind. The world’s technology includes steam-powered carriages, revolvers, rifled artillery, and airships (see [[tekhnika]]); its currencies are escudos and marks (see [[dengi]]).

## Publication and conception
According to the author, Traces on the Water is the first work set in the second of the worlds he wanted to create, with the Polari series coming first. The novel was serialized on Litspace, Author.Today, and Litnet, with chapters released on Fridays and holidays, and two per week during the final month. In spring 2026, when the livestreams took place, publication was approaching completion. The author noted that typos might remain and asked readers to report them. He stressed that the novel was finished, so readers need not fear an unfinished story.

The author described aspects of its conception as follows.
- He considered Traces on the Water more dynamic and concentrated than Arrow, Coin, Spark.
- All three protagonists are men; he regretted not making one of them a woman.
- Alvaro is partly a projection of himself: he shares the character’s philosophy in most respects. Beatriz, he said, would receive further development in later novels.
- He does not write drafts, instead writing a finished version straight away, then revising and proofreading aloud; the text goes to a proofreader before print publication.
- He consulted AI for the autopsy scene; AI was also used for illustrations accompanying posts, translations, and reference information, while he writes the actual prose and world details himself.
- He is learning Spanish, which he began while inventing this world, and studied German; readers sometimes find mistakes in the German words.

Kirill Golovin is producing the audio version, whose release was promised “by the end of spring.” According to the author, a print edition will not come soon: first he will finish Fantasies, the last Polari book. Alexey Safin drew the map of the World of Wells’ three continents; only the portion where the first book takes place has been published so far, with the rest promised later.

### Sequels and the spiral of worlds
The author said that after finishing the novel, he felt drawn to continue the same characters’ story. According to him, the second World of Wells book will explore Dangarian court affairs, with the Kaiser, patriarch, and chief magnus interacting; they do not appear in Traces. The origins of Catalia’s and Dangar’s systems, the stones’ civil war, and the reasons for slavery are also reserved for future books. The author did not announce the second book’s title or release date.

According to the author, the worlds lie on different turns of a single “spiral,” Polari’s sacred symbol. It is not possible to pass physically from one world to another; the World of Wells occupies a different turn from Polari.
