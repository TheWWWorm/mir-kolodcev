The Eliste Gallery is one of the landmarks of [[laerta|Laerta]]. Marble statues of the [[mirny|mirnas]] stand on its roof, turning pink at sunset: this is the very image that opens [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro’s]] evening walk in the preface.

## The collection
The gallery is famous for its three-hundred-year-old collection: faded icons from the previous millennium, resembling children’s drawings with patches of gilding; battle panoramas from the feudal era (“The Battle of Lamertina,” “The Conquest of Brega,” “The Death of King Augustus the Pale”); portraits by Velanyes, with stiff poses but lifelike faces; and engravings by Emil Flare. Scandalous works are shown on the temporary-exhibitions floor.

## The “Philosophy of Horror” exhibition (ch. 16, 18)
In chapter 16, the gallery hosts “Philosophy of Horror: An Artist’s View of the Myths of the Ancients.” [[filippe|Don Filippe]] invites [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] and takes him from canvas to canvas: [[iolanta|Iolanta]] and [[alessina|Alessina]] argue about art, [[darredzho|Darreggio]] inserts a small steam engine into his belly, Ikhtrodan the Shipbreaker is coiled into a ring like a serpent, and the mirna of luck, [[eliga|Eliga]], is shown with a bullet hole in her temple. The exhibition shares its title with [[liess-van-khalm|van Halm’s]] unfinished book ([[filosofiya-uzhasa|Philosophy of Horror]]). According to the characters, four days before chapter 18, Doña [[izabel|Isabel]] disappeared near the gallery during this exhibition.
