The Grand Opera is a theater in the center of [[laerta|Laerta]]. In the preface, the narrator observes that on the evening the story begins, a line for tickets stretches from its steps: the posters advertise “Señorita Violet,” featuring the celebrated [[filippe|Don Filippe]], making his first stage appearance in a year.

## “Señorita Violet” (ch. 1, 3)
In chapter 3, [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] watches the performance from a box to which Filippe’s Muari servant has invited him. The plot runs as follows: the hidalgo Don Laurent loves blue-eyed Violet but seduces women of ever higher rank; the queen exiles Violet to an island, where she marries a kindly sailor; Laurent then returns with pleas, and in the classic version Violet stabs herself. Don Filippe, who sings Laurent’s part, demanded a rewritten ending: Violet takes a double-barreled pistol, it misfires twice, and she falls into Laurent’s arms. During the interval, the audience debates the ending in the refreshment rooms; most approve of the moral that dying for love is the last thing anyone should do. After the performance, Filippe, without his makeup, receives Alvaro backstage and commissions a book about his friends, the heroes of a famous exploit (see [[solantijskaya-ekspeditsiya|The Solantan Expedition]]). Later, [[karlos|Carlos el Niña]] jokes about the opera’s misfire when they meet (ch. 7).
