Mercy Clinic is a hospital for the mentally ill in [[kataliya|Catalia]]. The clinic is important to the novel because the philosopher [[liess-van-khalm|Liess van Halm]], a witness to the secret of the five heroes of Solanta, is confined there.

## The journal entry (ch. 24)
The butler [[emil|Emil]] is the first to mention the clinic. Returning from a nighttime call, Dr. [[khuanes|Juanes]] wrote in his journal: “Liess van Halm, 49. Prelim. diag.: schizophrenia. Transferred to Mercy Clinic.” When [[beatris|Beatriz]] asks where the philosopher is now, Emil replies: “At Mercy Clinic, unless they’ve moved him. Or perhaps he’s dead.” Beatriz suggests that the doctor “packed him off to a madhouse” to silence him.

## The visit (ch. 26)
Alvaro and Beatriz come to the clinic to see van Halm. Miguel, an orderly wearing a sleeveless white coat, demands twenty escudos for a visit: “That is the set price. Everyone pays it.” Alvaro argues that, as a free man of the word, he has the right to speak to another man of the word without charge, but Beatriz pays anyway.

The narrator explains the practice: Catalians are prepared to pay only for results, and a stay in a hospital does not restore reason. Thus, among the Medicine esfera’s “flourishing gardens,” institutions for the mentally ill stand like “dry straw scarecrows” and seek new ways to earn money. On the way to the ward, Miguel offers Beatriz the establishment’s “services”: she can watch pyromaniacs through fireproof glass for five escudos, bet on cockfights against the patient Marques, who believes himself to be a rooster, see Conchita’s drawings in ward ten (“art therapy”), attend Don Montego’s self-confidence classes, or have a massage from Miguel himself.

Van Halm’s room is lined with soft felt, torn in places; the window is barred, and one wall panel serves as a chalkboard. The patient is fed the cheapest rice cakes with grass, which are difficult to chew; without food, he eats chalk and felt. Miguel was on duty the night Juanes brought in the philosopher and tells the visitors the story for two escudos; he tells it to Beatriz for free. According to him, van Halm’s wounds were treated here, and no rope marks were found on his arms. For details of the meeting, see [[liess-van-khalm|Liess van Halm]].

## A later mention (ch. 30)
In ch. 30, Alvaro tells [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]] that van Halm is now in a hospital for the mentally ill, where the late Dr. Juanes placed him, and that the philosopher is “not very capable of writing… or even speaking.”
