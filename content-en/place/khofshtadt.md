Hofstadt is the capital of [[dangar|Dangar]], “Mighty Hofstadt,” as the narrator calls it in chapter 10. It stands on the River Wunde, which swells to a width of half a verst in spring and teems with barges and steamships, but shrinks to almost a stream by late summer. Once a year, for roughly a month, the river disappears entirely. This month is called **Gehai**, meaning “sacred”: people say that the [[seyatel|Sower’s]] tears fill the Wunde, and when it dries up, the god is happy. On the author’s map, an arrow beside Hofstadt bears the note “And this is where Dietrich returned to his wife.”

## The city’s layout (ch. 10)
The river runs from north to south, forming an elbow within the city. On the western bank, inside the bend, lies the **Schlissel** district—the “key to power over the empire.” Here stand ten ministries, the general staff headquarters, the patriarchal cathedral, the treasury, the treasure house, the Life Guards’ barracks, the cadet school, and the star-shaped imperial palace with its ten towers. Six bridges fan out from Schlissel across the Wunde, connecting the center with the districts on the left bank. The city has docks, a railway bridge, and Widows’ Square; the poor travel in human-drawn carriages. The overcrowded city requires an immense amount of labor, from bringing in water when the river dries up to servicing the palace, so it has countless shops, taverns, bathhouses, and theaters catering to every taste.

## The Witwen district (ch. 10)
Seventy years ago, during the Chinkhar Wars, Empress Augusta decided to care for officers’ widows and ordered a house built for each widow on the southern outskirts, within sight of the palace. As the war dragged on, the houses multiplied, and the district became known as Witwen (“Widows’”). Its houses are narrow and three stories high: two floors for the mistress, her children, and her stones, with the third let to lodgers. Teachers, bookkeepers, and impoverished nobles took a liking to the district. The von Dort family lives in one of these houses on Witwenplatz, its windows facing the blank wall of the Cathedral of the All-Good Sower. The empress had the cathedral built to hide part of the Widows’ district from view, but it did not help.

## Hofstadt in the story (ch. 10–33)
In chapter 10, [[ditrikh|Dietrich]] returns home after the Ollandian campaign to a cold reception from his wife [[mari|Mari]]. In chapters 14, 17, and 25, he visits Baroness [[elza|Walbrecht’s]] house and Count [[lurd|von Lurd]] in the Schlissel district; his batman [[milosh|Milosh]] comes to see him, and Frau [[gizeke|Gieseke]] appears in the apartment. [[joriksen|Joriksen’s]] mansion is also here (ch. 21). In chapter 29, Dietrich says goodbye to his wife, and at the station the magnus [[amir-sedoj|Amir the Grey]] meets him in his compartment. In chapter 33, [[klyajn|Wolf Klein]] is transferred here.
