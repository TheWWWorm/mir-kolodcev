Tres Molinos (“Three Mills”) is a small town in a gorge in [[seleziya|Selezia]], where [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]] and [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]] arrive in chapter 32 to investigate what happened to the philosopher [[liess-van-khalm|van Halm]]. An overnight train from [[laerta|Laerta]] reaches the station, a hundred miles from Seleza; in the morning, after coffee, the passenger gets off.

## The town (ch. 32)
The narrator ironically describes Tres Molinos as “the dullest of backwaters”: three hundred and twenty inhabitants, a town hall with a clock, running water, an inn called Comfortable Rooms, taverns called Breakfasts in Boxes and the Holy Torero, and a shop called Odds and Ends. The three windmills that gave the town its name do not grind wheat—there is none in rice-growing Selezia—but pump water into the water tower’s tank. Locals navigate by three directions: “near the temple,” meaning the temple of the Ancient Lords a mile south, closed shortly after the First Judge; “up above,” meaning the House Above, an old castle on the plateau; and “over there,” meaning everywhere else. After the temple closed, the locals say, the region stopped developing.

The characters get to know the town through two guides: Javier Salam, nicknamed “Need Anything?” ([[khavyer-salam|Javier Salam]]), and the coffeehouse owner Senora [[markes|Marques]], whose “museum of misfortunes” consists of stories of disasters connected with the castle.

## The castle and its curse (ch. 32–34)
The town has long talked about the castle’s curse: its victims include the former owner [[nivendej|Nivendey]] and van Halm himself. The climb to the castle and Alvaro’s investigation are described in chapter 34; there Alvaro also observes that the water tower stands lower than the castle and calls this one of the two facts everyone else overlooked. See [[posledniaya-zima|Last Winter]] and [[posledniaya-zima-shturm|The Night at Last Winter]].
