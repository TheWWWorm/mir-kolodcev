Last Winter is a ruined feudal castle on a plateau above [[tres-molinos|Tres Molinos]], known to the locals as the House Above. [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]] gives it the name “Last Winter” in chapter 34. In chapter 32, it is simply an ominous house: a boarded-up wing, a raven on the chimney, and red ribbons marking the areas visitors may enter.

## The House Above (ch. 32)
The Merganan recluse [[liess-van-khalm|Liess van Halm]] bought the castle two years earlier. He lived in seclusion and sent out manuscripts with his servants, then suddenly dismissed them, ordered mirrors delivered, and sent out letters. A week later, guests arrived at the castle: a factory owner, a noble corsair, a grey-haired gentleman with a military bracelet, and a veiled lady—the heroes of the Solanta expedition. The factory owner later returned with a doctor. Afterward, van Halm was found insane. The townspeople are convinced the castle is cursed: before the philosopher, it belonged to [[nivendej|Nivendey]], who won an enslaved woman—the fifth wife of a Muari sultan—at dice and was arrested. Senora [[markes|Marques]] has collected an entire catalogue of misfortunes connected with the castle.

## The Dangarian servants (ch. 32–34)
As chapter 32 reveals, van Halm hired six new servants before his guests’ visit. They came from the empire and spoke Dangarian among themselves. Their leader, who spoke Catalian well, had letters branded beneath his collarbone. The Dangarians lived in the castle for a month or two, buying up tools, and left the day after the guests. In ch. 34, Alvaro realizes that the group was led by [[red|Rodrigo da Vira]]—in other words, [[red|Red]], branded with “THOU SHALT NOT KILL.” His men were the ones who dug the dark schönbrun beneath the castle.

## The castle in chapter 34 (ch. 34)
Alvaro and Fernan climb up to the castle in the evening. The northern residential wing is inhabited; the southern wing is boarded up. Alvaro solves the mystery of the “cursed” door: on that cold night, the grease in the lock had thickened, preventing the unlocked door from opening; when the weather warmed, Maduro and Juanes opened it with a single turn. In the cellar, beyond a hall of armored knights, a pipe and a small door lead to a staircase. Below lies the dark schönbrun: an underground room with mirrors on the ceiling and a grille made of skulls, from which a wind-powered pump supplies the castle with water. The confrontation with the killers takes place here ([[posledniaya-zima-shturm|The Night at Last Winter]]); [[fatima|Fatima]] explains the dark schönbrun’s three laws: anyone who looks into the water at a particular time, including in a reflection, becomes a victim; misfortune strikes at random; and it attacks what the victim holds most dear.
