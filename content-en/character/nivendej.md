Nivendey is a man about whom the novel reveals very little: his name and the fact that he talked a great deal.

## In the Cell with Red (ch. 12)
Telling Greta his story, Red mentions that he shared a cell with a fellow named Nivendey. The man “talked and talked, always about the same thing.” Red would have killed him if it had not meant smelling a rotting corpse for weeks afterward. Then they were led out to be executed, but instead of facing the noose, Red unexpectedly found himself enslaved. This episode does not reveal what happened to Nivendey.

## Nivendey in Tres Molinos (ch. 32)
In ch. 32, Senora [[markes|Marques]] shows Alvaro and Fernan her “museum of misfortunes” in Tres Molinos and explains the curse of the House Above. Among the exhibits are dice and a medallion bearing the portrait of a dark-skinned girl. She tells them about the house's last owner before Liess van Halm: his name was Nivendey, and he won a slave woman wagered by his opponent by throwing four sixes, then lived with her for a year. Men of a Muari sultan then arrived: the girl proved to be his fifth wife, stolen from his harem. The slave woman was returned to the harem, while Nivendey was arrested and thrown into a dungeon on an island. This happened about fifteen years earlier.

The novel does not explicitly say whether this is the same man as Red's cellmate. The name, the island prison, and Red's description of Nivendey as a chatterbox obsessed with one subject all match. However, in the book's chronology Red's storyline precedes Alvaro's, and the text gives no direct indication of a connection.
