Javier Salam, nicknamed Need Anything, drives visitors to the House Above in a light carriage. His nickname comes from his question to customers: “Do you need anything?” He has given tours since the day two visiting ladies replied by asking him to show them “something a little creepy.”

## The tour (ch. 32)
He is chosen as their guide by a show of hands, for a fee of twenty escudos; the second consultant is Senora [[markes|Marques]]. On the way, he tells them that the Merganan settled there two years ago, sent manuscripts by train, then dismissed his servants, ordered mirrors delivered, and sent out letters. A week later, five guests arrived and left in steam carriages during the night, while Javier came down with influenza. Later, he saw the manufacturer drive up and down, then return the next morning with a doctor. In the castle, Javier shows them the key in a crack in the wall and the red ribbons they must not step beyond.

## About the servants (ch. 32)
The next morning, Alvaro reproaches him for an omission: apparently the servants were dismissed twice. Javier replies that the tour has been paid for, but no one asked about servants, and demands five escudos, citing the audience’s approval. Having taken the money, he describes the six Dangarians whom the philosopher hired to replace his former staff: a tall, strong man, three retired soldiers with flintlock muskets, a young man, and a woman. They lived in the house for a month or two, stole nothing, and left the day after the guests.

## Other customers (ch. 34)
As [[fatima|Fatima]] explains, his first customers were she herself and her chauffeur [[frej|Frey]], who visited Tres Molinos incognito. Deserey’s agents report that, for a couple of coins, Javier told Beatriz where Alvaro and Fernan had gone.
