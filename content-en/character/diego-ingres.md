**Diego Ingres** is the alguacil at the Foucault Street station whom [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] approaches after finding a woman’s severed finger at home.

## Alguacils and guards (ch. 13)
The narrator explains that two bodies maintain order in [[kataliya|Catalia]] ([[algvasily]]): guards from the esfera of Shadow — bearded, without mustaches, and with red scarves on their heads — intervene in obvious crimes, while alguacils from the esfera of Law — with pointed beards and a white feather of the mirna [[iolanta|Iolanta]] in their hats — determine the extent to which the [[svitok|Scroll]] has been violated. Ingres is an alguacil. He loves his work; his habits include saying “hmm-hmm” when interested and tilting his head to the left when he hears nonsense.

On the white land of the Arts district, there are no straightforward crimes: neither murders nor thefts. Instead, creative people devise situations in which it is difficult to tell whether any crime has occurred at all. Ingres relates two anecdotes, one about a composer who burned someone else’s cantata, the other about a singer and her poet neighbor.

## The finger (ch. 13)
Alvaro’s house has no lock: none is needed on white land. Ingres examines the study: in a desk drawer are a notebook containing the message “Stop this!” and a woman’s severed finger. He says there is no law against bringing severed fingers into someone else’s home: freedom of speech is absolute, a verbal threat is not punishable, and any voluntary transaction is legal. Only the finger’s owner can bring a claim. Alvaro himself has suffered no harm. When he cries out that this will make him unable to write, the investigator offers to take the finger away and suggests a cup of coffee. He takes the evidence with him.
