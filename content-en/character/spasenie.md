Salvation is a stone soldier in NCO [[gelb|Gelb's]] platoon. As the NCO explains, he received his nickname for his answer to the question of how the war would end: instead of reciting the regulations, he says salvation will come and God will save everyone. The others see him as a simpleton.

## Faith and Character (ch. 2)

During [[ditrikh|Dietrich von Dort's]] inspection, he smiles joyfully, certain that God will save everyone “to the very last,” even the officers and the field marshal, but not the enemy: good people, yes; enemies, no. Dietrich envies the ease with which he has made peace with his conscience. At the well in the cellar on the hill, Salvation turns the winch, repeating that their salvation lies in the dear water. He condemns his escaped comrades: he says there will be no salvation for them.

## Escape and Killing (ch. 2)

The platoon escapes at night, while Salvation, who refused to go, is left tied up in the hut beside Gelb, who has been nailed to the wall. In the morning, Dietrich wants to carry the wounded man away, but the holy fool stubbornly urges him to fetch water and pulls him toward the well. After the battle, Dietrich finds Salvation beside the well with his hands in the water. He rejoices in the victory and wants to tell everyone about the “dear water,” but at the exit Dietrich wraps his pistol in his cloak and shoots him in the back of the head. The text does not explicitly state his reasons.

## Consequences (ch. 6–21)

In ch. 6, Dietrich invents a story for Major [[klyajn|Klein]] that Salvation disappeared, unable to endure the horror of battle. In ch. 10, he confesses in the Sower's church that he killed a stone who was a believer, and in ch. 14 and 21 he remembers him: as Dietrich tells [[joriksen|Joriksen]], the only man who guessed about the schönbrun was the one who fetched water most often, and he is already dead. The body, shot in the back of the head, lies in the well—Klein points this out in ch. 31, without naming him.

## Killnot and Salvation (ch. 33)

In ch. 33, it becomes clear that Killnot, the rifleman who carried water with Salvation, is Red; Salvation himself does not appear in that chapter.
