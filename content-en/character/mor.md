**Mor** leads the reformist movement whose members wear yellow ribbons in [[laerta|Laerta]]. The name is first heard in ch. 11, when Alvaro attaches it to the wrong person: the crowd shouts “Mor is coming!” before a giant with an iron arm, but he is actually [[malysh|Little One]], an associate of Mor herself. The protagonist meets her in person only in ch. 20.

## Appearance and Position (ch. 20)
Mor is a woman of about fifty, stocky and resembling a butcher's wife, with tight lips and attentive eyes surrounded by wrinkles. Her bracelet is not simply black like an obrero's, but black with three gold gears—the mark of an engineer of the highest rank in the esfera of Production; its standing is equivalent to Alvaro's title of maestro. She also owns two percent of the factory, making her, as she jokes, one-fiftieth of a donna. She speaks roughly, enjoys a joke, and does not mince words in her letters.

## The Story of Esperanza (ch. 20)
According to Mor herself, ten years earlier, during an unemployment crisis in [[brega|Brega]], she—a talented engineer and inventor of the pressure condenser—organized her dismissed comrades. They challenged the dons to a debate but lost: a factory owner decides who works there (“whose machines, whose rules”). Mor and her companions then decided to pool their resources and build their own factory for two thousand people. To obtain a loan, they pledged their homes; Mor promised the bankers a share of future profits. Three years later, the factory began operating, and a year after that it turned a profit. Its [[esperantsa|Esperanza]] vehicles—compact, fast steam carriages—are sold even in the Muari Emirates.

Now the dismissed obreros of Laerta are asking her advice, but the familiar route is closed: with help from lawyers of the esfera of Law, the manufacturers won a debate that barred banks from offering workers loans on favorable terms. Mor wants to overturn this ruling in a new dueling debate, but only if [[fatima|Fatima's]] lawyers do not side with the manufacturers.

## Conversation with Alvaro (ch. 20)
Mor first outlines the “rebels'” program to Alvaro: destroy the steam-powered machines, seize the esfera of Production, and confiscate the manufacturers' property. When he objects in horror, she smiles: he has believed the newspapers. The dons paid the press to portray the reformists as malicious idiots, whereas their true aims are to reverse the credit ruling and restore workers' access to favorable loans. She then asks Alvaro to influence Fatima-allen. The writer decides they intend to “put him in bed” with the allen, accuses everyone of indifference to his book, and leaves.

## Letters and the Esfera of Law (ch. 24–30)
In ch. 24, Mor tries to deliver a letter to Alvaro through [[osa|Wasp]]. In ch. 26, the woman explaining the supremo election to Alvaro mentions that Mor and Esperanza have created competition for the manufacturers, but the esfera of Law has unexpectedly sided with them.

In ch. 30, Mor sends an angry letter: she writes that Alvaro is a fool, that his student Beatriz is Fatima Ir-Reya's daughter, which is why she said Ir-Reya would receive him, and that she had no intention of manipulating either him or Fatima, but was seeking an honest deal. Mor reproaches him for barging in on Don [[kristobal-maduro|Maduro]] and saying too much on the reformists' behalf: Maduro has decided they are intent on gaining seats at the High Table and is recruiting hundreds of shadows. Having come to Laerta to negotiate with three people—Ir-Reya, Maduro, and Alvaro—she will now take another path. In a postscript, she asks whether his hand hurts badly and invites him to Three Bulls if he feels worse. [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]], working as a scribe, composed the letter in her presence.
