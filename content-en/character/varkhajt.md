Friedrich von Wahrheit is a Dangarian count and newspaper magnate. He owns four newspapers, including Glory, also called Glory of the Empire in the text. Baroness Elsa introduces him to her guests as “His Majesty's companion” and “the voice of the imperial court.”

## The Baroness's Reception (ch. 14)
At the [[rybalka-valbrekht|fishing party at Baroness Walbrecht's]], the count sits at the festive table and, unlike Joriksen, enjoys flattery: when Elsa praises Glory, he nods as though urging her to continue. The baroness recounts how she once cited his newspaper in a conversation with the Kaiser, and the sovereign immediately agreed. When a dragoon cornet refuses to drink with an Ollandian prince, the count endorses their hostess's statement that there are no enemies at her table. He then approves Dietrich's toast, and at these moments the narrator calls him “the Kaiser's voice,” the man with the best knowledge of the sovereign's thoughts.

That evening, the count holds the bank in a thousand-mark game of Stoss. When the players ask him about [[dunkvald|Dunkwald]], he answers with a question: “Did you read about it in my Glory?”—then adds that in that case it is true: the newspaper does not print nonsense.

## Glory of the Empire and Dunkwald (ch. 17–29)
In Chapter 17, Olkha names the count among the nobles Prince [[lents|Lenz]] has become acquainted with in the capital. In Chapter 29, Magnus [[amir-sedoj|Amir the Grey]] says that Glory printed the plain truth about the [[gibel-dunkvalda|destruction of Dunkwald]]. Joriksen speaks indignantly of Wahrheit: the moment he caught a “whiff of carrion,” he blew it up into a sensation. The magnus replies that one cannot blame a faithful dog for knowing its master's tastes, and adds that while dining with the patriarch, the Kaiser listens to news from that newspaper.
