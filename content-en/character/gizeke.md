Frau Gieseke owns the house in [[khofshtadt|Hofstadt’s]] Widows’ District where [[ditrikh|Dietrich von Dort’s]] family rents an apartment. She is the third generation of widows in her family: her mother and grandmother were also widows, and she traces her ancestry to the first widows for whom Empress Augusta ordered narrow three-story houses built within sight of the palace instead of granting allowances.

## Character

The narrator calls her an exception among landladies: family honor prevents her from descending to landladies’ favorite occupation, quarreling with tenants. She does not spy on them or find fault with trifles such as an unlocked door. At the same time, she is too fond of mentioning death and seems to flaunt her close acquaintance with it, which unsettles Dietrich. She is convinced that [[mari|Mari]] will soon be widowed, so she calls little [[khajnts|Heinz]] “the little orphan” and readily helps Mari with water or cloth for diapers. She loves Dietrich like a son and is old enough to be his mother.

## Role in the story (ch. 10–25)

In ch. 10, she meets Dietrich on the stairs with tears of joy, serves him and his orderly [[milosh|Milosh]] tea and schnapps, and gives Milosh a bench in the servants’ room. When Dietrich complains of his wife’s cold welcome, she advises him to distinguish hurt feelings from a heart that has ceased to love, and shows him a newspaper notice: Baroness [[elza|Walbrecht]] is holding a fishing party at the Beautiful Well, with tickets selling for two hundred marks despite the “select society.” Thus Frau Gieseke unwittingly directs Dietrich toward the first schönbrun of his life. Later that night, she accepts twenty ten-mark banknotes from him and undertakes to buy a fishing ticket (see [[rybalka-valbrekht|Fishing at Baroness Walbrecht’s]]).

After the fishing (ch. 14), it is she who, at Mari’s request, arranges a search of the stone servants after they discover that money is missing. In ch. 17, Milosh invents a story for her that the lieutenant stayed late at the minister’s, and she agrees to let Dietrich sleep in the entrance hall. In ch. 21, that is where Dietrich wakes. In ch. 25, comforting his wife, Dietrich says that Frau Gieseke has “worked her up” with talk about how someone or other was killed.

## Fate

Dietrich leaves for the front in ch. 29, and Frau Gieseke does not appear in subsequent chapters. The novel does not say what becomes of her or her house.
