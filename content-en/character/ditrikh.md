**Dietrich von Dort** is an artillery lieutenant in the Dangarian army, an “eagle” nobleman without land or slaves. He is one of the three main characters of Traces on the Water; his storyline concerns a man who once saw the future in a well, used it in war, and has been paying for that step ever since. His story begins in chapter 2 and passes through occupied Ollandia, [[dangar|Dangar’s]] capital [[khofshtadt|Hofstadt]], and back to Ollandia. According to the author, the characters’ storylines are offset in time and will “come together in a particular way”; Dietrich’s story takes place later than [[red|Red’s]] (see the interview section for more).

## Character and appearance
Dietrich’s middle name is Wolfgang, also the first name of Major [[klyajn|von Klein]], but the novel does not indicate any connection behind the coincidence.

The novel’s first description of Dietrich takes the form of a letter: Staff Captain [[zejdvist|Claus von Zeidvist]] describes his twenty-three-year-old subordinate to the colonel. A graduate of the Kaiser Ferdinand II Artillery Academy, he is strong in mathematics and ballistics, an unremarkable-looking, gentle man: short, with a bulbous nose, a small chin, and a slight paunch. He dislikes duels, speaks with pauses (“umm…,” a questioning “yes?..”), is too lenient with his orderly, and can make jokes that set people thinking. His commander advises against advancing him.

Dietrich sincerely believes in the [[seyatel|Sower]], has a conscience, fears the word “Fire!” and is awkward with women. At the same time, he is ambitious and bears grudges over humiliations: speaking to Joriksen, he admits that he wants to “win.” His self-esteem rests on the honor of his estate: he says he differs from the enslaved [[kamni|stones]] because an eagle is guided by honor and a stone by his master, and because he knows how to doubt. This also makes him refuse employment under hares, even though his family needs money. Faced with difficulty, Dietrich initially tells the truth, then starts wriggling out of trouble: he lies to his wife, impersonates someone else’s relative, and gives his officer’s word about land that does not belong to him.

## Three volleys of canister (ch. 2)
In autumn, a month after the capture of [[plyutva|Plyutva]], Dietrich’s battery of four guns is stationed behind the lines in [[ollandiya|Ollandia]], on a bare hill beside a railway halt. Dietrich confesses to his orderly [[milosh|Milosh]] that he is glad of the lull: his conscience has tormented him since the campaign’s first day. The staff captain sends him to inspect the stone fusiliers of NCO [[gelb|Gelb’s]] platoon. There he meets the holy fool [[spasenie|Salvation]] and a strong foreigner nicknamed Killnot because of the words “THOU SHALT NOT KILL” branded on his chest. Dietrich envies his inability to kill.

During the night, the stones escape after maiming the NCO. At dawn, Salvation leads the lieutenant to a well in the cellar of a ruined farmstead. The chapter does not directly reveal what Dietrich sees, but afterward he acts like a man who knows the future: he whispers to Zeidvist that he will die in seven seconds and counts aloud — a stray bullet strikes the staff captain’s face on “one.” Dietrich then assumes command and defeats a squadron of Ollandian hussars with three volleys of canister (for details, see [[tri-zalpa|The Battle at the Well on the Hill]]). He conducts the battle as though he knows its course in advance. After the victory, he removes the only witness to the well: wrapping a pistol in his cloak, he shoots Salvation in the back of the head.

## Interrogation in Latsen (ch. 6)
Three months later in [[latsen|Latsen]], Dietrich, nominated for promotion, waits in a summer restaurant for a summons from the Kaiser’s Claws. Three actions committed “against God’s will” torment him: he did not save Zeidvist, although he had time; he assumed command instead of Lieutenant [[shpeer|Speer]]; and he did something after the battle. Here he meets [[olkha|Olkha]], who jokingly calls him “Mr. Occupier,” and tells her the Dangarian parable of the Sower and the well.

Major [[klyajn|Wolfgang von Klein]] of the Claws conducts the conversation politely, then lays five bullets on the table — five peculiarities of the battle — and presents a theory in which Dietrich himself arranged the stones’ escape and his commander’s murder, making him a traitor. Dietrich cannot bring himself to reveal his principal defense: the well, which no one but he has seen. He is removed from service without an allowance and sent to Hofstadt to await a decision: “consider discharge your reward for the heroic deed.”

## Home, sweet home (ch. 10)
In Hofstadt’s Widows’ District, Dietrich receives a cold welcome: his wife [[mari|Mari]] has been waiting for his letter about his discharge, which never arrived, and discharge without an allowance means poverty. He conceals the fact that he is under investigation and that he shot a stone. Their landlady, [[gizeke|Frau Gieseke]], takes pity on him and shows him a newspaper notice about fishing at Baroness Walbrecht’s. His search for employment founders on the pride of his estate: the fair-haired [[frafra|Franz Freiber]] offers temporary “eagle’s” work, and Dietrich refuses. In church he confesses to the Sower, admitting that he ordered the deaths of at least fifty hussars and shot a devout stone; at home, he quarrels with Mari. At night he wanders through the city with Milosh: vagrants sleep in the dry bed of the Wunde, stones collect manure, and a military airship of the Life Guards glides silently overhead. Dietrich wonders how he differs from the stones. Later, he takes almost all the family’s hidden savings and asks Frau Gieseke to buy a ticket to the fishing party.

## A miracle for sale (ch. 14)
At the fishing party at [[elza|Baroness Elsa von Walbrecht’s]] house, Dietrich first sees the master fisherman [[joriksen|Fritjof Joriksen]] at work. The baroness charges him two hundred marks; Joriksen declares him the “extra,” thirty-sixth guest, but Elsa insists that he stay. Dietrich wishes that Mari would love him as much as he loves her. His toast “to worthy former adversaries who will become allies” saves the evening from a duel with a dragoon cornet and wins Count [[varkhajt|von Wahrheit’s]] approval, but the cornet is hostile to him from then on.

Later, Dietrich finds the courage to speak to Joriksen about “his” schönbrun, a well in Ollandia absent from the guild’s maps. After an initial refusal (“paupers do not own schönbruns”), he secures an invitation to a meeting. At home, his spending of the hidden savings is discovered. Mari is initially pleased that his wish concerned her, then cries: the love, she says, was already there.

## Eyring (ch. 17)
Princess Olkha, whom Dietrich has already met in Latsen, asks him to accompany her to her friend [[katrina|Katrina]], wife of Deputy Minister [[lurd|Ludwig von Lurd]]. To explain to her uncle, Prince [[lents|Wladyslaw Lenz]], why an unfamiliar officer defended them at the reception, Olkha calls Dietrich “Dietrich von Eyring,” Katrina’s cousin. Katrina plays along, calling him her “dear little brother,” and together they invent a history of their shared childhood. This fabrication later shapes the entire plot: Dietrich uses it to conceal his surname and enjoy the protection of the powerful [[ejring|Eyring]] family.

Over valisyan, Count Lurd explains his views on world politics to Dietrich: Catalia, [[solanta|Solanta]], [[muaris|Muaris]], and the three centers of power in Dangar — the emperor, the patriarch, and the magnus of the fishermen. On the way back that night, Dietrich and Olkha kiss, and “the evening had been very much like a date.” At the door, Milosh reports that Mari has locked herself in and refuses to let her husband enter.

## The terms of the contract (ch. 21)
In the morning, Dietrich assesses his situation soberly: he has one mark, no post, and no rights to the land. In Joriksen’s mansion, arranged like a museum of curiosities, the master speaks to him like a merchant. He sets his terms: silence, Dietrich’s officer’s word that he owns the land containing the well, the right to fish in it for a year, and reports of its “successes.” Studying a new schönbrun costs ten thousand marks, which Joriksen is willing to “forgive,” but the deed names Dietrich’s son [[khajnts|Heinz]] as collateral: in the event of nonpayment, the boy can be made a stone. Dietrich leaves in fury, but learns from the guild’s atlas that the plot by the station belongs to Prince Lenz.

Dietrich returns with conditions of his own: three hundred marks a month as a “salary,” a promise that the fisherman will not buy up the land before he can, and that he will tell Mari he helped Dietrich return to service. Joriksen agrees, and Dietrich signs the paper, although he is ashamed to think of the pledge. He takes a hotel room and conceals the source of the money from Mari.

## Allow me to suggest a solution (ch. 25)
Mari visits her husband at the hotel, treating it as a romantic “secret affair.” Dietrich thinks increasingly often of Olkha. On the way to the station, [[otto|Otto]], Joriksen’s pupil, tells him about dark schönbruns: they do not kill directly, but harm the body, mind, emotions, and prosperity (for more, see [[temnyj-shyonbrun|Dark Schönbrun]]). Dietrich decides to see Olkha once more and goes to Walbrecht’s mansion, where he meets Katrina and Count Lurd. In the guest wing, he knocks at Olkha’s rooms and finds Olkha in a dressing gown and Katrina in her underwear amid scattered clothing. Katrina tears his shirt, scratches him, throws her belongings out of the window, and jumps after them. The prince and the baroness enter the rooms. The prince demands pistols; Count Lurd meanwhile offers “a solution to two problems at once.”

## Catastrophe (ch. 29)
Instead of a duel, Lurd proposes that the prince marry Olkha to the supposed Eyring: the prince would thereby become related to an ancient Dangarian family, and the Kaiser, according to the count, likes modest artillerymen. As his final argument, the count cites the recent hussar attack on the station on Lenz’s land: combined with the duel and the “fishing,” it would allow the prince to be declared “unsicher,” unreliable, whereupon his lands would be confiscated and part of the population branded. The prince yields and allows a written request for Olkha’s hand; in return, Dietrich asks for a plot of one hundred desyatinas. At that moment Milosh and Otto arrive: Joriksen demands immediate departure. Dietrich tells Mari that he is leaving to rejoin his unit and asks whether she would agree to move to Muaris if necessary; Mari agrees.

In Joriksen’s compartment, Dietrich is tested by the guild’s magnus, [[amir-sedoj|Amir the Grey]]. He says that a postman found everyone in the settlement of [[dunkvald|Dunkwald]] dead: four hundred people, including three platoons of soldiers. As an artilleryman, Dietrich explains the marks as the work of canister and bayonets and suggests looking for a cannon on the forest road. The magnus orders the train to remain still while water runs in his clepsydra and finally says that there are no schönbruns in Dunkwald. After the conversation, Joriksen says he has never seen the Grey so furious.

## Arrest and release (ch. 31)
At Latsen station, gendarmes arrest Dietrich on Major Klein’s orders for “violating house arrest” in Hofstadt. Klein has found the well near the railway halt himself: it contains the decomposing body of a man whom, according to the major, Dietrich shot in the back of the head. In Klein’s eyes, Dietrich is a dangerous, unpredictable man with whom one can cooperate only cautiously. The major decides to send him as a “volunteer” near Mershants to face Catalian rifled howitzers, but a document concerning his engagement to Olkha is found on the lieutenant. Olkha and Katrina, Countess von Lurd, burst into the gendarmerie, invoke Lurd’s name and that of Margrave von Eyring, and Klein backs down and apologizes. Dietrich pretends not to understand the hint about the “principal place,” thereby concealing the well even from his fiancée and Katrina. Klein lets everyone go.

## The secret of the well (ch. 2–31)
One secret runs through Dietrich’s entire story: the well in the cellar of a burned village near the railway halt at the hundredth verst of the Plyutva–Latsen line. Only Dietrich and Salvation, whom he killed, knew about it. Dietrich conceals it from the Kaiser’s Claws (ch. 6), uses it as a bargaining chip with the fisherman (ch. 14, 21), and tries to acquire the rights to the land through Olkha and Prince Lenz (ch. 21–29). Dietrich himself does not know whether the schönbrun is light or dark and fears that the victory was a coincidence; Joriksen explains that “cursed” wells do not exist, but fishing in dark ones is dangerous. Having found the well earlier, Klein wants to use it for General [[khofmajer|Hofmeyer’s]] army. By chapter 31, Dietrich remains the only person who understands exactly what happened on the hill, making him both valuable and dangerous to every side.

## Killnot’s past (ch. 33–34)
The “stone” rifleman Killnot from the platoon guarding the battery in ch. 2 is [[red|Red]]. At the end of ch. 33, he is taken from Dunkwald into the army, and in ch. 34 he is revealed as [[red|Rodrigo da Vira]]. This is how Dietrich’s story intersects with Red’s and Alvaro’s: the stones’ escape on the night of the battle at the well was apparently the route by which da Vira escaped slavery and returned to Catalia “in the company of Dangarian deserters.”

## Relationships
- [[mari|Mari]] — his wife, whom he does not deny loving; she is jealous and concerned about money, but has a stronger character than his. Their son is [[khajnts|Heinz]].
- [[milosh|Milosh]] — his orderly and stone slave, purchased by Dietrich’s father; perceptive, he invents plausible explanations for his mistress.
- [[olkha|Olkha Lenz]] — a young woman he knows from Latsen, a “kindred spirit,” as shy and awkward as he is. His engagement to her results from deception rather than choice.
- [[joriksen|Fritjof Joriksen]] — a fisherman who is both patron and creditor; he keeps Dietrich on the hook through the pledge of Heinz.
- [[klyajn|Wolfgang von Klein]] — a Kaiser’s Claw who interrogated Dietrich in Latsen and arrested him at the station.
- [[katrina|Katrina von Lurd]] and [[lurd|Ludwig von Lurd]] — patrons who support his invented relationship to the Eyrings.

## Conception and interviews
The author names Alvaro, Red, and Dietrich as the three main characters and regrets that none is a woman. He says that he likes Dietrich “to a lesser extent, but still”; among the Dangarians, he also likes Joriksen and Katrina. In the discussion, the host suggested that Dietrich’s story was contemporary with [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro’s]], while Red’s took place earlier. The author confirmed that the stories are offset in time and would later “come together,” without revealing details. According to the author, the novel is structured so that each protagonist is introduced in a different form: for Dietrich, this is the denunciatory letter assessing his character at the beginning of chapter 2.

## At the end of the novel (ch. 31)
By the end of chapter 31, Dietrich is free, engaged to Olkha, married to Mari, indebted to Joriksen under the pledge, and concealing the well near the railway halt from everyone. The novel does not show the consequences of his double deception or whether he will be able to fulfill his promise to Joriksen.
