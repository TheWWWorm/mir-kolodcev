**Don Enhal** is a first-level judge and [[beatris|Beatriz’s]] father. He never appears in person in the novel: his daughter and the letters he exchanges with [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] provide the account of him.

## Beatriz’s father (ch. 9–11)
In ch. 9, it emerges that Beatriz lives with her father, a first-level judge. In ch. 11, to rid himself of his persistent pupil, Alvaro writes Don Enhal a letter praising his daughter as a capable writer but asking him to prevent her from associating with him because he is being followed. Beatriz reads the letter over his shoulder.

## The judge’s letter (ch. 20)
In ch. 20, Beatriz gives Alvaro her father’s letter. Enhal apologizes for his daughter: her stubbornness and boldness, he writes, sometimes exceed the limits of prudence. He explains that Alvaro’s work has given meaning to her life and led her into the esfera of Art, and asks him to allow Beatriz to remain his pupil. He promises to remove the surveillance and asks Alvaro not to enter black lands, where his authority does not apply. If his daughter becomes unbearable, a note will suffice, and she will not be sent again. At his daughter’s request, he has also made inquiries about the green Cherrato and discovered that it belongs to a hired driver, but has pretended not to notice Alvaro’s lie.

## Daughter and assistant (ch. 24)
In ch. 24, Alvaro presents Beatriz to the butler [[emil|Emil]] as Judge Enhal’s daughter and assistant, opening the doors of the late Doctor [[khuanes|Juanes’s]] house to them.

## Fatima’s former husband (ch. 28)
Only in ch. 28 does Alvaro realize that Beatriz is the daughter of [[fatima|Fatima Ir-Reya]] and Judge Enhal. According to Beatriz, her mother divorced her father after [[solanta|Solanta]]. Their daughter bears her father’s surname, left her mother to live with him and join the esfera of Art, loves her father, and hates being compared with her mother.
