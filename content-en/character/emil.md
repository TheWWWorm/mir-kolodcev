Emil Diale is Doctor [[khuanes|Juanes’s]] butler: an imperturbable man who hides his bracelets beneath his sleeve and drags one leg. He appears twice in the novel, and on both occasions it is his account that advances the investigation.

## The first visit (ch. 5)
He meets [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] and [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]] at the house on the edge of the Valentian Gardens and shows them into the study. Emil says that the doctor was a very good man and describes his last day: visitors, tea, nausea toward evening, and a climb onto the roof. He argues with the widow over who should tell the story and shows the cabinet containing patients’ gifts, including a bottle of ten-year-old Almagarian bonk from the last visitor. To reassure Alvaro, he says that he tried the bonk himself on the day of the funeral and found no poison. During the visit, in his own words, he “could not fail to notice the white land.”

## Exposure (ch. 24)
When Alvaro returns with [[beatris|Beatriz]], Emil tries to keep them out. The writer reconstructs the day of the death. Left alone in the study, Emil poured some bonk into an empty vial to take away: a glass would have retained the smell. The widow entered and told him to buy a newspaper; the vial remained on the shelf. When her husband mistakenly drank the general’s drops and asked his wife to bring the blue balsam, she grabbed that very vial. The doctor drank the strong bonk without tasting it because of the burn. Thus Emil killed Juanes without knowing it and did not violate the taboo of white land. He confesses, calling it a monstrous accident and [[eliga|Eliga’s]] “serpent face.” He was also the one who removed the black ribbons from the [[sinkho|sinkho]] tree to conceal the death.

Alvaro places the butler’s fate in Beatriz’s hands. She releases him on condition that he help with another investigation, pointing to Liess van Halm’s name in the patient register. Emil recalls the philosopher’s invitation, Juanes’s journey, and the nighttime arrival of a chauffeur with uneven shoulders sent by Don [[kristobal-maduro|Maduro]]. He also supplies the information that the entry “Liess van Halm, 49, schizophrenia, transferred to the [[klinika-miloserdiya|Mercy Clinic]]” was made personally by the doctor. In this way, Emil points the way to [[liess-van-khalm|van Halm]].

## At the end of the novel (ch. 24)
After his confession, no punishment is announced for Emil: Alvaro concludes that he meant no harm, while Beatriz is satisfied with his information. The novel does not show the butler’s later fate.
