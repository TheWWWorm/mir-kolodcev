Count Ludwig von Lurd is one of Dangar's most influential officials: officially deputy minister of foreign affairs, but in practice the head of the ministry, since the minister himself is often away. He lives in Schlissel, likes valisyan, and considers everyone a fool except His Majesty. He is the only character who sees politics “as a whole,” enabling him to decide Dietrich's and Olkha's fate in Chapter 29.

## Character and Appearance
Lurd is no longer young: Olkha puts his age at fifty “at the most conservative estimate.” At home, he sits in a dressing gown with a newspaper and a glass. The bar in his study is shaped like the continent; by habit, Lurd offers his visitors twelve-year-old valisyan from the [[mergana|Mergana]] shelf. He speaks enthusiastically, does not listen to others' answers, and draws conclusions on his interlocutor's behalf. His wife warns Dietrich that the count loves talking with other men and advises him to spout any nonsense he likes, so long as he sounds confident.

As Katrina observes, “Ludwig considers absolutely everyone a fool except His Majesty.”

## The House in Schlissel (ch. 17)
Lurd receives Dietrich as his “wife's cousin.” In the study, he questions him about his victory and trade with [[kataliya|Catalia]], then corrects him: in politics, one must think on a global scale. His views, as recounted in the chapter:

- To him, war and peace are “merely instruments of politics.”
- Trade with Catalia is more likely to bring losses to Dangarian manufacturers: Catalian goods will displace Dangarian ones, and Laerta does not recognize tariffs.
- Solanta is surrounded by reefs and served as a base for the [[muaris|Muari]] against Catalian pirates. Thirteen years ago, a pirate schooner passed through the reefs at night, captured the coastal fort, and trained its guns on the Doge's palace. The alliance treaty with [[kataliya|Catalia]] was lenient, but the Muari lost their mastery of the Miriam Sea. Since then, according to the count, Muaris's trade with Dangar has withered while its trade with Laerta has flourished: silk, steam locomotives, saltpeter.
- Dangar has three centers of influence: the emperor, the patriarch—who influences the stones through the church and the [[seyatel|Sower]]—and the magnus, whose guild of [[fishmajster|fischmeisters]] sells miracles to the new nobility. He asks Dietrich to assess who benefits from war.

The conversation is interrupted by the butler's summons to dinner. At the table, Dietrich tells army stories and invented details of his childhood with Katrina; the count laughs, but quickly checks himself and leaves to deal with correspondence.

## A Politician at the Baroness's Reception (ch. 25)
In autumn, Lurd arrives at [[elza|Elsa's]] mansion with his wife to speak with Prince [[lents|Lenz]]. “We are engaged in politics, not espionage,” he says. “There are no great secrets in politics.” The presence of his “kinsman” Dietrich benefits him: it forces Lenz to speak briefly while allowing Lurd to display his eloquence.

The count explains to the prince that Ollandia's governor will be an Ollandian noble who has formed family ties with the clergy or the military aristocracy. The baroness offers “society lions” close to the Fishermen’s Guild, but in the count's view the Guild does not serve the empire zealously enough. When the prince discovers Olkha and Dietrich and demands pistols, Lurd refuses to act as a second or keep silent about the duel. He warns that killing an “Eyring” would cost the prince his chance of becoming governor.

## The Wedding Plan (ch. 29)
The next morning, at the baroness's house, Lurd proposes “compensation”: marriage. His argument is that this alliance with an ancient Dangarian family is needed by the Kaiser himself, who values artillerymen and modesty. The count points out that the Kaiser usually trusts his analysis in matters of dynastic marriage and promises to introduce Dietrich to His Majesty. When the prince resists, Lurd plays his last card: the attack on the station on Lenz's lands three months earlier ([[tri-zalpa|The Battle at the Well on the Hill]]), the duel, and a fishing party involving a fischmeister could make the prince “unsicher.” The prince yields.

Lurd summons his wife and asks her opinion of the groom. The novel does not show whether the count suspects that the “Eyring” is an impostor; he describes himself merely as “one of the advisers.”

## The Card in Latsen (ch. 31)
Katrina throws the count's and Margrave von Eyring's visiting cards down before Major [[klyajn|Klein]]. The major realizes that the threads of his connections in the capital are weaker than the roots “the von Eyrings and von Lurds have put down at court,” and backs down.

## Relationships
- [[katrina|Katrina]]—his wife; the count calls her “my treasure.”
- [[lents|Wladyslaw Lenz]]—the prince with whom Lurd plays a game over the governorship.
- [[ditrikh|Dietrich]]—the “Eyring” whom the count takes under his wing.
- The Kaiser—according to Lurd, trusts his analysis on dynastic marriages; as the count says, he needs the loyalty of the entire Lenz family.

## Conception and Interviews
According to the author in the first stream, Count Lurd and his wife Katrina are among the Dangarians he finds likable. The transcript is inaccurate in its rendering of names.
