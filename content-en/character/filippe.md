Filippe di Berro is an opera singer and prim-allen of the esfera of Art, one of the rulers of the [[vysokij-stol|High Table]]. He commissions [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]] to write a book about the heroes of the [[solantijskaya-ekspeditsiya|Solantan Expedition]], setting the novel’s main action in motion. He himself is not on the list of heroes: he calls the expedition’s participants his friends, but the novel says nothing about any role of his own in the voyage.

## Character and appearance
According to the narrator, Filippe is known for his vindictiveness and his tendency to cause scenes. He has kept his seat at the High Table for six consecutive years, although the Table expels the worst of its allens every year, by using theater, books, epigrams, and caricatures against his political enemies. Offstage he looks like an aging, puffy-faced gentleman with yellow patches on his face; onstage he wears a corset. A Muari servant in a white tailcoat with scarlet buttons accompanies him.

Filippe is polite and ironic, but sharp when necessary: he can lower his voice an octave and speak harshly. The fate of newspaper pieces about creative people depends on him: the esfera of Knowledge sends articles concerning artists to him for approval, and he can retain or withdraw them.

## The opera evening and the commission (ch. 0–3)
The Preface mentions that the famous singer has appeared onstage for the first time in a year, at the premiere of Señorita Violet. In ch. 3, Alvaro watches the performance from a box at the [[grand-opera|Grand Opera]]. Filippe sings Don Laurent and has himself demanded a changed ending: in the classic version, Violet stabs herself; in the new one, she takes a double-barreled pistol, both shots misfire, and she falls into Laurent’s arms as he swears to die if he is unfaithful. On the whole, the audience approves of the moral that killing oneself for love is the worst course of action.

Backstage, having removed his makeup, Filippe asks Alvaro to write a book about four heroic friends: ballads have already been composed about them, and their names are inscribed on the [[svitok|Great Scroll]]. He wants the writer to meet them and describe their lives now, sixteen years after the “great event.” Alvaro suspects a catch, but Filippe says he wants to glorify his friends and clear their reputations. His terms are to cover Alvaro’s entire debt — he lost seven thousand escudos in his debate with [[vinsente-ramires|Ramires]] — pay three thousand for the book’s outline, and another three thousand for the completed manuscript. The list contains a buccaneer and a general whom Alvaro knows personally, an arms manufacturer, and a woman with an exalted title. Alvaro suggests crossing one out to avoid an even number; Filippe does the opposite, adding a fifth hero, Doctor [[khuanes|Juanes]], who died the day before yesterday, and writing his name in black eyebrow pencil.

By ch. 5, the book is treated as a settled matter, but in ch. 13 Alvaro sends him a letter withdrawing from the work: his second rejection of seven thousand escudos in one month, as [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]] furiously observes.

## The Philosophy of Horror (ch. 16)
After receiving the rejection, Filippe invites Alvaro to the [[galereya-eliste|Eliste Gallery]] for the exhibition [[filosofiya-uzhasa|The Philosophy of Horror]]. Moving from painting to painting, he repeatedly sidesteps discussion of Alvaro’s withdrawal and returns to his own point: to write, one must first “bring oneself to life.” Before a painting of the mirna [[eliga|Eliga]], he notices only the bullet hole in her temple, ignoring Alvaro’s interpretation of the inseparability of success and failure. Alvaro tells him about Juanes’s strange death and the threat he has received, a severed finger; Filippe admits that he too received a certain message, without giving details.

Then he shows him an article from the Laerta Sunday about the fight at the station, in which Alvaro struck guards with the butt of his revolver. Only Filippe can decide the article’s fate. Dropping his voice, he then lists what Alvaro is: a coward, a mediocrity, a brawler, a rebel, a whimpering woman, a genius — adding up to “barren ugliness.” He has only one condition: has the book been written or not? If it has, there will be no threats, poverty, or hostile articles. Alvaro promises to write it, and Filippe adds that the exhibition was inspired by the philosopher [[liess-van-khalm|Liess van Halm’s]] unfinished book.

## Relationships
- [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]] — the commissioned writer, dependent on Filippe for money and reputation. In ch. 20, the wounded Alvaro observes that Don Filippe would appreciate his talent.
- [[fatima|Fatima Ir-Reya]] — his colleague at the High Table; in ch. 28 she admits that she was checking whether Alvaro was a spy sent by Filippe the allen. When Alvaro wonders in ch. 28 which allen might be her second lover, he silently thanks the Ancients that it is “at least not Don Filippe.”
- The heroes of Solanta — Filippe’s “friends”; the novel does not explain exactly what connects him to them.

## What remains unclear (ch. 34)
The novel does not show whether Filippe receives what was promised: the book is unfinished at the end, and its patron never explains what message he himself received. The threats and pressure he employs remain part of his character rather than a separate intrigue.
