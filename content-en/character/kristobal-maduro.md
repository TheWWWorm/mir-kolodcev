Don Cristobal Maduro is an arms manufacturer, one of Catalia's seven major industrialists, and one of the heroes of the [[solantijskaya-ekspeditsiya|Solantan Expedition]] on Prim-Allen [[filippe|Filippe di Berro's]] list. [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]] needs him as a subject for his book, but Maduro refuses to speak to him, and his guard receives the writer instead.

## The Manufacturer and His Position
Maduro makes revolvers, and Alvaro himself carries “a product of Don Maduro's factory.” Together with six other manufacturers, according to the mysterious lady in ch. 26, he controls all production of weapons and steam engines: they have colluded to set prices. Their competitor is the [[esperantsa|Esperanza]] factory, run by [[mor|Mor]]. Maduro is allied with Don [[balboa|Balboa]] against the reformists and the dismissed obreros.

## Invitation and Guards (ch. 13)
Alvaro receives a letter summoning him to a conversation. Fernan persuades him to go. Maduro's mansion is protected by a wall topped with iron spikes, along with sentries, dogs, and barred windows—strange in a white district, where locks are unnecessary. Instead of the owner, [[salvador|Salvador]] receives Alvaro in the guardroom, and it is to him that Alvaro explains his views on reforming the esfera of Craft. Salvador interrogates the writer, accusing the reformists of sabotage—pouring water over coal dust and jamming shoes into gears—and demands to know what they want.

## The Man Behind the Chauffeur and the Threats (ch. 24–30)
The butler [[emil|Emil]] recalls that Maduro was not [[khuanes|Juanes's]] patient, but sent a chauffeur with uneven shoulders to fetch him one night when the philosopher [[liess-van-khalm|van Halm]] needed help. Alvaro concludes that Maduro was the first to learn of the philosopher's madness. [[mor|Mor's]] letter in ch. 30 says that after Alvaro's visit to Maduro, the manufacturer decided the reformists were intent on gaining seats at the [[vysokij-stol|High Table]] and is recruiting hundreds of shadows.

## A Guest at the House Above (ch. 32–34)
In Tres Molinos, people say that the guests who visited van Halm—a manufacturer, a noble corsair, a gray-haired military man, and a veiled lady—left at night; the manufacturer returned later, peered through the small window in the door, and brought a doctor and a local carter. Alvaro observes that Maduro apparently became so frightened that he lost his clear judgment. In ch. 34, Alvaro explains Maduro's role in the expedition: Carlos showed him a chart of the approach to [[solanta|Solanta]], and it was Maduro, “versed in all kinds of politics,” who reasoned that the Muari would not allow them to trade, but that the expedition could be organized differently. Maduro aspires to become an allen, and Alvaro believes the Well's curse (see [[temnyj-shyonbrun]]) hangs over him: a man who has visited Last Winter is unlikely to gain a seat at the High Table. Fatima says Maduro told her in horror what had happened to the philosopher.

## Fate (ch. 34)
Maduro remains a manufacturer; his hired man Salvador is killed, but the effect on his employer is not shown in the novel.
