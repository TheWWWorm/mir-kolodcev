Otto is the secretary and apprentice of the fischmeister [[joriksen|Fritjof Joriksen]]. To [[ditrikh|Dietrich von Dort]], he seems a warmhearted chatterbox who willingly talks about the guild and his master but reveals nothing of substance. Who he really is becomes clear toward the end of the novel.

## Character and appearance

Otto is pale and sickly-looking, but his gaze is lively and pleasant. He talks nonstop and jokes about it himself: in his view, talkativeness is not the worst of sins. His favorite exclamation is “God’s white birds.” His sincerity is so disarming that Dietrich trusts him immediately.

## Joriksen’s secretary (ch. 21)

Otto opens the door to Dietrich when he comes to see Joriksen and immediately introduces himself as the master’s secretary and pupil. In the reception room that doubles as a museum, he shows him the collection of curiosities and explains how the master received an icon from an emir, but his chatter only depresses Dietrich. He then studies a thick book at his desk: he is required to know every schönbrun east of [[khofshtadt|Hofstadt]] by heart, including its location and markings. Later, when Dietrich discovers the guild’s atlas on his desk, Otto explains that it contains no secrets. He calls Joriksen the best fisherman of his generation, a pupil of Magnus [[amir-sedoj|Amir the Grey]] who has not yet become an oberfischmeister, and assures him that the master is honest with clients once a deal has been concluded. He also warns that people will come to question the master about a recent incident at three o’clock and asks Dietrich to avoid encountering them.

## The journey to the station (ch. 25)

Otto invites Dietrich to the station to buy tickets to Ollandia. On the way, Dietrich asks about dark schönbruns, and Otto explains in detail: fish are rarely caught in a Well, so it is usually safe, but there is no way to predict this. A dark schönbrun can cause illness of body or mind, loss of memory, love, or strength, and financial ruin. It cannot kill directly, but can lead to death indirectly — trembling hands before a duel, or fear of dogs. The strangers visible in the mist live “on the other side of the window.” For more on the concept, see [[kolodets|Schönbrun]] and [[temnyj-shyonbrun|Dark Schönbrun]].

## The baroness’s house and the train (ch. 29)

When Otto arrives with Milosh at Baroness [[elza|Walbrecht’s]] house, where Prince [[lents|Lenz]] is demanding satisfaction from Dietrich, the lieutenant fears that the secretary will reveal his true surname. Otto politely greets everyone in order of rank and gives nothing away. On the train, he joins Dietrich and Joriksen in the conversation with the magnus: the Grey introduces Otto as Joriksen’s apprentice since the previous winter and remarks on his good fortune in becoming the master’s personal assistant immediately. When the magnus asks whether he knows what happened in [[dunkvald|Dunkwald]], Otto replies that he is afraid even to think about it.

## Klein’s son (ch. 31)

In Latsen, Major [[klyajn|Klein]] meets Otto. Otto calls him “Father,” describes the gendarmes seizing Dietrich at the station, and chatters for five minutes without conveying anything useful, for which Klein praises him. The narrator explains that the father is not proud of everything about his son. Otto was little when Wolf was sent to serve in Dunkwald, and his mother, raising him alone, instilled compassion, a dislike of drill, and dreams of the schönbruns’ miracles. On the other hand, Otto controls what he says: his chatter creates the image of a good-natured fool and contains no secrets.

Klein takes Otto and Joriksen to the well in the hillside and suggests that Otto look down. Otto takes out a glass flask with a silver coating on one side — under the rule “water cleanses water,” a dark schönbrun must not be examined without an instrument — and peers into the Well, then runs outside and vomits: a rotting corpse lies below. His father teaches him to see through other people’s masks. In Klein’s view, Dietrich is unpredictable and dangerous, although Otto thinks they could have cooperated with the lieutenant. Joriksen reminds Klein that he is risking his son too: if they rush their work with an unfamiliar dark schönbrun, “both will be blown up.”

## Relationships

- **[[joriksen|Joriksen]]** — his mentor; Otto speaks of him with admiration, but admits to Dietrich that he gives the master trouble by being a “stubborn layabout.”
- **[[klyajn|Klein]]** — his father. Joriksen knows whose son Otto is and calls the major by his first name, like an old friend.
- **[[ditrikh|Dietrich]]** — someone to whom Otto readily talks about the guild; judging by ch. 31, however, he reveals exactly as much as his father needs.

## At the end of the novel (ch. 31)

After ch. 31, Otto remains with Joriksen: Klein demands that within two weeks the master “fish” for General [[khofmajer|Hofmeyer]] in an unknown dark schönbrun, to which Joriksen replies that it is absurd. The novel does not say what happens to Otto.
