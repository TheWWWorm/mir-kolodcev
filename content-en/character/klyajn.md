**Wolfgang von Klein**, or **Wolf**, is an officer of the [[kogti-kajzera|Kaiser’s Claws]], Dangar's special service. In [[red|Red's]] storyline, he is initially known as the **Black Falcon**: a silent man in black with a falcon badge on his sleeve. Different storylines show different stages of his service—a lieutenant in Dunkwald and a major in Latsen.

## The Black Falcon in Dunkwald (ch. 4–8)
Red first sees him in [[joriksen|Fritjof Joriksen's]] residence: a man in black, quiet as a shadow, with an iron falcon badge on his sleeve. When Yakka and Joriksen laugh at Red's gestures, the Falcon makes no sound and gazes sleepily at the ceiling.

At the church service in Chapter 8, he feeds the slaves porridge while wearing a black glove: he is disgusted by the thought of touching their mouths. Red refuses to open his mouth. For the first time, a predatory interest flashes in the Falcon's eyes; he repeats “Sorge um dich,” then smiles, gives the porridge to another slave, and claps Red on the shoulder.

## The Falcon and the Captive (ch. 12–19)
In Chapter 12, the Falcon and Joriksen bow to a visiting fischmeister in an Arctic fox coat. In Chapter 15, the Falcon listens to the argument about Red's fate: interest and pride can be heard in his voice, as though he had predicted the escape. When Baron [[landau|Landau]] demands to examine the sick man, the Falcon reluctantly draws a “Mardur”—a compact Catalian revolver of 0.29 caliber. The sight of the weapon makes Red long for his native Catalia.

Later, the Falcon feeds Red, tells him with the help of a dictionary that they will fulfill his dream, and lays a pen and paper on the table. Together with Joriksen, he asks the captive what it means to be a Catalian. When the letter disappears in Chapter 19, Red rules the Falcon out as a suspect, considering him too insignificant for such a dirty trick. In the temple, the Falcon and Joriksen feed Red and take pride in him.

## The Lieutenant and the “Falcon” in Dunkwald (ch. 23–27)
In [[red|Red's]] storyline, in Chapter 23, Lieutenant Klein visits Greta's house in the women's settlement of the [[dunkvald|Dunkwald]] camp, where Red lies unconscious. He wears a patch bearing a silver falcon, reminds Baron [[landau|Landau]] that he answers only to Schlissel and the Kaiser, and receives a lesson from the baron in an oberfischmeister's power: his eyelids stick together and his arm goes rigid. He later comes with the hunter [[yakka|Yakka]] to see Red after he regains consciousness—this “falcon” speaks Catalian with the help of a dictionary and gives his name as Wolf, meaning “wolf.” Red is surprised: “A falcon named Wolf?”

In Chapter 27, Klein visits the shackled Red at night and, in broken Catalian, tells the story of the camp in the third person. It becomes clear that he himself is Wolf: a “Claw,” only a second-generation eagle, sent to Dunkwald because that is where the unlucky and low-born go. On the journey, he befriended the young fischmeister [[joriksen|Fritjof Joriksen]] and learned Catalian, believing the Kaiser would one day need officers who knew the enemy. He gave Fritjof his “word as an eagle” that he would help, and sent a forged letter to the camp about the fischmeister's mother's illness. At the end of the scene, Klein shows Red the Laerta Herald with his portrait: he has discovered who Red is and told no one. He offers to grant one wish, tosses him a penknife, and calls it a lesson that Red “failed to learn.”

## Dunkwald After the Departure (ch. 33)
Lieutenant Wolf Klein attends the executions performed by Red, the “beast,” and near the end of the second year comes to say goodbye: Fritjof has kept his promise, and Wolf is being transferred to [[khofshtadt|Hofstadt]]. Asked for a pistol, he replies that in Dangar one must wait for permission. At the end of the chapter, the tribute collector informs the baron that the order to take the Catalian came on the recommendation of the Kaiser’s Claws; Landau spits and recognizes its author: “Klein…”

## Dietrich's Interrogation (ch. 6)
In Latsen, Major Klein receives Lieutenant [[ditrikh|Dietrich von Dort]] in the town hall. He looks good-natured: round, bulging eyes, shaggy eyebrows, curled mustache, and a black undress uniform with silver falcons on the sleeves. Introducing himself, he jokes about his “comical” surname and calls Dietrich a hero. He then methodically builds a case against him: he lays five bullets on the table—five inexplicable oddities in the battle at the station—and offers a version in which Dietrich organized the stones' escape, the commander's murder, and a witness's disappearance. The conclusion is “traitor to the motherland.” Dietrich is suspended from service while the investigation continues; the major cites witnesses who confirm that the lieutenant had no weapon on him.

## The Major in Latsen (ch. 31)
Several years later, at the height of the invasion of Ollandia, Major Wolfgang von Klein appears at a conference with Field Marshal [[rajniger|Reiniger]]: his specialty is Catalia; he knows everything about it to be found in newspapers, books, and intelligence reports, and has spies in Laerta. He likes bay horses and dreams of a patch bearing a red phoenix. To achieve that, he believes, he needs a war, and he subtly encourages Lieutenant General [[khofmajer|Hofmeyer]] to delay his divisions' departure so that he will have to take Mershants.

This also reveals how long he has been connected with Joriksen: the master greets him by name (“May your hand be strong, Wolf”) and complains about the lieutenant's arrest; Klein asks, “Remember this? ‘I believe in you, Catallian’”—a phrase from the Dunkwald camp. Klein himself was in Dunkwald when his son [[otto|Otto]] was small, and describes him as a boy raised by his mother. He finds Dietrich's well, demands that Joriksen study it for the army within two weeks, and wants to send Dietrich to Mershants. At the gendarmerie, Olkha and Katrina von Lurd make him stand, and the major is forced to apologize; inwardly he repeats the parable of a snake lying on a turtle: a partnership in which both will die.

## Names and Chronology (ch. 23–33)
The Black Falcon, Wolf, Lieutenant Klein, and Major Wolfgang von Klein are one character. In Chapter 23, the Falcon introduces himself to Red as Wolf and translates his own name as “wolf.” In Chapter 27, Lieutenant Klein tells his own story in the third person, calling himself Wolf. In Chapter 31, the major recalls his service in Dunkwald and repeats to Joriksen the words once addressed to Red: “I believe in you, Catallian.”

The difference in rank is explained by the timing of the events: Red's camp story takes place before Dietrich's storyline. In Chapter 33, Wolf is transferred to Hofstadt near the end of the second year, and by the events of Chapter 6 he is already a major. The book does not state how many years passed between those points.

## Character (ch. 6–33)
Klein is a man whose gentle manner coexists with merciless logic. In Chapter 6, he is kindly and caustic; in Chapter 27, he “overcomes his own pity”; in Chapter 31, he arrests Dietrich to make him controllable and is ready without hesitation to send him under rifled howitzer fire. Order matters more to him than compassion: he tells Red that in Dangar, “order rules, not chaos.” Yet he is honorable in friendship: he fulfills his promise to help Joriksen and keeps his word.

## Conception and Interviews
The author did not discuss Klein separately in either of the two streams. His general remarks that the protagonists' storylines are offset in time and will later “merge” explain why the same Dangarian appears at different stages of his service.
