Don Balboa owns an arms factory in [[laerta|Laerta]] and is one of the manufacturers of the esfera of Production. The reader knows of him only through other people's accounts: he does not appear in any scenes, but his decisions set the events of ch. 11 in motion.

## The Obreros' Riot (ch. 11)
According to [[lyusil|Lucille]], two days earlier obreros dismissed because of steam-powered machines, backed by bankers from the esfera of Finance, challenged the factory-owning dons to a debate in the [[palata-slova|Chamber of the Word]]. The manufacturers did not attend, sending lawyers from the esfera of Law instead; the friends believe they were directed by [[fatima|Fatima-allen]]. The obreros lost the debate, went to Balboa's arms factory, and were beaten there by guards and Shadow's thugs. Newspapers repeated the factory's account of “madmen with torches,” which outraged the esferas of Craft and Service.

The narrator observes that Balboa himself had already miscalculated: had he honestly admitted ordering the dismissed workers to be beaten, he would have been called cruel, but it would not have caused a sensation. News of the lie, however, inflamed the crowd. The obreros want to stop the train from Brega carrying a shipment of steam-powered machines specifically for Balboa, and [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]] becomes caught up in the fighting on the tracks.

## Maduro's Ally (ch. 13)
[[salvador|Salvador]], a guard at Maduro's mansion, previously fought on Balboa's side. He tells Alvaro that Maduro and Balboa have formed an alliance to keep the reformists in check. Balboa is not mentioned again in the novel after ch. 13.
