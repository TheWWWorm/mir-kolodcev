Lid Anteodor is a hero of Catalian tales, a great knight whose victory over the hosts of the Muari ruler Faid-Musa is known throughout the country. He is present in the novel only in conversations and songs: his name exemplifies the scale of a victory in [[duelnyj-spor-ramiresa|Alvaro’s debate with Ramires]] and he is the beloved hero of legends that [[red|Red]] knows and sings.

## His image in legend (ch. 15)
Catalians consider Anteodor their own in a very real sense: each of Catalia’s five lands claims to be the knight’s birthplace. There are therefore a great many stories about him — at least five complete sets and numerous smaller variants. The novel says nothing about his origins or biography beyond these tales.

As Red recounts them, the tales of Anteodor combine the buoyant optimism of stories about the [[mirny|mirnas]] with stern lessons about the power of the [[drevnie|Ancients]]. Hidalgo Lid is brave but not stubborn, decisive but not blind. He charges into battle when necessary, but also knows how to reach an agreement when words can settle the matter. To achieve his aim, he is ready to use cunning, guile, and even seduction; in a hopeless situation, he can retreat to save himself and his warriors.

## Mention in the debate (ch. 1)
In the first chapter, Alvaro da Silva, certain of victory, promises [[vinsente-ramires|Vincente Ramires]] that he will defeat him in the dueling debate “more terribly than the hosts of Faid-Musa were defeated by the great Lid Anteodor.” The comparison assumes that the listener needs no explanation of the feat: every Catalian knows the legend. Alvaro ultimately loses the debate.

## Red and the legends of Anteodor (ch. 15)
Red, a Catalian condemned to forced labor in Dangar, has loved the tales of Anteodor since his youth and committed to memory the versions he heard in different regions. They form the third and most personal “layer” of his store of stories; the first two are the parables about the mirnas told by his mother and sea legends about the lords of the abyss. At night, while digging the pit for a schönbrun, he quietly sings them to himself.

Red admires the knight not for courage or strength — he has enough of those himself — but for the flexibility he cannot find in himself: when angered, he becomes ferocious and charges into battle. The author puts it this way: “Red was a bull, while Lid was a snow leopard.”

Red recounts one legend to [[greta|Greta]], who does not understand a word but listens “with her whole soul.” The king sends Anteodor to crush Faid-Musa. Lid’s army crosses the Muari Desert, but vultures spot it and report to the Muari ruler. A powerful sorcerer, he makes a sacrifice and asks the Black Maiden for help. She sends four demons against the army: Ishmal (Thirst), who dries up wells and poisons springs; Vinshah (Disease); Avissaya (Loneliness), who kills messengers and carrier birds so that the soldiers receive no news from home; and Mirshella (Ignorance), who confuses maps and shifts the dunes. When despair overcomes the warriors and nothing remains but to lie down in the sand, Red asks: what did Lid do then? He has no time to answer: Greta’s next customer knocks at the door, and the tale breaks off. The novel does not say how the legend ended.
