**Fatima Ir-Reya** is prim-allen of the esfera of Law, one of the lawyers’ three representatives at the [[vysokij-stol|High Table]]. At first she is heard about through others: [[obrero|obreros]] call her a “serpent” and “Iolanta’s merciless sword,” her associates wear bracelets with a black snake, and she herself rarely appears in court, directing her pupils from the shadows. To [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]], she is one of the five heroes of the [[solantijskaya-ekspeditsiya|Solantan Expedition]] whom [[filippe|Don Filippe]] asks him to write about, and the one he fears most: the lawyers of Law whom she leads have long symbolized the law’s cold power. The novel repeatedly compares her to the mirna [[iolanta|Iolanta]], patron of law.

## Character and position
Fatima heads an esfera whose craft is debate. Her pupils, the “little serpents,” enter the [[palata-slova|Chamber of the Word]] with a single task: to win. Even one who is not among her best, [[vinsente-ramires|Vincente Ramires]], easily defeated Alvaro in his first debate. Her detractors call her the “judicial cobra” and “serpent.” According to Fernan, she does not personally address juries but oversees important cases. The early chapters say only one thing about her youth: Fatima was once an ordinary lawyer.

## The serpents of Law and the manufacturers (ch. 1–20)
Fatima’s name first arises in [[kataliya|Catalia]] in ch. 1: the advocate who crushed Alvaro in the debate about “one equaling seven” wears a bracelet with a black snake, marking him as a member of her circle. In ch. 5, while listing the five heroes of the future book, Fernan calls her “the chief snake of the judicial rabble.” In ch. 7, the pirate [[karlos|Carlos el Niña]] brushes aside questions about the Solantan Expedition, saying it was all “talk” and telling them to ask Fatima.

Her reputation is largely shaped by the industrial crisis. Steam-powered machines throw workers onto the streets; obreros challenge the manufacturers to a debate, but the latter send the lawyers of Law, and the workers lose (ch. 11). Later, in ch. 20, Fernan and [[mor|Mor]] explain that the lawyers of Law have won more than this case: they also demolished the bankers’ case in the dispute over loans for workers. In the past, the esfera of Law did not side with manufacturers in such cases; now it does, and Fatima herself “oversaw” the handpicked “vipers.” Mor asks Alvaro to influence her: if Fatima forbids the serpents to represent the manufacturers, the obreros could win a new debate. Alvaro concludes that they want to put him in the allen’s bed and leaves in anger.

Another thread is connected to this: in ch. 5, [[khuanes|Doctor Juanes’s]] widow related that, shortly before her husband’s death, the prim-allen of Law visited him, received a bad diagnosis, and stormed out of the study, slamming the door. In ch. 18, Carlos adds that the doctor “talked too much,” which displeased both him and Fatima.

## Alvaro’s theory of an affair with da Vira (ch. 22–34)
In ch. 22, Alvaro and [[beatris|Beatriz]] search the [[svitok|Great Scroll]] for records about Solanta’s heroes. Beatriz notes that Fatima is half Solantan, something Fatima herself later confirms. Alvaro learns that Fatima Ir-Reya was married in the year of the expedition, and that after the navigator [[red|Rodrigo da Vira’s]] death she and Carlos secured him a posthumous Order of Courage: on the night the coastal fortress was taken, he supposedly covered a thrown grenade with his body. From these fragments Alvaro constructs a theory: on the island Fatima had an affair with da Vira, became pregnant, and the doctor disclosed the secret to her husband, while the child grew up to become an avenger. The theory proves false. In ch. 30, Alvaro acknowledges its collapse, and in ch. 34 Fatima herself laughs at it: she could not stand da Vira, nor he her, and there was no affair.

## The stranger (ch. 16–28)
Alvaro and Fatima first meet without exchanging names. In [[khose-flarbo|Jose Flarbo’s]] shop (ch. 16), he encounters an intelligent customer in a gray dress who deduces his profession from his bracelet and callus but does not recognize his face. She writes an address and time on the flyleaf of the book she has bought, but gives no name. The address turns out to be an anatomical theater; she takes Alvaro to a public autopsy (ch. 24), arriving in a black steam carriage driven by her gloomy female chauffeur, [[frej|Frey]], while Alvaro takes her for a political commentator. In the former royal wine cellar near the Bridge Bastion (ch. 26), wearing a dark-red dress and gold snake bracelets without esfera insignia, and holding a Muari pipe, she explains the balance of power in the election of the new supremo, scheduled for six months later. Suspecting nothing, Alvaro responds by inventing Fatima the allen’s “secret love” for a corsair. That night they become lovers.

Alvaro wakes in her bedroom the following morning (ch. 28). He studies the room like a detective: black silk sheets, but beside them a diary, newspapers, a clock, and ten bookmarked books, including his own novel as well as The Philosophy of Horror and a work on [[dangar|Dangarian]] politics; she waters the flowers in the garden herself. Then General [[dezerej|Deserey]] arrives. Alvaro eavesdrops at the secret door and realizes who she is. Deserey asks “ma donna” to secure mobilization at the High Table: six months after [[ollandiya|Ollandia’s]] surrender to Dangar, the Dangarian armies have not left, while Catalia has only fifteen combat-ready divisions against the fifty that the empire could redeploy. At least two hundred thousand new soldiers are needed from among the unemployed and former servicemen. Fatima replies that this is political suicide: the money would have to come from the unpopular “[[tri-byka|Three Bulls]],” and if the Kaiser changed his mind about attacking, she would be removed at the very first purge. Deserey says that after “what happened to the doctor,” they cannot wage war; Fatima objects that above all it is he who cannot command an army, whereas she is under no obligation to be a victim: she has already performed her heroic deed on [[solanta|Solanta]]. The general replies that she is doomed anyway, and that it is better to die for Catalia.

The conversation after the general’s departure becomes a scene of mutual accusations. Fatima is stunned that Alvaro failed to recognize her: she is half Solantan — her mother is from the island, and she grew up there and knows the language, which is why she was taken on the expedition — a heroine of Solanta, and he is writing a book about her. She knew who he was from the start and had made inquiries beforehand: his loss of seven thousand to her secretary, his fight on the side of the unemployed, his defense of Mor’s servants. She was checking whether he was [[filippe|Filippe’s]] spy or a simpleton; she left unanswered the letters Alvaro sent to Solanta’s heroes. Hurt, Alvaro strikes a sore point by mentioning Rodrigo da Vira. Fatima shouts “Out!” and throws a shoe after him. Outside, he is hit by a vehicle bearing the letters ESP: its driver is Beatriz, Fatima’s daughter, who has concealed their relationship.

## Beatriz’s mother (ch. 28–34)
Beatriz Enhal is her daughter from her marriage to Judge [[sudya-enkhal|Enhal]]. They divorced after Solanta; the girl left her mother to live with her father and join the [[akademiya-iskusstv|Academy of Arts]], so that she would not be compared with Fatima. Beatriz calls her mother arrogant, devious, and selfish, but stresses that she did not lie about the important things: Fatima has no son of the kind Alvaro imagined. Fatima herself says almost nothing about her daughter; in ch. 34, they trade barbs in front of Alvaro like people long accustomed to this tone. The novel offers no further evidence to confirm or disprove Beatriz’s account of the Ir-Reya family.

Another detail emerges in ch. 30: Fatima reads The Philosophy of Horror before bed, and Deserey visited her for breakfast. Alvaro tells Fernan this while considering how the five heroes secretly keep in contact.

## Confession in the castle (ch. 34)
At [[posledniaya-zima|Last Winter]], after the fight with the killers, Fatima and Frey enter the cellar containing the dark [[temnyj-shyonbrun|schönbrun]]. Fatima asks them not to look into the well, then decides to “play fair,” at least with Alvaro and Beatriz. Her account adds what they did not know:

- After Solanta, da Vira was believed terminally ill in a Solantan prison; she helped Carlos win the court case that declared the navigator dead, and no one tried to buy him out of slavery.
- Later, van Halm wrote to the five heroes saying that he had seen them in the well and invited them to his castle. Expecting blackmail, Fatima took guards with her; what the well showed was not people, and she gives no details.
- Two days later, Carlos received a threatening letter from the living da Vira: he wrote that his revenge had already been accomplished and that the Two-Faced One’s bullet would soon find its target. He blamed the heroes for abandoning him and failing to care for his son; according to the Scroll, [[karmensita-estevos|Carmensita Estevos]] had petitioned the court concerning the child. The secret route to Solanta was drawn on the reverse.
- She composed the anonymous letter to Doctor Juanes herself: the physician insisted that he was right, and she wanted to rub his nose in his mistake.
- After learning from [[kristobal-maduro|Maduro]] what had happened to the philosopher, she traveled incognito to [[tres-molinos|Tres Molinos]] with Frey alone, found a rusty component in the castle, and understood how the well of misfortune works. She says she saw the final link in the chain of coincidences at the autopsy in the anatomical theater.

She states the three laws of the schönbrun of misfortune as follows: whoever looked into the water at a particular time becomes a victim; calamity arrives through chance; misfortune strikes what is dearest. That is why she asks Alvaro and Beatriz, “…do not become too dear to me.” Alvaro kisses her. Ten alguacils and five cars have come with her; Frey keeps the wounded killer at gunpoint.

## Relationships (ch. 34)
- [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] — first an enemy from afar, then a lover; she learned everything about him in advance, while he knew only rumors about her.
- [[beatris|Beatriz]] — her daughter. Their relationship is strained, but Fatima comes to the castle after warning her daughter of the danger.
- [[dezerej|Gabriel Deserey]] — the general who addresses her as “ma donna.”
- [[frej|Frey]] and Yasmina — her bodyguard-driver and housekeeper-companion.
- [[karlos|Carlos el Niña]], [[kristobal-maduro|Maduro]], Deserey, and Juanes — the other heroes of Solanta.

## At the end of the novel (ch. 34)
Fatima opens up to Alvaro, but worries about the future. As they travel together to [[laerta|Laerta]], he observes that calamity may come if they love each other more deeply. She replies, “That is tomorrow’s Fatima’s problem,” and kisses him. The outcomes of the supremo election, mobilization, and Fatima’s own fate remain open in the novel.
