Gabriel Deserey is a general in the Catalian army and third on the list of heroes whom Prim-Allen [[filippe|Filippe di Berro]] commissions [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]] to describe. He took part in the [[solantijskaya-ekspeditsiya|Solantan Expedition]], but he stands out in the novel for another reason: he is the character most concerned by the threat of war with [[dangar|Dangar]] and prepared to demonstrate it with figures. This anxiety drives him in his scenes with Alvaro and [[fatima|Fatima]].

## Character and appearance
Deserey is polite and warm, yet somewhat detached. He speaks of his children and subordinates with warmth, but as though from a distance. He takes literature seriously: he admires Alvaro’s prose, but criticizes the fighting in his novels with professional severity. He keeps diaries and promises to give them to the writer.
## Meeting at Ortiyar (ch. 9)
Deserey invites Alvaro by letter to an inspection at [[ortiyar|Ortiyar]] fortress, thirty miles from [[laerta|Laerta]]. The writer arrives with [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan Gereira]] and [[beatris|Beatriz]]. The general introduces him to the officers as one of literature’s great hopes, but over lunch dissects one of his novels: the marquis who acts as strategist divides his forces, conducts no reconnaissance, and meddles in tactics; Cousin Ilana sends her vassals into a hopeless attack; their opponents are even more foolish; and the battle scenes resemble a provincial theater production. Beatriz argues that wars are decided by artillery, whose power depends on the number of shots; Deserey agrees with her, and Alvaro promises revenge.

Deserey asks what Alvaro thinks of Dangar. Alvaro replies that he sees the empire as an obsolete monster of slavery; the general reminds him that the Kaiser crushed the Ollandian princes and Ollandia has become Dangar’s vassal. He then arranges a demonstration of canister fired at four barrels, reducing them to splinters. His figures are as follows: Catalia’s armed forces number 270,000, of whom 140,000 are fit for combat — 40,000 at sea and 100,000 on land; the Kaiser can send a million, lose half a million while Catalia loses 50,000, and then send a second million. He asks Alvaro to make fear of Dangar the book’s main theme: “make the readers afraid.”

When Alvaro mentions the other heroes during their conversation, Deserey refuses to recommend him to the remaining two: he has not seen any of them for six months and could not attend Juanes’s funeral. Alvaro, however, finds his name in [[khuanes|Juanes’s]] patient register for the day of the doctor’s death and writes a note to Fernan saying that the general is lying (ch. 9). On the way back, a green steam car follows their carriage, but its driver turns out to be an ordinary townsman (ch. 11).

## The diaries and the mystery of Juanes’s death (ch. 11–24)
The general sends Alvaro a box of diaries (ch. 13); the last volume is missing. The mystery is resolved in ch. 24, revealing why he needed to conceal his visit to the doctor: it was Deserey who brought Juanes a bottle of Almagarian bonk, with a vine on the vessel, as a gift, and it was his medicine that the doctor accidentally drank while absorbed in the general’s story. The novel does not show whether Deserey himself knows exactly how Juanes died, but in ch. 28 he tells Fatima that “after what happened to the doctor,” they cannot wage war. Alvaro hears this phrase through the keyhole.

## The appeal to Fatima (ch. 28)
Having woken in the “stranger’s” house, Alvaro eavesdrops on Deserey’s visit. The general addresses his hostess as “ma donna,” the form used when asking a great favor. He reports that six months after [[ollandiya|Ollandia’s]] surrender, the Dangarian troops have not withdrawn: Kurtzleben’s and [[brojenshtoff|Brojenstoff’s]] armies remain in the center of the country, [[khofmajer|Hofmeyer’s]] army is in [[mershants|Mershants]] near Catalia’s border, the [[plyutva|Plyutva]]–[[latsen|Latsen]]–Mershants railway is nearing completion, and Hofmeyer now has 25 siege mortars. Catalia has only fifteen combat-ready divisions, while Dangar can field as many as fifty against them.

He asks her to raise the question of mobilization at the [[vysokij-stol|High Table]]: at least two hundred thousand unemployed men and former soldiers should be called into the esfera of War. This would cost three hundred million escudos in the first month and a hundred million in every subsequent month. Field Marshal Cortago will not propose it himself: the Table regards requests from the military as motivated by greed. This is how Alvaro learns that his lover is Fatima Ir-Reya, prim-allen of Law. Fatima replies that it would be suicide: the money would have to come from the hated “Three Bulls,” and if the Kaiser changed his mind about attacking, she would be removed at the first purge. Deserey objects that a war with such an imbalance of forces would be ruinous and that “after what happened to the doctor,” they cannot fight. Fatima retorts that he, specifically, cannot command an army, whereas she has no reason to become a victim: she performed a heroic deed on [[solanta|Solanta]]. Deserey replies that she is already doomed, and that it is better to die for Catalia than pointlessly.

## The spy and the agents (ch. 30–34)
In Deserey’s diaries, which Alvaro and Fernan read on the train and in Tres Molinos (ch. 30–32), thirteen notebooks are devoted mainly to Dangar: its armies, ranks, the Ollandian campaign, and its commanders. The general considers Reiniger an experienced coward, [[brojenshtoff|Brojenstoff]] a career soldier, and Kurtzleben a butcher, while naming the young Hofmeyer as the best. Yet every mention of the heroes of Solanta has disappeared from the last year, and he has not sent the first diary, which describes the expedition itself, at all.

Alvaro comes to the general staff with Deserey’s note and persuades him that a Dangarian spy is hunting him and demanding that he abandon his warning book. He proposes to “give” the spy to the general: Deserey’s men should watch whoever is watching the writer. The general agrees, hands over a thousand escudos, and promises “surveillance”; when Alvaro tosses out “The Philosophy of Horror!” the general flinches (ch. 30). Later, Alvaro admits to Fernan that he lied: he does not believe in the spy and needs the money.

In [[tres-molinos|Tres Molinos]], Deserey’s agents mistake a pair of lovers for spies, then seize [[beatris|Beatriz]] as a “spy” (ch. 32–34). On the night the castle is stormed, two agents fall — one is killed, the other badly wounded. According to Fatima, Deserey is “playing his own clumsy game,” while Alvaro suspects that the agents had secret orders to check whether he had reached the Well.

## Conception and interviews
The author said that, when creating the general, he recalled a military acquaintance who reads the Polari books and sends five pages of criticism — that a battle is described incorrectly, or the swords are the wrong length — before ending with “really cool.” The author’s remarks also indicate that General Deserey is pessimistic about war with Catalia, as are the Dangarian generals on their side: the rulers of both sides fear war.

## Fate (ch. 34)
At the end of the novel, Deserey remains in service. Alvaro supposes that the dark schönbrun’s “misfortune” has not yet caught up with him, and suspects that catastrophe awaits him on the battlefield. The novel does not show what became of his request for mobilization.
