Brojenstoff is a Dangarian general of infantry, one of the three commanders of the armies that invaded [[ollandiya|Ollandia]]. He appears in person only once in the novel, at a conference with Field Marshal [[rajniger|Reiniger]]; on other occasions, the Catalians discuss him.

## The Conference in Latsen (ch. 31)
The senior officers of the Ollandian theater sit around a green table at the field marshal's headquarters in [[latsen|Latsen]]: the “lean” Lieutenant General [[khofmajer|Hofmeyer]], two “fleshy” generals of infantry—[[kurtsleben|Kurtzleben]] and Brojenstoff—and Reiniger himself. The field marshal calls on Brojenstoff to report first.

After the capture of Latsen, the three armies went their separate ways: Brojenstoff led his into the northern “shepherd” provinces, Kurtzleben went south toward [[bargot|Bargot]], and Hofmeyer went east toward [[mershants|Mershants]]. The narrator calls Brojenstoff the least capable of the three commanders, while noting that little had been expected of him in the first place. Northern Ollandia is mountainous, and the Dangarian infantry of [[kamni|stones]] is weak in the mountains. The Ollandians staged ambushes, destroyed supply trains, and blew up depots, but avoided the large battles the general dreamed of.

The red-faced general blames his setbacks on the enemy's baseness and lack of honor:

> “What we are witnessing is not war according to the laws of strategy, but some sort of guerrilla warfare or peasant uprising.”

He promises to corner the northerners and crush them, but Reiniger is doubtful: the autumn rains will wash out the roads, leaving the army stranded. Still, the field marshal is not overly troubled by Brojenstoff's failures: the plan calls for him chiefly to distract the northerners, preventing them from helping the southerners, and he is managing that task “after a fashion.” In Reiniger's new plan, his task remains the same: advance north as far as he can without pursuing territorial gains, because “the Kaiser sees no value in sheep-covered hills.”

When Hofmeyer proposes a night sortie against Mershants by dismounted cavalry, Brojenstoff gives a chuckle. After the conference, Hofmeyer tells Major [[klyajn|Klein]] that Kurtzleben, Brojenstoff, and the field marshal himself are all convinced Catalia will utterly defeat Dangar, and that describing their current difficulties is intended to convey this thought to the Kaiser.

## Through Catalian Eyes (ch. 28, 32)
In ch. 28, General [[dezerej|Deserey]] says that six months after Ollandia's surrender, the armies of Kurtzleben and Brojenstoff are still concentrated in its central lands. Deserey's journals, which [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] and [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]] read in ch. 32, contain portraits of the Dangarian commanders: he considers Brojenstoff a “dim-witted career soldier.”
