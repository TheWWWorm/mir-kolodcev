The Kaiser is Dangar’s ruler. He does not appear as a character in Traces on the Water, but is constantly invoked: people fight, serve, and arrange marriages for his sake. The novel does not give the sovereign’s name; the characters know him as “the Kaiser,” “the emperor,” or “His Majesty.”

## What is known about the ruler (ch. 2–33)
- **Power and the army.** Regulations prescribe the response to a greeting: “By the great Kaiser’s victory! Our service is our pride!” Officers regard their duty to the Kaiser as paramount, and Dietrich speaks to Major Klein of duty to the Kaiser and the fatherland. The special division known as the [[kogti-kajzera|Kaiser’s Claws]] obeys orders only from Schlissel and calls itself the sword in the Kaiser’s hand.
- **Preferences.** According to Count [[lurd|Lurd]], the emperor likes meek, modest people and has a weakness for artillery. In matters of dynastic marriage, he usually trusts Lurd’s own analysis.
- **Information.** He reads Count [[varkhajt|Wahrheit’s]] Glory of the Empire, and in chapter 14 the baroness relates that the Kaiser doubted her word but agreed once he heard that Glory had reported the matter.
- **Three powers.** According to Lurd, Dangar has three centers of influence: the emperor, the patriarch, and the magnus. Magnus [[amir-sedoj|Amir the Grey]] says that the Kaiser is strongly influenced by the patriarch and dines with him on Tuesdays and Saturdays.
- **Conquests.** Dangar has conquered Ollandia ([[olandskaya-vojna|Ollandian War]]); the emperor seeks a reliable governor for the conquered lands. In chapter 31, Major Klein tells Field Marshal [[rajniger|Reiniger]] that the emperor wishes to know what obstacles Dangar would face in a possible war with [[kataliya|Catalia]].

## Conception and interviews
According to the author in the first stream, Dangar’s court affairs and the Kaiser himself will appear in the next World of Wells book, rather than in Traces on the Water. He said it would contain much about the relationships between the Kaiser, the patriarch, and the chief magnus. In the book, Dangar has a German-Prussian style in its ranks, clothing, and Kaiser, although the author stresses that “Dangar is not Prussia.”
