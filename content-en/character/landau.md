Baron von Landau commands the slave camp at [[dunkvald|Dunkwald]], where the [[gildiya-rybakov|Fishermen’s Guild]] seeks a way to create a [[kolodets|schönbrun]]. He is arrogant, pedantic, and convinced that any soul can be understood and given a suitable “Zustand”; it is precisely this conviction that turns [[red|Red]] into a chained “beast.”

## Character and appearance
Landau is a gray-haired, wrinkled old man with an aquiline nose and aristocratic bearing. He wears an azure cloak with a gold catfish brooch, the emblem of a senior master fisherman, and smokes a pipe. He is shortsighted and squeamish: he does not touch the sick, but uses a listening tube after ordering an assistant to undress the patient. Like all fishermen, he is a physician, and diagnoses Red with “sarcoma of the lungs.” An arrogant nobleman, he despises slaves and resents his exile: he dreams of returning to the capital, and in ch. 27 of a margrave’s title and a seat at the Kaiser’s table.

The baron’s name appears in two forms in the book. In ch. 15, he introduces himself as Alfred von Landau, while in ch. 23, 27, and 33 the narration calls him Friedrich. The second form is more frequent; the first occurs only when he speaks for himself.

## Arrival (ch. 15)
Landau is sent as senior commander to the camp previously run by the young [[joriksen|Joriksen]], after the former fischmeister’s death on the road. He first sees Red, returned from his escape, as a runaway slave deserving execution. Using prepared dictionary phrases, he speaks to him, examines him, agrees with the diagnosis, and declares that a swift execution would be merciful for a dying man. He notices that the slave has no brand or stone around his neck and reproaches Joriksen for weakness. When Red answers Joriksen’s question “Who are you?” with “a Catalian,” the baron concedes defeat and calls Red a beast.

## The experimental camp (ch. 19)
In ch. 19, the baron personally shows Red what is happening in the camp: hundreds of slaves dig wells under elaborate conditions — with bare hands, a spoon, a bottle shard, an awl, or a whip — and no one has dug a single schönbrun. Landau explains nothing, asking only a question translated in advance: “You believe you can?” Red answers yes. When the [[brigadir|Foreman]] asks for the Catalian to be punished, Landau refuses to support him and protects the Catalian.

## The oberfischmeister (ch. 23)
After the schönbrun collapses, Lieutenant [[klyajn|Klein]] argues with Landau. The baron suspects that Joriksen is hiding the Zustand’s “secret ingredient” and urgently sends him home. When Klein reminds him that he answers only to Schlissel and the Kaiser, the baron declares himself an oberfischmeister and demonstrates his power: Klein’s eyelids stick together and his arm turns rigid, while [[yakka|Yakka]] yawns and cannot close his mouth. He explains that a fischmeister can fish, while an oberfischmeister can take the catch with him. After an apology, the spell lifts. The baron forbids any report of Klein’s suspicions to the Kaiser and, to restore the schönbrun, agrees to feign gratitude toward Red. He examines the recovered Catalian and orders the well repaired at night, according to the Zustand. Two nights later, when Red reaches water again, the baron bends over the pit and whispers that this is a deception, not a schönbrun at all.

## Täuschung and the beast (ch. 27)
The baron is furious: Joriksen has deceived him, and complaining would mean admitting he was a fool, so he takes revenge on Red. Sitting alone by a gravestone with his pipe, he stops Red without touching him: the Catalian’s leg cramps, three shots go wide, and Red strikes himself on the head with the gun’s butt. The baron calls him a beast and taps his own forehead with his pipe.

## The beast’s Zustand (ch. 33)
Landau decides that a beast lies at the core of Red’s soul, while Joriksen’s songs and women were mere trappings. He assigns him an “unprecedented” Zustand: Red is chained in a pit, executes the condemned with a shovel, and must remove every last ounce of blood-soaked earth. The baron considers this a form of care: he allows the beast to be itself. Fear of the beast makes the camp obedient, and Landau reports “perfect order” to the magnus. But victims are scarce, and the baron seeks them out: he appoints [[greta|Greta]] as the “prize” less often, makes arrangements with authorities for a hundred versts around, and buys ten condemned men for fights on which bets are placed. The magnus hints that it is time to request a transfer, but Landau pretends not to understand and praises Joriksen.

Every spring, the baron conceals the beast from the army’s tribute collectors with the words “Catalian, show your chest” — “THOU SHALT NOT KILL” is branded there. When Greta ends up in the pit, he gives Red until noon and considers using the oberfischmeister’s power to force the beast to carry out the sentence. But the tribute collectors take Red away on General Brauschwitz’s orders, and Landau yields after mentioning the Kaiser’s Claws’ recommendation.

## Fate (ch. 29)
In ch. 29, Magnus [[amir-sedoj|Amir the Grey]] reports that many dead have been found in Dunkwald and considers the oberfischmeister dead. He wonders why Landau did not take revenge on Joriksen and refused a transfer himself: he seemed to have come to enjoy his service. The baron’s body has not yet been found.

## Relationships
- [[red|Red]] — his experimental subject and “beast”; the baron values him in his own way.
- [[joriksen|Joriksen]] — an assistant whom the baron considers weak; he later discovers that Joriksen has deceived him.
- [[klyajn|Klein]] — lieutenant of the Kaiser’s Claws; Landau despises and fears him.
- [[amir-sedoj|Amir the Grey]] — the magnus, Landau’s superior.
