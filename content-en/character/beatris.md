**Beatriz**, more often called **Golden Quill** in the novel, is an Academy of Arts student who attends [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva’s]] literature lectures. To him, she is initially an irritating star pupil in the front row, then a companion, driver, and investigative assistant. Unlike the heroes of the books Alvaro writes on commission for [[filippe|Don Filippe]], Beatriz has not yet completed a single book of her own. According to the author, she is a bright, straight-A student who knows the theory but has yet to write anything in practice.

## Character and appearance
Beatriz is red-haired, confident, and bold; Alvaro considers her presumptuous. She is methodical: every morning she records the previous day’s important events in her diary and carries a notebook and pencil. She reads widely, enjoys technical details, and understands them better than one might expect of a writer: in ch. 9, she presents General [[dezerej|Deserey]] with a theory that artillery strength increases with the square of the number of shots, and he agrees. Beatriz’s passion is speed. Her vehicle is a red-lacquered steam carriage, the [[esperantsa|Esperanza]], with a compact engine from [[brega|Brega]]; she wears goggles at the wheel and drives so fast that her passengers cling to their seats. She can also imitate Dangarian speech — she says she knows only a couple of phrases — knows the Dangarian word “[[kolodets|schönbrun]],” and speaks Merganan.

She states her aesthetic principles bluntly: a real book about war concerns an ordinary soldier separated from his beloved, whose friends die and who also dies himself. Alvaro is outraged: in his view, literature should uplift. Beatriz argues in lectures; she declares that “creative block” does not exist and that one must learn to overcome laziness.

## The student in the front row (ch. 3–5)
Beatriz first appears in ch. 3, still unnamed: a student with a gold fountain pen objects that rich people do not have to be villains, and Alvaro mocks her. She then asks him to read a page containing the idea for a novel, The Despised Heir: a son worships his heroic father, then discovers a bitter truth about him after his death. Alvaro replies that novels are not written with “ideas” and notes that she has decided in advance that the father will turn out to be a scoundrel. In ch. 5, she rewrites the plot to add a mystery and again requests an assessment; her mentor refuses, citing her overconfidence. After reading the newspaper, she reasons that murder is impossible on white land and suggests that [[khuanes|Doctor Juanes]] was not the best of physicians.

## The journey to Ortiyar (ch. 9–11)
In ch. 9, Beatriz arrives in the Esperanza to show Alvaro her first chapter and takes the opportunity to drive him, Fernan, and [[lyusil|Lucille]] to [[ortiyar|Ortiyar]] fortress to meet General Deserey. Alvaro reads her chapter in the back seat, beneath the chimney. At Ortiyar, she tries binoculars with rangefinding marks, describes her idea for a “real” book about war, and argues with Alvaro about what literature ought to do. Her remarks reveal that she lives with her father, the first-level judge Don Enhal, while her mother is a selfish woman who remembers her daughter when she wants to humiliate someone. On the return journey, a green steam carriage follows them, and in the middle of the chase Beatriz runs out of water: she forgot to fill the vehicle. The friends push the carriage to an inn.

In ch. 11, Beatriz devises a theory about a Dangarian spy pursuing the general’s diaries and offers to keep them at her house. To get rid of his persistent student, Alvaro writes a letter to her father asking him to stop his daughter from associating with him. She reads over his shoulder, becomes dejected, but admits he is right. On the way back to [[laerta|Laerta]], they encounter crowds of unemployed workers, and Beatriz turns the vehicle away. In ch. 13, during a lecture on a protagonist’s flaws, where she objects that perfect people do not exist, Alvaro asks whether she has given her father the letter. Beatriz has not yet done so, and he orders her not to approach him without the judge’s reply. At the same lecture, she again asks him to read her chapter and worries that the maestro looks unwell.

## Reconciliation (ch. 20)
After being wounded in the [[staryj-port|Old Port]], Alvaro storms out of the [[tri-byka|Three Bulls]], and Beatriz is the one who picks him up. She has come with a letter from her father, apologizing for his daughter, promising to call off the surveillance, and asking Alvaro not to enter black lands. She has also remembered the green Cherrato’s number, found its driver, and learned that the surveillance was not real: Alvaro lied to her about a spy to get rid of her. He defends himself by saying that there was a spy after all. They make peace, Alvaro accepts her as his pupil and assistant, and at the end of the chapter she asks, “So I am your only friend?”

## Practical lessons in investigation (ch. 22–26)
In ch. 22, Alvaro gives Beatriz a “practical lesson” on antagonists. She argues and, for example, corrects his use of “bastard”: “An antagonist is a role in the plot, not a moral judgment.” Together they develop their theories: a Dangarian spy and a dispossessed avenger. In the [[svitok|Great Scroll]], Beatriz undertakes to find records about Fatima and brings back the story of her daughter Unoko Ir-Reya, a rebel who dropped out of law school. See the section below for more on this.

In ch. 24, after a lecture, she invokes the conventions of the genre to persuade Alvaro not to tear up a letter from [[mor|Mor]] that [[osa|Wasp]] is trying to deliver: such letters usually contain the solution. She herself distrusts Wasp, naming him as the prime suspect for espionage in ch. 22. She goes with Alvaro to the late doctor’s house, where he introduces her to the butler [[emil|Emil]] as Judge Enhal’s daughter and assistant, and she draws information about the philosopher [[liess-van-khalm|Liess van Halm]] out of the butler. Explaining why the doctor needed to hide the philosopher, she says that van Halm claimed to have seen all five heroes of Solanta in a well. She refuses to explain how she knows this.

In ch. 26, at the [[klinika-miloserdiya|Mercy Clinic]], she pays the orderly Miguel twenty escudos for a visit to van Halm, calling Alvaro her mentor rather than “her man.” While Alvaro writes words for the philosopher, she draws a well and acts out a vision; she already knows the word “schönbrun.”

## Unoko (ch. 22–28)
“Unoko Ir-Reya” is the fictional daughter of Fatima whom Beatriz describes to Alvaro after working in the Scroll’s reading room: the girl drinks, steals, causes scandals, and eventually leaves law school after a quarrel with her mother. Alvaro only discovers the true origin of this story in ch. 28 (see below). In later chapters, he jokingly calls Beatriz herself by the same name.

## Fatima’s daughter (ch. 28)
Leaving [[fatima|Fatima Ir-Reya’s]] house, Alvaro is hit by a vehicle bearing the letters ESP: Beatriz is at the wheel. He notices a quarter of foreign ancestry in her features and the dark roots beneath her dyed red hair — its natural color is black with an ashen tint — and realizes that she is Fatima’s daughter, while her invented “Unoko” is her own mirror opposite (an “anti-anagram”: the names have no letters in common). Beatriz confesses: her mother really did divorce her father, Judge Enhal, after Solanta; Beatriz herself went to live with her father and joined the esfera of Art. She hid the relationship because she was constantly compared with her mother, and she loves her father. She says she did not lie about the important things: Fatima has no son, and her dream of becoming a writer is real. Van Halm wrote to her mother saying he had seen the five heroes in the well, which is how she knew this in ch. 24. Alvaro calls her a lying coward, and when she reproaches him for his lie about the spy, he answers that she has much in common with her mother and leaves her in tears.

## Relations with Alvaro after the quarrel (ch. 30–34)
In ch. 30, Mor names Beatriz as Fatima’s daughter in her letter to Alvaro. The novel does not explicitly describe Alvaro’s reconciliation with his pupil, but by ch. 34 they are behaving as they did before.

## Last Winter (ch. 34)
On the day of the assault on [[posledniaya-zima|Last Winter]], Beatriz comes to [[tres-molinos|Tres Molinos]] on business of her own — Fatima has forbidden her to go near van Halm’s estate — and falls into the hands of Deserey’s agents, who mistake her for a spy. Alvaro jokingly introduces her as Unoko, a drunkard and libertine. Then the killers attack the castle: [[salvador|Salvador]] takes Beatriz hostage, strikes her, bloodying her nose, and uses her to pressure Alvaro. In the cellar beside the dark [[temnyj-shyonbrun|schönbrun]], she goes ahead with [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]], pretending to be weak; while Fernan extinguishes the lantern, Salvador tilts his head back to examine the mirrors and is struck with a burning lantern. After the fight, Fatima enters the cellar; in conversation with her mother, Beatriz grumbles that she does not need protection. Describing her own role in the adventure, she jokes that she added variety to the male company. Fatima does not join in and reminds her that the general’s agents were watching Beatriz rather than their enemies. In response to her mother’s warning about the laws of the well of misfortune, Beatriz says that, in her case, avoiding becoming too dear will be easy.

## Conception and interviews
According to the author in the first of the two streams, Beatriz is a “bright, straight-A student.” He compares her with Hermione Granger, says that she has not yet written a single book, and that later World of Wells novels may reveal what she achieves in practice. According to the author, the history of how the systems of Catalia and Dangar developed has been left for future books.

## Relationships (ch. 34)
- [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] — her mentor; in ch. 20, she calls herself his only friend.
- [[sudya-enkhal|Judge Enhal]] — her father, whom she loves and to whom Alvaro writes.
- [[fatima|Fatima Ir-Reya]] — her mother; their relationship is strained.
- [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]] — Alvaro’s friend, who also becomes her ally at Last Winter.

## At the end of the novel (ch. 34)
Beatriz is alive, has survived captivity, and travels to Laerta with Fernan in her Esperanza, which she calls the vehicle best protected against curses. Her literary career has not begun within the novel.
