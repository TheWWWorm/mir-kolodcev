Greta is a slave from the women's hamlet attached to the [[dunkvald|Dunkwald]] camp, where [[red|Red]] digs a well. She first appears as a “prize” for completing a well, then becomes his lover and the only person to visit him regularly in the pit. Her fate is one of the book's most harrowing storylines.

## Character and Appearance
Greta is rosy-cheeked and healthy, with a black braid and a high bosom; Red first sees her in an embroidered shift. She does not know Catalian and is initially frightened of her visitor. It later emerges that she is deeply devout: she believes in the Sower as Red has always believed in himself, and considers everything in life, including slavery, to be God's will. She loves fine clothes and ribbons, weeps with joy on visiting town for the first time in years, and fears breaking the law to the very end.

Her trade in the camp is to be a “prize” for slaves rewarded for their work. The other stones hate her because she has “bought her way out with other work” and does not dig the pit. Red sees a blue swallow on her door: every woman in the hamlet has a different picture on hers.

## The “Prize” (ch. 12)
When the well reaches water, [[yakka|Yakka]] takes Red to a hamlet of fifteen huts and presents Greta to him as a “prize.” She feeds him pie, serves him tea and sweets, takes him to the bathhouse, washes him, and examines his scars. Red reassures her with words she does not understand and initially embraces her like a sister. He then tells her his entire story: how he taught himself from books, earned a silver bracelet in the Chamber of the Word, lost his fame, was chosen as a sacrifice, and went from a prison cell into slavery. Greta asks with gestures whether he loves another woman; Red takes off her shift. In the morning, he discovers that the “prize” is a night rather than freedom, and he strikes Yakka and runs away.

## The Letter and the Swallow (ch. 15)
After Red returns, Greta becomes his messenger: he names her when demanding that a letter be sent to [[laerta|Laerta]], and she travels to town with two soldiers. For her, this is a great occasion. She returns with a receipt and purchases—ribbons, a bird-shaped hairpin, garters, and fur mittens—and speaks enthusiastically about the town. On his day off, Red brings her fish and asks for no services, wanting only to talk; he recounts the legend of [[lid-anteodor|Lid Anteodor]]. The story is interrupted by the foreman, who has come as a customer. Red kisses Greta in front of him and leaves.

## The Pit and Joriksen's Departure (ch. 19–23)
In ch. 19, Red sees that Greta is not among the hundreds of slaves digging unsuccessful wells and promises to buy her freedom. She catches his eye in the temple. After the schönbrun collapses, Greta is ordered to nurse Red, but genuinely falls in love, and they become lovers. She teaches him Dangarian and uses drawings to explain that it was really the Sower who chose him and directed the ray. Red dreams of returning to Catalia, taking his son, joining the esfera of Medicine, and digging a healing schönbrun; Greta objects: she does not want to live in Catalia, and Red must not betray the Sower. The only thing she will agree to is a visit followed by a return. On the eve of his recovery, they go to the empty temple: Greta is dressed like a whore and Red like a pirate. There, Greta prays and kisses the saint's feet while Red mocks. In the kitchen, Greta tells him that the Sower controls everything.

Later, in ch. 27, Lieutenant [[klyajn|Wolf Klein]] tells Red that [[joriksen|Joriksen]] used Greta in his scheme: he instructed her to tell the foreman that Red was a madman and that she merely pitied him. The novel does not specify whether Greta knew she was taking part in a deception.

## The Beast's Pit (ch. 33)
When Red is chained in the pit and appointed executioner, Greta visits him every other day: she brings food, water, and soap, teaches him Dangarian words, tells him about God, and comforts him. The narrator explains that she is flattered to be the only caring soul beside the beast, that this confirms her faith in the principle of “Sorge um dich,” and that in this way Red will never go away or belong to anyone else. Baron [[landau|Landau]] assigns her as a “prize” less often so that she has time to care for the beast.

In the fifth year, she brings a temple priest to the pit, then disappears for a month. She returns with [[pisarenok|the young scribe]], a future fischmeister who intends to buy her. Red tells her sharply, “Well done. You've landed on your feet.” Three months later, pregnant, she appears at another execution. The following spring, she herself is sentenced and lowered into the pit: she says she has sinned. Red refuses to kill her and proposes that they escape, but Greta admits she did not send his letter to Laerta: she mailed an empty envelope and threw the letter away so that he would love only her. The scene breaks off at her words, “Do what God commands.” In the morning, the tribute collectors find her body in the pit, her throat hacked open with a shovel. The novel does not show Red at the moment of the killing.

## Relationships
- [[red|Red]]—her lover. Red loves her more than the woman in Catalia, but less than his son.
- [[yakka|Yakka]]—brings her to Red as a “prize.”
- [[pisarenok|Young Scribe]]—the man who promises to buy her.
- [[landau|Landau]]—the master who controls her life in the camp.
