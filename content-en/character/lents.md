Prince Wladyslaw Lenz is an Ollandian nobleman whose lands lie along the railway between [[plyutva|Plyutva]] and [[latsen|Latsen]]. He surrendered the voivodeship to the Dangarians without a fight to preserve his people's freedom; now he lives in the conquerors' capital and hopes to become the Kaiser's governor in Ollandia. To Dietrich von Dort, the prince is the owner of land concealing a [[kolodets|schönbrun]] and the stern guardian of the girl with whom the lieutenant falls in love.

## Character and Appearance
Lenz is tall and gray-haired, his face deeply lined, with a long mustache curving around his mouth and hanging below his chin. He speaks in a low voice with a barely noticeable accent. Beside the baroness's colorful guests, he looks “like an oak among chestnuts.” To Dietrich, he seems “a noble, guileless old soldier.” But according to Olkha, appearances are deceptive: the prince is far-sighted and knows how to think ahead.

He is conservative, proud, and quick-tempered. Recalling the customs of his youth, he says he fought from the age of fourteen, that his “ladies” were a musket and sword, and that he knew a woman only at twenty-five, after his wedding. The honor of his family is no empty phrase to him: he is prepared to wash away an insult in a duel. There was no slavery in Ollandia before the Dangarians arrived, and he rejects the thought of his family's blood mingling with that of people descended from stones.

## His Past and His Calculations (ch. 17)
Olkha tells Dietrich her uncle's history. Years before the war with [[dangar|Dangar]], Lenz realized that war was inevitable and Ollandia would lose. The Kaiser's vast empire is under a single ruler, while the Ollandian princes quarrel among themselves, maintain separate retinues, and fail to act together. Lenz tried to persuade the nobles to combine their forces and establish a unified command, but they would not listen. He then began preparing for defeat: he cultivated connections in Hofstadt with noble eagles, including [[varkhajt|Count von Wahrheit]] and Baroness [[elza|Elsa]].

He was advised to cement these friendships through a dynastic marriage. His niece was the only suitable girl in the family, so he sent her to Saint Adelia's boarding school in Hofstadt for five years. But Olkha returned with honors and no connections, while her friend Katrina, whom he had considered a poor match, married Deputy Minister [[lurd|Ludwig von Lurd]]. Afterward, the prince did not speak to his niece for a week. He cannot stand Katrina: she reminds him of the failure of his plan.

## Fishing and Meeting Dietrich (ch. 14)
Lenz first appears in the novel at [[rybalka-valbrekht|the fishing party at Baroness Walbrecht's]]. Elsa introduces him to her guests as marshal of the nobility of the Latsen Voivodeship. His presence in the enemy capital prompts a jab from a dragoon cornet; Dietrich eases the tension with a toast, and Lenz drinks along with the other guests. In the smoking room, a millionaire merchant assures the prince that the war will soon end and offers to buy land for the railway; the prince replies that he has come to arrange his niece's future. He asks Elsa to take Olkha under her protection and accepts an invitation for five o'clock the next day.

## Guardian and Politician (ch. 17–21)
On Saturday, the prince moves into the baroness's house with his luggage and a cage containing a cat. Before leaving, he asks Dietrich, whom he believes to be Katrina's cousin, not to indulge Olkha's whims and to bring her straight back to the baroness. Later, Dietrich sees in the Fishermen’s Guild atlas that the land beside the station at the hundredth verst belongs to Lenz. The prince thus becomes a link in his plan: obtain the governorship for Lenz through the schönbrun, and receive the land in return. The novel does not say whether the prince knows about the schönbrun on his land.

## The Duel That Never Happened (ch. 25–29)
Invited by Count Lurd, the prince discusses with him how to become governor. Lurd explains that the governor will be an Ollandian who has formed family ties with either the clergy or the military aristocracy; the society lions the baroness is finding are unsuitable. Lenz refuses to marry Olkha to a “former slave”—a priest from among the stones—or to a Dangarian officer.

The prince then finds Olkha in a dressing gown and Dietrich in a torn shirt, and demands pistols. Lurd promises to tell his wife that the prince has shot her beloved brother and reminds him of the influential Eyrings. In Chapter 29, the count develops a whole plan: marriage to an “Eyring” would connect the prince with an ancient Dangarian family, and the Kaiser, he says, likes modest artillerymen. To vehement objections, Lurd responds with a threat to label Lenz “unsicher,” unreliable: this is the empire's term for nobles who harmed the conquerors during the settlement of a conquered country; his estates would then be confiscated and some of the inhabitants enslaved. The prince reminds him that he surrendered the voivodeship without a fight, but yields. He tells Dietrich to ask for Olkha's hand in writing and agrees, when asked in return for a hundred desyatinas of land, to draw up a document.

## Afterward (ch. 31)
When the lieutenant is arrested by gendarmes, Olkha returns to Latsen without her uncle. A gendarme informs Major [[klyajn|Klein]] that Dort's fiancée is the niece of Prince Wladyslaw, an influential man. Lenz himself has gone to Hofstadt. His subsequent fate is not shown in the novel.

## Relationships
- [[olkha|Olkha]]—his niece and ward. He orders her about, but worries about her future.
- [[lurd|Ludwig von Lurd]]—an adviser to the Kaiser who plays a political game with the prince.
- [[elza|Elsa von Walbrecht]]—the hostess with whom he stays in the capital.
- [[ditrikh|Dietrich von Dort]]—the “Eyring,” later his niece's unwanted fiancé.
