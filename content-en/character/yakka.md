Yakka is a hunter serving Fischmeister [[joriksen|Fritjof Joriksen]]. He is the first person [[red|Red]] meets in Dangar and one of the few to remain beside him throughout his forced labor: he guards the pit, carries water, takes him to his “prize,” and once brings the fugitive back alive from the winter forest. He is neither slave nor officer, speaks only Dangarian, and develops a strange friendship with Red, ended by killings rather than a quarrel.

## Character and Appearance
Red first sees Yakka as an escort with narrow eyes and a flat, round face, wearing a fox-fur cap and a reindeer-hide coat. He smokes a long-stemmed pipe, carries a silver-trimmed double-barreled flintlock gun, and softly draws out the last sound of Red's name: “Reda.” This form—“Reda, grabt” (“Red, dig”)—becomes his habitual refrain.

Yakka is unflappable and seldom raises his voice. He is an accurate shot—in ch. 8, he shoots a cone off a stump from about twenty paces—but does not fire without need or orders; he laughs at threats and calmly turns his back on Red. He enjoys chatting in his own language and carving wood. He believes in ancestral spirits and in the “soul” of his gun, named Eikha: a spell upon it prevents its bullet from killing its owner. Before going out, he chews “chamka” leaves so that his breath has no smell.

## First Encounters (ch. 4–8)
In ch. 4, Yakka leads the new slave to a clearing marked with a flag and commands “grabe”—dig. When Red thinks he is digging his own grave and asks him to send a letter, Yakka merely laughs. It later becomes clear that he was following custom, not an order to kill. Yakka is not quite an overseer: he does not obey the foremen, only the master fisherman.

In ch. 8, Yakka guards the clearing, and Red first sees him carving a figurine of a girl. He answers questions readily, but only in Dangarian and without gestures. He repeats the main thing Red must learn: the Catalian digs a well—the Catalian is free. During the church service, he tries to feed Red porridge by hand, and Red, disgusted by the ritual, drives him away.

## Guard and “Prize” (ch. 12)
As the well grows deeper, Red persuades Yakka to help from above by hauling up earth and holding the rope. Yakka agrees, although he often goes off on his own business; afterward they smoke a pipe together beside the finished well. When water is found, Yakka is the one who tests it with a bucket and nods.

That evening, he takes Red not to the residence but to the women's hamlet and introduces him to the slave [[greta|Greta]]: “your prize.” The next morning, on their way back, he says the word “Zustand,” and Red realizes this is not freedom. At a fork in the path, Red strikes Yakka in the stomach and temples, takes his gun—the one with Eikha's soul—gunpowder, bullets, and fire striker, and leaves for the forest. He could kill the hunter, but does not.

## Pursuit and Gift (ch. 15)
Usually no one is sent after runaway stones: in summer they reach people, in winter they die. But Joriksen asks for the Catalian to be brought back, and Yakka goes alone. The chapter shows him as a master of his craft: from broken bushes and the deep tracks of a tall man, he reads every decision Red made. He does not fear being shot—Eikha will not kill its owner—and his first concern is whether the fugitive has taken the gun's “soul.” Finding Red unconscious near a hill, he lights a fire, gives him schnapps and food, and scolds him for escaping. He will take no revenge, but warns that he will not go after him a second time. He asks the “two-faced” mirna on Red's arm to translate his words.

After this, Yakka becomes almost an ally. He takes Red to the clearing at night, where he digs beside a fire and sings, and records the songs and coughing fits with marks in the snow. Later, Yakka gives Red a present for the first time—a carved figurine of a Catalian girl, called “Yulma” in ch. 23.

## Surveillance and Collapse (ch. 19–23)
In ch. 19, Yakka is the first to notice someone watching the clearing and explains from the tracks that the person came during the day as well. Later, he tells Red that snowfall muffles sound, which is why the spy has stopped coming.

Yakka is not nearby when the foreman fills in the pit. In ch. 23, he sits with his gun beside the sick man's bed, and Baron [[landau|Landau]] demonstrates his power in response to Yakka's presumption: after yawning, Yakka cannot close his mouth. Afterward, he brings Red his belongings, drinks schnapps with him, and, together with Lieutenant [[klyajn|Wolf]], asks on the baron's behalf that he restore the well. Here he also says Red dug better after he received Greta.

## Red's Escape (ch. 27)
After the well proves ordinary and Red, following his escape and the slaughter, drives the supply convoy along the road, Yakka calls to him from the forest, asks him to return, and fires between his legs, then shoots through the reins. Red reminds him that he once spared his friend and drives on. Yakka asks him not to harm the young scribe and does not follow.

## The End of the Friendship (ch. 33)
Yakka visits the pit twice. The first time is immediately after the foreman's execution. He says that killing people is wrong, that he has repaid his debt, and that nothing binds him to Red any longer. The second time, three years later, he comes to say goodbye: he is leaving Dunkwald as a free man, leaves Red a pipe, fire striker, and tobacco pouch, and explains that the stones endure their Zustands out of fear of the beast in the pit. He promises to consult Eikha about the future. The novel does not say where he goes.

## Relationships
- [[red|Red]]—his charge, a fugitive, a friend, and later a stranger. Yakka never once shoots him.
- [[joriksen|Joriksen]]—his master and superior; according to the author, Yakka is his right hand, but “not quite an overseer.”
- [[greta|Greta]]—Yakka himself brings her to Red as a “prize”; later, he brings Red his belongings together with her.
- [[landau|Landau]]—Yakka fears and dislikes him.

## Conception and Interviews
According to the author, Yakka was conceived as a northerner, hunter, and tracker, a representative of another people living alongside the Dangarians. At first, it was simply an idea for showing a different type of person, but the author then “grew fond of” the character and developed him. He calls Yakka “not quite an overseer” in Master Joriksen's service.
