Isabel is [[karlos|Carlos el Niña's]] wife. When first seen at the fort in the [[staryj-port|Old Port]], she seems a fragile, sickly woman whom her husband protects like a little bird. By the middle of the novel, she disappears; it later emerges that nothing was as her husband or the reader believed.

## Appearance and Character
Isabel is thin and nervous, with a long neck bearing a large mole and eyes of unequal size: the left is larger than the right. She speaks softly, her phrases trailing off into ellipses. The novel does not name her illness: for many years she was tormented by an affliction that made her “wither like a flower without water.” She reads [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva's]] novels. Her laugh rings out clearly; according to Carlos, she once had the loveliest laugh of all the girls in Laerta.

## At the Fort (ch. 7)
Carlos tells his guests how they met on the day of the running of the bulls along Del Toro Boulevard: Isabel stood on the terrace of the [[galereya-eliste|Eliste Gallery]], and he, hoping to win her heart, ran with the bulls and escaped them at the cost of his trousers. Everyone laughed except her. She was the one who guessed that the buried well in the fort's cellar was special and insisted it be dug out. Seeing a wilting chrysanthemum entangled in a vine in the [[kolodets|Well]], she barely holds back her tears and leaves.

## Disappearance (ch. 18–22)
Two weeks before these events, her illness receded: Isabel went to the opera, the hairdresser, and jewelers' shops. Four days earlier, she visited the [[filosofiya-uzhasa|Philosophy of Horror]] exhibition with her husband. As the couple walked around the gallery, they heard a bottle thrown from an archway land at their feet; Carlos chased the prankster, and his wife vanished. The last trace was a carriage turning the corner. Alvaro is certain she is dead; in ch. 22, he speaks of the black carriage in which she was taken away.

## Isabel Alive (ch. 32–34)
In [[tres-molinos|Tres Molinos]], a ringing laugh draws Alvaro's attention to a strolling couple, and he recognizes her. Isabel's companion is a mustachioed man with a sword. She tells him that over the past year Carlos became a tyrant: he would not let her go out alone, allow letters or meetings, or even let her pour her own tea. She compares herself to an object locked in a chest and says she ran away. The well she saw in the fort showed a vine choking a flower.

Alvaro promises to keep silent if she tells him the truth about the death of [[red|Rodrigo da Vira]]. Later, in ch. 34, he recounts what he learned: Carlos once told his wife, while drunk and pleased with himself, how he had handed da Vira over to the Doge of Solanta. According to Alvaro's theory, losing Isabel's love was the result of the “misfortune” from the [[temnyj-shyonbrun|dark schönbrun]]: Carlos loved too intensely, and his wife left him.

## Fate (ch. 34)
Isabel remains with her lover in Tres Molinos. The novel does not show whether Carlos learns she is alive.
