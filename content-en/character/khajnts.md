Heinz von Dort is the little son of [[ditrikh|Dietrich]] and [[mari|Mari]]. Dietrich went to war when the boy was two months old, so on his return he barely knows his child: in ch. 10, the mother shows him how to hold his son properly. Heinz matters to the plot as the thing that makes Dietrich’s debt to the fischmeister impossible to repay with money.

## In the family (ch. 10–14)

The reader first sees Heinz while Mari bathes him and scolds the stone maid. In the household, he is the chief argument in every dispute about money: Mari fears he will catch a cold in winter without firewood. Their landlady, Frau [[gizeke|Gieseke]], is convinced that his father will soon die and calls the boy nothing but “the little orphan.” Mari tells her husband that Heinz took his first steps on his hundred-and-first attempt. Dietrich plays with his son mainly to please his wife.

## Collateral (ch. 21–25)

When Dietrich visits the fischmeister [[joriksen|Joriksen]], the latter offers services worth ten thousand marks in exchange for the right to fish in Dietrich’s schönbrun. As a guarantee, he slides over a pledge with the collateral already entered: “Heinz von Dort, son of Dietrich von Dort.” Under its terms, nonpayment would assign the child to the estate of stones. Joriksen knows the boy’s name in advance and explains that he fears disclosure: Dietrich is under investigation by the [[kogti-kajzera|Kaiser’s Claws]] and might reveal the schönbrun out of fear. He says the son will become a slave in only one case: if Dietrich deceives the master.

Dietrich furiously refuses and leaves, but on returning he negotiates a salary and other conditions with Joriksen; the pledge remains in force. By ch. 25, Dietrich is already thinking of “the signature beneath the monstrous pledge,” which is why he moves into a hotel, unwilling to look his wife in the eye. Mari knows nothing of the collateral.

## At the end of the novel (ch. 29)

Joriksen retains the pledge. Later, in ch. 31, Major [[klyajn|Klein]] observes that this is precisely what keeps Dietrich on the hook; the novel does not say how the matter will end. Heinz remains in Hofstadt with his mother, and Mari is ready to join her husband at his first summons.
