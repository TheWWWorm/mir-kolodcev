**Red** is a Catalian whom the reader first meets in Chapter Four: bound, without boots, and thrown from a cart into a forest slave camp on the edge of Dangar. The author explicitly calls him the novel's third main protagonist, alongside the writer [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]] and the Dangarian officer [[ditrikh|Dietrich von Dort]]. His storyline tells of a slave forced to dig a well and shows Dangar at its darkest: according to the author, Red's position in Dangar is comparable to that of a concentration-camp prisoner.

## Character and Appearance
Red is tall—taller than all his dugout companions—broad-shouldered, and dark-haired. He is quick-tempered, cannot stand baseness, stupidity, or worthlessness, and fights better than anyone he has encountered: he handles a shovel so skillfully that he can scatter four men in a minute. He says he believes in three things—intelligence, his fists, and the mirna [[eliga|Eliga]]—“in precisely that order”; he also invokes the [[drevnie|Ancients]] and the [[pervyj-sudya|First Judge]]. Eliga, the mirna of luck, is also depicted on his forearm: a beauty sitting on a cannon, with two faces, one woman's and one snake's.

At first, Red knows no Dangarian, although he speaks Muari and Solantan, neither of which helps here. His name is too difficult for the Dangarians, so they call him “Catallian”—“the Catalian.” He loves songs and can tell stories and legends: he heard them from his father, uncle, and boatswain, while his mother taught him to cook.

## What Is Known of His Past (ch. 4–33)
Red's past is recounted in fragments scattered throughout the chapters, and much remains unsaid.

- **Trade.** Red came from the [[teni|esfera of Shadow]], whose trades include thieves, robbers, boarding fighters, and prostitutes. He tells [[greta|Greta]] that until recently he wore a bracelet of the same rank as hers. He calls himself a “bandit” (ch. 15); he is famous enough for his portrait to have appeared on the front page of the [[vestnik|Laerta Herald]] (ch. 27). His familiar weapon was a Catalian Mardur revolver.
- **Silver bracelet.** Red's two dreams were to have a son and change his trade. He studied from books for four years, barely able to read: he struggled over formulas and once nearly shot himself in despair. He then went to the [[palata-slova|Chamber of the Word]], passed the examination with the best result of that year, and received a silver [[braslet|bracelet]] with stars. That was the day he “believed in intelligence.” But he wore the bracelet for only six months.
- **Betrayal and sentence.** According to Red's own account to Greta (ch. 12), he became involved in “a mad venture to pluck a star from the sky,” but another party—a “vile bitch”—stole his glory, supported by a man he had considered a friend. At the same time, he learned he was terminally ill. When a sacrifice was needed, they chose him, and he did not object because he believed he would soon die anyway. A talkative fellow named [[nivendej|Nivendey]] shared his cell. Instead of the gallows, Red was sentenced to slavery. The novel does not say what the venture was, who these people were, or why they chose him specifically.
- **Illness.** A Catalian doctor diagnosed “sarcoma of the lungs” and gave him a year to live. Nine months have passed by the beginning of the novel. Red suffers from a dry cough.
- **The road to slavery.** At first, he was held in a dungeon on an island in the middle of the ocean; later, he was bought for a substantial sum “in another country”: before the auction, the slave trader gave him bonk to quiet his cough and drive up the price. Red traveled by ship and spent a month in a railway car; he attempted to escape, which is why he was unloaded from the cart bound and bootless.
- **His son, the letter, and Kara.** Red has a son he has never seen. The boy's mother is [[karmensita-estevos|Kara]], an obrero of Shadow, a kind woman whom he does not love (“I would have been a fool to fall in love with her, and she a fool to fall in love with me”). Against his chest, Red carries an envelope containing a letter from Laerta, and answering it is the one thing he wants to accomplish before he dies. Red thinks of “her” and dreams of returning, leaving money, and saying what remains unsaid; the novel does not explicitly identify the letter's author. Greta later calls its recipient “his woman” in Laerta; according to Salvador in ch. 34, da Vira wrote to Carmensita from Dangar.

## The Mushroom Patch, Joriksen, and the “Schönbrun” (ch. 4)
In late autumn, Red is brought to the forest “mushroom patch”—a camp of dugouts surrounding the master fisherman's residence. He secretly wears through the ropes binding him, fights off the dugout foreman and four subordinates who wanted to take his boots, and puts a shovel to the foreman's throat; the conflict then subsides. In the morning, the crowd of slaves—“Stein” is Dangarian for “stone”—is lined up before the residence, and the scribe announces each one's “Zustand,” a working condition. Red is assigned “kein Zustand,” and the crowd's murmur tells him things are bad. The hunter [[yakka|Yakka]] takes him to a clearing, and as Red digs he decides he is digging his own grave.

Waiting in the residence is [[joriksen|Fritjof Joriksen]], a master fisherman, or [[fishmajster|fischmeister]], who has learned a few Catalian words from a dictionary. He calls Red “my stone” and “luck,” and explains that he must dig a [[kolodets|schönbrun]], after which he will receive freedom, money, and a ticket home. Red does not understand that the word means more than an ordinary well. He bargains: he will dig, but first they must send his reply to the letter from Laerta. Joriksen tests his luck with a coin, sees the tattoo of Eliga, and decides he has found the right man.

## Taking Control of the Dugout and the Ritual (ch. 8)
Red digs through frozen ground in winter, cold and hungry: the foreman takes half the food, while his henchmen—[[shults|Schultz]], [[siplyj|Hoarse]], [[boroda-lopatoj|Spadebeard]], and [[tot-bez-nogtej|The One Without Nails]]—are crippled by their labor and never think of escape. Yakka guards Red and carves wood. When the pit reaches one and a half yards, Red attends a service in the [[seyatel|Sower's]] temple for the first time and sees the “Sorge um dich” ritual, in which free people feed slaves porridge by hand. He refuses to open his mouth, and [[klyajn|the man with the falcon badge]] unexpectedly smiles. The foreman and his henchmen beat Red in the forest and knock out two teeth, but the next morning Red scalds the foreman with boiling water, deals with the others, and institutes an equal division of food in the dugout.

## The Well, the Prize, and the Escape (ch. 12)
For fighting the foreman, who is considered property of the [[gildiya-rybakov|Fishermen’s Guild]], Red is laid on the altar and given twenty lashes without salt. The foreman learns that Red is dying and begins to fear him. Red persuades Yakka to help from above, and at the thirteenth yard water appears. He dreams of freedom and of getting home in time, glad that he bears no slave brand.

But instead of freedom, Yakka takes him to a hamlet of fifteen huts and introduces him to a girl, [[greta|Greta]]: “your Preis”—prize. Red spends the night with her; she feeds him, washes him, and listens to his story. In the morning, he realizes the prize is a handout rather than freedom—when Yakka says “Zustand” on the way, Red understands everything—strikes Yakka in the stomach and temples, takes his double-barreled gun, and heads into the forest. He does not kill Yakka.

## Return and Recipe (ch. 15)
Runaways are not usually pursued in winter, but Joriksen asks Yakka to bring Red back. Yakka follows his tracks and finds him unconscious on a hilltop, having spent the night fighting wolves. Yakka takes no revenge, but warns that he will not come a second time. Red regains consciousness in the residence: [[landau|Baron Landau]], the new senior master fisherman, calls him a runaway slave, listens to his chest through a listening tube, and confirms sarcoma. Joriksen explains that Red dug an ordinary “brunnen,” not a “schönbrun” (“beautiful well”), and Red is astonished to learn that in Dangar people believe a mortal can create a [[kolodets|Well]]. Asked by Joriksen “who are you?”, he first says “a bandit” and “a dead man,” then answers “I am a Catalian”—which is the right answer.

Joriksen sends the letter, with Greta as messenger; Red receives a separate dugout, the status of foreman, three days' rations, and the right to dig at night beside a fire, with songs. He sings in Catalian and tells of mirnas, lords of the abyss, and [[lid-anteodor|Lid Anteodor]]; Yakka gives him his first present, a figurine of a Catalian girl, and Greta becomes his regular companion. Red guesses Joriksen's intention: warmth, songs, night (Shadow), solitude, skin untouched by a brand, and a beautiful woman make up the planned “recipe” for a schönbrun, tailored to Red's nature as a Catalian.

## Zustand, Camp, and Water (ch. 19)
Red is surrounded by suspicious attention: someone walks through the forest at night; the letter disappears from his dugout, then returns, tucked into a skull's eye socket. Red concludes that someone wants to discover the schönbrun's recipe, meaning at least two people believe in success. Two slave soldiers, Theo and “Toad Eye,” are posted at the dugout. Joriksen comes every morning with a ledger recording the depth, the number of songs, and the coughing fits. Landau shows Red the camp, where hundreds of slaves dig wells under the most bizarre Zustands and none has produced a schönbrun; using a prepared phrase, he asks whether Red believes, and Red answers “yes.”

At the full moon, Red reaches water and shouts the news to Yakka—then suddenly notices that his cough is gone. He promises to buy Greta's freedom, but it is the foreman who appears above, strikes him with a shovel, and begins filling the pit, burying him alive.

## Cured (ch. 23)
Lieutenant Klein finds Red with four broken ribs and a pneumothorax in Greta's house, the one with the blue swallow on the door. Landau, who is also a physician, establishes that the sarcoma is gone, so the schönbrun must have cured him—but the well has collapsed. The baron, now an oberfischmeister with the power to inflict harm, questions the officers about the “secret ingredient”; with the baron's permission, Joriksen has already left.

Red recovers at Greta's home. She nurses him first under orders and then out of love, teaches him Dangarian, and explains that the Sower chose him. Red, however, does not believe in the Sower—“he is not a god, only a man”—and wants to return to Catalia, take his son from Kara, enter the esfera of Medicine, and open a healing well there; he admits that he loves only his son. He digs again according to the Zustand and reaches water; Landau bends over the pit and whispers that it is a deception, no schönbrun at all.

## Täuschung and Slaughter (ch. 27)
At night, Klein—the Falcon whom Red knew as Wolf—visits the arrested Red and speaks to him in Catalian so the sentries cannot understand. He explains: “die Täuschung” means “deception”; a true schönbrun shows mist and monsters at the bottom, while Red's contains only water. Wolf tells the story: Dunkwald is where the Fishermen’s Guild has been sending diggers for half a century, and both the young Joriksen and Wolf himself, only a second-generation eagle, were exiled there. Joriksen devised the idea of taking a foreigner who did not know that a schönbrun could not be dug and inspiring him. Red proved suitable. Later, Joriksen realized there had never been sarcoma: the Laertan doctor had mistaken bronchitis for it; the damp earth aggravated it, while the warm dugout and fire cured it. While the baron searched for a miracle in this, Joriksen secured a transfer, leaving Red alone with Landau.

Wolf offers to grant one wish. Red asks for a pistol and is refused (“a gift”); he asks for death and immediately withdraws the request, rejects buying Greta's freedom because it would mean never seeing his son, and asks for something made of iron. Wolf gives him a knife and shows him the newspaper bearing his portrait: he knows who Red is and has told no one. Red, now hating the Sower—“gave me faith and trampled it”—cuts the ropes around his wrists, kills his escorts on the path by the raspberry bushes, and takes their weapons. Instead of escaping along the rails, he ambushes a supply convoy on the only road, kills several soldiers, wounds others, and uses homemade grenades made from flasks. Yakka calls on him to return, shoots wide, and begs him to spare the young scribe; Red releases the boy, ordering him to “carry the true faith.” But beside the gravestone, a solitary Landau waits for Red; without taking a fighting stance, he uses some unknown force to make Red's leg cramp, throw off his aim, and cause him to strike himself in the head with his gunstock.

## The Beast in the Pit (ch. 33)
Landau chains Red to a post in a pit near Dunkwald. The Zustand the baron chooses “according to Red's nature” is cruel: Red must execute the condemned and shovel out all the earth soaked in their blood. The first to die is that same foreman, followed by the others. Red comes alive only during executions and lapses into apathy the rest of the time. He is called “the beast,” “di bestia”; as punishment, the words “THOU SHALT NOT KILL” are burned into his chest.

Years pass in this condition. Greta comes every other day, brings food, and teaches him Dangarian; Wolf, who has been attending the executions, says goodbye near the end of the second year and leaves for [[khofshtadt|Hofstadt]], refusing Red a pistol. Yakka comes twice: first he says that murderers are no friends of his; then, three years later, he says goodbye and leaves a pipe, fire striker, and tobacco pouch. By the fifth year, Red has learned Dangarian, and the pit has reached four yards. For ten months, landowners bet on his fights with the condemned; then their interest fades. Greta leaves with the now-grown young scribe, who intends to become a fischmeister.

In spring, Greta herself, now pregnant and accused once more, is brought in for execution. Red refuses to kill her and proposes escape. Greta then admits that she did not send his letter: she mailed an empty envelope and threw the real letter away so that he would love only her. In the morning, army tribute collectors arrive: Greta lies at the bottom of the pit with her throat hacked open, while Red stands at the post. The baron shows the officer the burned inscription and lists the eleven soldiers Red killed, but the order to take him comes from General Brauschwitz on the recommendation of the [[kogti-kajzera|Kaiser’s Claws]]. Naming Klein through clenched teeth, Landau is forced to obey: Red is unchained and prepared for departure.

## Mentions in Other Storylines (ch. 29, 31)
In Chapter 29, Joriksen, questioned by the Grey—[[amir-sedoj|Amir the Grey]]—admits to deceiving both the Catalian and the baron: faith did not help, Red merely recovered, and the well proved ordinary. The magnus reports that Dunkwald was found dead, there is not a single schönbrun there, and “the Catalian did not even dig down to water.” In Chapter 31, Major Klein, Joriksen's old friend, reminds him of his words “I believe in you, Catallian”; Klein himself is mentioned as a man once sent to serve in Dunkwald. Red does not appear in these chapters; the connections between him and the other storylines remain unrevealed at this point in the novel. For the destruction of Dunkwald, see the [[gibel-dunkvalda|separate article]].

## Relationships
- [[yakka|Yakka]]—Red's hunter and guard; initially an escort, later almost a friend. He gives him a figurine, brings the fugitive back alive, and takes no revenge. After the first execution, he breaks with Red and leaves.
- [[joriksen|Fritjof Joriksen]]—his master and the schönbrun's “inventor”; promises freedom, keeps conscientious records, then abandons Red without saying goodbye.
- [[greta|Greta]]—the “prize,” lover, teacher, and ultimately the woman who betrayed Red.
- [[landau|Baron Landau]]—Red's enemy; punishes him with a Zustand that “kills the soul.”
- [[klyajn|Wolf (Lieutenant Klein)]]—the Falcon who knows who Red is and secretly pities him. Klein in Chapter 31 is a major of the Claws, the same man; the novel uses different names for him: “the Falcon,” “Wolf,” and “Klein.”
- [[karmensita-estevos|Kara]] and his son—left behind in Laerta.
- The dugout foreman—Red's first enemy, killed by his chain.

## Conception and Interviews
According to the author, Red's storyline takes place before Alvaro's and Dietrich's, but the storylines “converge in a certain way”; he gives no further details. He says Red's true name can be guessed by someone who knows Spanish, but naming it is “something that definitely must not be said.” The author calls Red's life in Dangar “utter hell,” which, he observes, accounts for readers' negative attitude toward Dangar. Among the Dangarians, he says he likes Master Joriksen—“Yurikson” in the transcript—who also appears in Dietrich's storyline. (The interviews were transcribed automatically, and Red's name appears in them with a variant Russian spelling.)

## From Dunkwald to the Army (ch. 2, 6, 33)
Red's storyline precedes the others. At the end of ch. 33, he is taken from Dunkwald into the army, and it becomes clear whom the reader has already met: he is Killnot, the “stone” rifleman in the fusilier platoon attached to [[ditrikh|Dietrich's]] battery in ch. 2.

**Killnot in ch. 2.** Among the platoon's stones, Dietrich notices a foreigner: black hair, sharp cheekbones, a dark face, unlike a Dangarian—but “every third stone is a foreigner.” He speaks with an accent and mixes up words. No chain with a stone is visible on him, and when he opens his shirt, his neck is ringed by a scar of linked circles left by a red-hot chain. Where a slave wears his protective stone, “THOU SHALT NOT KILL” has been burned into the flesh. “Doesn't hurt,” he answers when offered sympathy. NCO [[gelb|Gelb]] explains that the soldier is incapable of killing: “that is how he was brought up”; in formation, he fires into the sky. Yet he is the platoon's most valuable man, strong and tireless, and can dig as much as three men; together with [[spasenie|Salvation]], he carries water from the well.

Dietrich envies him: Killnot is the only person in the army entitled not to kill. The man objects that “he did not decide it himself,” and, asked whether he would trade places, replies, “For freedom.” That night, the platoon of stones, apart from Salvation, escapes after maiming Gelb. Killnot takes no part in the battle with the Ollandian hussars. In ch. 6, Dietrich suggests there was an instigator among the fugitives and tells Major [[klyajn|Klein]] about the rifleman with “Thou shalt not kill” on his chest.

**What comes together in ch. 33.**
- Red receives the brand “THOU SHALT NOT KILL” in Dunkwald. Handing him over to the army's tribute collectors, [[landau|Landau]] orders him to show his chest and explains: this stone killed eleven guards, and his punishment was a [[tsushtand|Zustand]] that would destroy his soul. The inability to kill is the opposite of the “beast's” Zustand.
- Red has worn a stone around his neck since ch. 15—“Du bist Stein. Ich bin Catalian”—and his foreign accent and facial features match what Dietrich saw.
- Red is taken into the army on General Brauschwitz's orders, having been recommended by the Kaiser’s Claws; Landau recognizes Klein's hand in it.
- The platoon's escape in ch. 2 is apparently how Red frees himself from slavery during the [[olandskaya-vojna|Ollandian War]]: da Vira returns to Catalia “in the company of Dangarian deserters.”

## The Storylines Converge (ch. 34)
In the final chapter, the storylines converge. Alvaro realizes that Rodrigo da Vira, navigator of the Santa Eliza, did not die at [[solanta|Solanta]]: he was sold into Dangarian slavery and has begun his revenge only now because before he was somewhere “where revenge was the least of his worries”; he returned with Dangarian deserters, a brand on his chest, and knowledge of [[kolodets|wells]]. Red's identity is thus revealed: the Catalian's storyline, which preceded the others, is the backstory of the avenger from Tres Molinos.

All the clues fit:
- Red is a Catalian from [[laerta|Laerta]], a former sailor with a tattoo of [[eliga|Eliga]].
- He received a fatal diagnosis of “sarcoma,” which proved mistaken in Dunkwald. Dr. [[khuanes|Juanes]] gave da Vira the same false diagnosis.
- Red's son in Laerta and da Vira's child by [[karmensita-estevos|Carmensita Estevos]] are the same boy: the Kara whom Red remembers is Carmensita. The novel does not explicitly state this, but the esfera of Shadow, Laerta, and the letters from Dangar all match.
- Red dug a schönbrun and saw how wells work. The avenger placed a dark schönbrun in van Halm's castle.
- Rodrigo is a Spanish name: the author did say that Red's real name could be guessed by someone who knew Spanish.

## In the Catalian Storyline (ch. 18–34)
In the Catalian storyline, Red is present only as a name—Rodrigo da Vira, navigator of the [[santa-eliza|Santa Eliza]] and the sixth participant in the [[solantijskaya-ekspeditsiya|Solantan Expedition]], absent from Prim-Allen [[filippe|Filippe di Berro's]] list of heroes. Officially, he died on [[solanta|Solanta]] and was posthumously awarded the Order of Courage. Da Vira's name is first a mystery, then a false lead, and finally the solution.

[[karlos|Carlos el Niña]] is the first to say the name: there was such a sailor, “he was, and then he was no more,” and [[khuanes|Juanes]] was to blame for the disaster that befell him. “It began with da Vira and ended with this.” Quartermaster [[zolotoj-dukat|Golden Ducat]], who has served Carlos for more than a year, does not know da Vira.

In ch. 22, [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] and [[beatris|Beatriz]] search for information in the [[svitok|Great Scroll]]. The records portray a hot-tempered brawler constantly brought before the courts: a trader's broken nose, a Muari shot dead, a brothel fight, a killed dog, a fire aboard someone else's ship, and a challenge to Captain el Niña himself, which the captain won. But da Vira neither drank nor caroused: he won a debate for the title of master navigator, and the court's wording declares his arguments “unquestionably compelling.” Three months after the expedition, Carlos and [[fatima|Fatima]], acting as the dead man's lawyer, won a case against the [[vysokij-stol|High Table]], and da Vira was posthumously awarded the Order of Courage: according to Fatima, on the night of the assault on the coastal fort, he threw himself over a grenade. Later records mention Carmensita Estevos, an obrero of Shadow, who demanded recognition as da Vira's widow and of her infant as his son; she was refused for lack of evidence.

Alvaro pieces together a theory: da Vira was Fatima's lover, she became pregnant, the doctor revealed her secret to her husband, he hired killers, and now Fatima's son is taking revenge. This theory proves wrong (ch. 28).

The name “RODRIGO DA VIRA” draws an intent stare from the mad [[liess-van-khalm|van Halm]] (ch. 26). In ch. 28, offended by Fatima's deception, Alvaro strikes a sore spot by reminding her of da Vira; she turns pale, shouts “Out!”, and throws a shoe at him. In ch. 30, Alvaro still believes da Vira was a navigator who died on Solanta, leaving a child, and that Fatima loved him. In ch. 32, Fernan objects that even an affair between Fatima and da Vira has not been established, and Carlos could not have killed the navigator: he was vital to the expedition in dangerous waters. In [[tres-molinos|Tres Molinos]], Alvaro demands the truth about da Vira's death from [[izabel|Isabel]].

## The Past and Revenge (ch. 34)
Held captive by killers in the castle, Alvaro is struck in the ribs, and an idea flashes through his mind: what if da Vira is alive and taking revenge himself? He sets out his theory, which Fatima later partially confirms. In it, da Vira was a hot-tempered navigator aboard Carlos's ship and argued with him in public. Carlos bought a chart of the approach to [[solanta|Solanta]] from an island sailor and devised the expedition with the manufacturer [[kristobal-maduro|Maduro]]. During the voyage, Juanes diagnosed da Vira with incurable sarcoma of the lungs and told the captain. When the Doge of Solanta demanded the man who knew the secret channel, the crew, according to Alvaro, handed da Vira over. The Doge did not hang him but sold him into slavery, which was crueller still. Rodrigo ended up in [[dangar|Dangar]], first with the fishermen, learning something about Wells, then in the army; he found comrades among the “stones” and escaped with them during the [[olandskaya-vojna|Ollandian War]], bringing them to Catalia. Alvaro believes he returned with a brand burned into his chest.

Then, according to Alvaro's theory, da Vira heard of the cursed house near Tres Molinos and realized it probably contained a “dark schönbrun” ([[temnyj-shyonbrun]]). He seized the castle Alvaro calls Last Winter ([[posledniaya-zima]]), boarded up the southern wing containing the well so that no outsiders would be harmed, and imprisoned van Halm. At his command, the philosopher wrote five invitations to the heroes. The guests came to discover how much the philosopher knew about their true “feat,” and drank the water of misfortune. Fatima confirms the letter Carlos received from da Vira: it was threatening and ended by saying his revenge had already been accomplished. The secret channel to Solanta was drawn on the reverse. Da Vira never appears under his own name in the Catalian storyline.

Fatima corrects Alvaro on the details. She never had an affair with da Vira: she cannot stand aggressive brutes, despised him, and received the same contempt in return; she was glad when he “landed in the Solantan prison.” She helped Carlos win the court case whose testimony established da Vira as dead. No one tried to ransom him from slavery: everyone believed he was terminally ill and felt no remorse. In the letter, da Vira reproached the heroes for abandoning him and failing to care for his son. According to Alvaro, all that is known of Carmensita is that she bore his child; Salvador responds that da Vira wrote to her from Dangar.

Alvaro himself calls the conclusion a theory. He considers Juanes's diagnosis mistaken, but the doctor insisted until his death that it had been correct. Alvaro believes [[salvador|Salvador]] is hunting da Vira on the orders of an unseen employer, but that too is his own deduction. Da Vira is not shown under that name in the Catalian chapters; ch. 34 ends with Fatima warning that trouble still lies ahead.

## Revenge Was Planned in Advance (ch. 32–34)
Red was preparing his revenge long before Alvaro took up the case. In ch. 32, the shopkeeper [[khavyer-salam|Javier Salam]] of [[tres-molinos|Tres Molinos]] tells Alvaro and Fernan that shortly before the visit of Solanta's five heroes, the philosopher [[liess-van-khalm|van Halm]] dismissed his old servants and hired six new ones. They came from the empire: a tall, strong man with an unkind face, three retired soldiers, a young man, and a woman. They wore no bracelets and spoke Dangarian among themselves. They lived in the House Above for a month or two and left the day after the guests departed the [[posledniaya-zima|castle]].

The leader of the six spoke Catalian well, bought a large quantity of tools from the shopkeeper, and overpaid without bargaining. Burned letters were visible beneath his collarbone. Neighbors remembered the Dangarians as polite, quiet people, while Fernan considers them runaway deserters. In ch. 34, Alvaro understands who led the group: da Vira, that is, Red, with “THOU SHALT NOT KILL” branded on his chest. The dark [[temnyj-shyonbrun|schönbrun]] beneath the castle is his work. The reader learns of this visit retrospectively: by the time Alvaro arrives in Tres Molinos, Red is long gone.

## At the End of the Novel (ch. 33)
At the end of Chapter Thirty-Three, Red is alive: he is unchained and taken from Dunkwald into the army on General Brauschwitz's orders. “THOU SHALT NOT KILL” is burned into his chest; Greta lies at the bottom of the pit with her throat hacked open by a shovel, and over the years he himself has become a “beast.” In the army, he becomes the “stone” rifleman Killnot whom Dietrich meets in ch. 2; ch. 34 reveals that he is Rodrigo da Vira (see the sections above).
