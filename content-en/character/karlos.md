Carlos el Niña is a pirate-smuggler, a don of the [[teni|esfera of Shadow]], and the owner of a former fort in the [[staryj-port|Old Port]] of [[laerta|Laerta]]. He appears first on the list of heroes for the book commissioned from the writer [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]] by Prim-Allen [[filippe|Filippe di Berro]]: a “buccaneer,” a participant in the [[solantijskaya-ekspeditsiya|Solantan Expedition]]. To Alvaro, he is a man easily made into a hero, but Carlos himself dreams of something else: being known as the book's villain.

## Character and Appearance
Carlos wears a black captain's outfit with gold epaulettes and a hat with three scarlet feathers; a double-barreled pistol and a dirk are tucked into his belt. He is boisterous and fond of theatrical gestures; he attended the premiere of the opera Senorita Violet and borrowed his pistol “joke” from it. His main affliction in peacetime is boredom. He keeps his men under strict discipline; Alvaro considers him an honest man, although he remembers that Carlos once shot a captain who had done wrong.

Carlos treats his wife with conspicuous tenderness: he calls her his “little bird,” orders a shawl and tea with honey for her, and forbids “gloomy talk”—discussion of deaths—in her presence. This tenderness ultimately proves to be his weakness as well.

## Meeting Alvaro and the Fort in the Old Port (ch. 7–9)
After receiving Alvaro's letter requesting a meeting, Carlos replies with a black envelope bearing the crossed knives of the esfera of Shadow and a black spot. When the friends, Alvaro and [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan Gereira]], arrive at the fort, pirates with curved knives greet them, while their host offers Alvaro a choice between the barrels of a double-barreled pistol: only one is loaded. The weapon fails to fire both when aimed at Alvaro and when the other trigger is pulled while aimed at Fernan; Fernan faints. It turns out to be a prank.

Carlos asks to be made a “tiger”—a cruel villain—but Alvaro explains that the book is biographical and has no antagonist. As compensation, he immediately invents a feat involving a Muari galleon and three Ollandian princesses, and Carlos eagerly elaborates on the legend himself. In reality, he is a smuggler rather than a corsair, and the “slave girls” in the courtyard are his wife's nieces sorting silk.

The narrator supplies his true biography. Carlos began as a marksman aboard a corsair schooner in the Long Sea, rose to command ten marksmen and then became quartermaster; after the captain died, the crew elected him captain. When the crew asked how they could die less often, he came up with the idea of buying goods duty-free from island traders instead of plundering them, then reselling them in Laerta at a double markup. He later met a woman who demanded that he give up his risky trade; he won a [[duelnyj-spor|dueling debate]] in the [[palata-slova|Chamber of the Word]], received the highest bracelet, and bought the old fort.

He waves aside questions about the [[solantijskaya-ekspeditsiya|Solanta campaign]]: there was nothing but talk, so ask [[fatima|Fatima]]. He says Dr. [[khuanes|Juanes]] was no hero at all. Showing his guests the Well in the cellar, he pretends there is nothing special about it and does not look into it himself (see [[kolodets|Wells]] for details). Alvaro later notices this oddity and records it among the heroes' secrets (ch. 9).

## His Wife's Disappearance (ch. 18–22)
When Alvaro comes to the fort to question Carlos about Juanes's death, he finds the pirate drunk in a darkened room. Four days earlier, Isabel disappeared near the [[galereya-eliste|Eliste Gallery]] during the [[filosofiya-uzhasa|Philosophy of Horror]] exhibition; all that anyone saw was a carriage turning the corner. Carlos believes a lone individual did it, rather than the dons of Shadow: if he learned otherwise, he would “bombard the entire district.”

He presses Alvaro's revolver to his temple, chin, and chest in turn and pulls the trigger—but no shot goes off. He has an explanation: [[eliga|Eliga]] gives everyone a cup of misfortunes, at the bottom of which lies the single greatest one; once it has happened, the cup is empty, and nothing worse can occur. His final shot pierces a barrel of bonk, and he and Alvaro get drunk.

During their conversation, Carlos says Juanes “talked too much,” which displeased both him and Fatima: “It began with da Vira and ended with this.” He swears “on his little bird” that he did not kill the doctor. His quartermaster [[zolotoj-dukat|Golden Ducat]] adds that on white land, “there are no white murderers.” On his way back, Alvaro is attacked in an alley by an unknown assailant and shot through the palm.

In ch. 22, Alvaro builds a mistaken theory from Carlos's words: [[red|da Vira]] was Fatima's lover, and someone is taking revenge. In this theory, Carlos is merely a friend in need of help.

## The Secret of Solanta's Heroes (ch. 30–34)
Alvaro notices that Carlos took his wife to the Philosophy of Horror exhibition on the day she disappeared, and includes him among the five heroes connected with the mad philosopher [[liess-van-khalm|van Halm]] (ch. 30). In [[tres-molinos|Tres Molinos]], it emerges that Isabel is alive and has left him for a lover. According to her, over the past year Carlos became a tyrant and treated her as a possession; she repeats his remark about a “little bird” that “will not fly out of its cage” (ch. 32). Alvaro tells her that her husband is drinking and tried to shoot himself, but the gun misfired.

In ch. 34, Alvaro unravels the past. Carlos bought a chart of the approach to [[solanta|Solanta]] from an island sailor, devised an expedition with the manufacturer [[kristobal-maduro|Maduro]] to bypass the Muari monopoly, and took Rodrigo da Vira as his navigator. After learning from the doctor that the navigator was terminally ill, he handed da Vira over when the Doge of Solanta demanded the man who knew the secret channel. Many years later, drunk and pleased with himself, Carlos told his young wife about it. According to Alvaro's theory, losing Isabel was the price he paid for the water from the “dark” Well (see [[temnyj-shyonbrun]]). Fatima says that a couple of days after visiting van Halm, Carlos received a letter from the living da Vira, ending with the statement that his revenge had already been accomplished.

## The Secret of da Vira (ch. 34)
The ending reveals that the navigator Carlos handed over to the Doge of Solanta is the same man as [[red|Red]] in the Dangarian chapters (see [[red|Rodrigo da Vira]]). The threatening letter Carlos received was written by Rodrigo after his return from slavery.
## Relationships
- [[izabel|Isabel]]—his wife; the novel depicts both his tenderness and his suffocating protectiveness.
- [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]]—the author of his “official” biography; Carlos considers himself in his debt and threatens to kill him if he dies without finishing the book (conveyed through Golden Ducat in ch. 13).
- [[zolotoj-dukat|Golden Ducat]]—his quartermaster and loyal aide.
- [[red|Rodrigo da Vira]]—his former navigator; their enmity is the hidden driving force of the plot.

## At the End of the Novel (ch. 34)
Carlos is left without his wife, close to despair. The novel does not separately show whether he learns the truth about who is behind his troubles.
