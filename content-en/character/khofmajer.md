Hofmeyer is a Dangarian lieutenant general commanding one of the three armies that invaded [[ollandiya|Ollandia]]. The novel scarcely shows him in action: before ch. 31, he is discussed as the commander whose regiments advanced east, and in ch. 31 he appears onstage for the first time. He matters to the plot as the man connected with Major [[klyajn|Klein's]] plans for a schönbrun of victory, and as an example of a Dangarian commander who realistically assesses their chances in the coming war with Catalia.

## Mentions (ch. 2–14)

In ch. 2, the narrator describes how the war “moved east”: following the Ollandian infantry scattered at [[plyutva|Plyutva]], Hofmeyer's and [[kurtsleben|Kurtzleben's]] regiments advanced, supply wagons and trains rolled past, and Lieutenant [[ditrikh|Dietrich von Dort's]] battery was left in the rear. In ch. 6, Major Klein explains to Dietrich how important his victory was: had the hussars captured the guns, they could have destroyed the station and disrupted supplies to Hofmeyer's army. In ch. 14, a conversation with Fischmeister [[joriksen|Joriksen]] reveals that the war is still being fought in eastern Ollandia and Hofmeyer's regiments are fighting outside Mershants; suspecting that a senior officer sent Dietrich to inquire about the schönbrun, Joriksen asks whether it was Kurtzleben or Hofmeyer.

## The Conference in Latsen (ch. 31)

In [[latsen|Latsen]], at the former nobles' assembly building that has become Field Marshal [[rajniger|Reiniger's]] headquarters, Hofmeyer is seen in full for the first time. The narrator describes him as lean and considers him the least distinguished of the three army commanders, but “probably the most capable.” He was given a comparatively weak army and a modest objective: to take the Mershants Voivodeship. Yet the direction of his advance reflects the emperor's own intentions: Mershants is a fortified district near Catalia's borders and will be “as necessary as air” in a future war.

Hofmeyer brings a pointed iron object to the conference: a shell from a rifled gun, at least a dozen of which fall on his positions every day. Major Klein, a specialist on Catalia, clarifies that such guns are expensive and rare, and Hofmeyer acknowledges that two rifled howitzers are operating against him in Mershants; however, their range is four versts against his mortars' one and a half, and they destroy his batteries before they can get into position. He proposes taking the fortress once the marsh on its eastern side freezes: an assault force of two or three hundred dismounted eagle cavalrymen would cross the ice on foot, blow up the wall, and enter the breach. The field marshal and the other generals ridicule the plan and order him to transfer four divisions to Kurtzleben for a flanking maneuver across the [[khursla|Khursla]] River; Hofmeyer is only to maintain the siege of Mershants. With a deadline of two days, he does not even have time to return to his army, and his subordinate, Major General Walbrecht, will lead the divisions south.

## Conversation with Klein (ch. 31)

That evening, Klein visits Hofmeyer's mistress and waits for the general at her home. He cautiously probes his views and is initially mistaken: Hofmeyer is by no means opposed to war with Catalia, as his report might have suggested. He says a war with [[kataliya|Catalia]] will be harder than any previous one: the Catalians have a dense railway network and command of the sea, while the Dangarians have a single Plyutva–Mershants line that the enemy will destroy; Catalian cavalry carries carbines with twice the range, and rifled barrels will soon enter mass production. But in his view, they must fight now: today a wolf pack can still defeat a lion; in ten years, it will be “a lion against a pack of cockroaches.” Klein confirms that he fully shares this view.

Klein then proposes a stratagem: if the courier carrying the order to transfer the divisions fails to arrive, and Hofmeyer stays in Latsen for another fifteen days, the autumn mud will have set in by his return; the divisions will be unable to make a rapid march, and he will have to take Mershants with his own army. Hofmeyer understands the hint and replies that Klein apparently has grounds for thinking the courier will not arrive. Klein jokes that everything is in God's hands. Hofmeyer dreams of a field marshal's star, Klein of a patch bearing a red phoenix; both need a war to achieve their ambitions. Later, the major tells Joriksen he has found him “the best client”: Hofmeyer, who needs a schönbrun of victory, and demands that the fischmeister fish within two weeks, while the general remains in Latsen.

## In Catalia (ch. 28–32)

In Catalia, Hofmeyer's name is heard after the war. In ch. 28, General [[dezerej|Deserey]] tells [[fatima|Fatima]] that six months have passed since Ollandia's surrender, yet Hofmeyer's army remains in the eastern province of Mershants, right on Catalia's border. The Dangarians are building the Plyutva–Latsen–Mershants railway and bringing in thirty-pounder siege mortars—there were fifteen, and ten more have been delivered: weapons useful only for destroying fortress walls, and thus for an offensive war. In ch. 30, [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]] comes to Deserey to ask how many mortars Hofmeyer's army has. In ch. 32, Deserey's journals show that he considers Hofmeyer the most gifted of the Dangarian commanders, “capable of unconventional thinking”; by contrast, he regarded Reiniger as an experienced coward, [[brojenshtoff|Brojenstoff]] as a dim-witted career soldier, and Kurtzleben as a butcher.

## At the End of the Novel (ch. 32)

By the final chapters, Hofmeyer commands the army in Mershants, and Deserey sees him as the Catalian army's main opponent. The novel does not say whether Klein's scheme to delay the courier succeeded or whether Mershants was taken.
