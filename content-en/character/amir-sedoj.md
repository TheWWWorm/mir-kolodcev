Amir the Grey is the magnus, head of the order and guild of fishermen, the [[fishmajster|fischmeisters]]. Dietrich calls him the third man in Dangar and the most mysterious of the world’s rulers. He appears in person in only one scene in the novel, but is discussed in other chapters as well.

## Appearance and manner
The Grey dresses in the fashion of high society, but his frock coat is azure with golden stars, resembling a wizard’s robe from a fairy tale. He has gray hair, a long, narrow beard, and woolen gloves: the old man’s hands get cold. He speaks smoothly, in a “voice of sea waves,” which calms his listeners. Joriksen addresses him as “Your Wisdom” and “mentor.”

## Joriksen’s teacher (ch. 21–23)
Otto, the secretary, tells Dietrich that Joriksen is the finest fisherman of his generation and a pupil of Magnus Amir the Grey, but not yet an oberfischmeister. In chapter 23, Baron [[landau|von Landau]], the oberfischmeister of Dunkwald, tells the hunter [[yakka|Yakka]] that everyone serves someone: the Grey serves the Sower and the great truth, while the baron himself serves the magnus and the guild.

## The conversation in the railway carriage (ch. 29)
At Hofstadt station, the Grey enters the compartment where Joriksen, Otto, and Dietrich are sitting. He takes a golden clepsydra from his pocket: while water runs through it, the train will not depart, and the sounds outside fade; when he touches its bulbs, the drops fall ever more slowly. The magnus speaks like a man who is not asking questions, but has long known the answers. He tests Otto, praises Joriksen for immediately making the young man his personal assistant, and checks Dietrich’s story: Dietrich has been presented as an intermediary between the master and his client, Prince [[lents|Lenz]].

In passing, the Grey raises the subject of [[olkha|Olkha’s]] reputation and utters a phrase in Ollandian that Dietrich does not understand, testing his reaction. In translation, the phrase turns out to praise Dietrich as a friend of the Lenz family.

Then the magnus turns to business. The Glory of the Empire has printed the truth: a postman found [[dunkvald|Dunkwald]] full of the dead — four hundred people, including three platoons of soldiers — and forest animals had torn the bodies apart. The authorities lost three months; the magnus’s people reached the site at the end of summer. The bones show that there were many killers, not all of them human, and that knives, bullets, teeth, and claws were used. There is nothing in the records for the last year. The magnus believes the patriarch has an interest in the guild’s troubles: the Kaiser dines with the patriarch on Tuesdays and Saturdays and listens to news from [[varkhajt|Wahrheit’s]] newspaper.

He then questions Joriksen about what he knows of dark schönbruns and his service in Dunkwald, about Baron Landau, and about why the baron released the young master. The Grey knows that Joriksen lied to Oberfischmeister [[flammel|Flammel]] and makes him confess. As an artilleryman, Dietrich explains the marks as the work of canister and bayonets, and suggests looking for a cannon on the forest road. Before leaving, the magnus announces that every pit has been inspected and there is not a single schönbrun there: according to him, the Catalian did not even dig down to water. As he leaves the compartment, he releases time, and the train starts moving. Joriksen afterward wipes away his sweat and says that he has never seen the magnus so enraged.

## Interest in Joriksen (ch. 31)
Joriksen tells Klein that Flammel and the Grey questioned him. He went “as close to the truth as possible,” but noticed that the Grey had taken an interest in him and would soon take an interest in Klein as well. Only the cover story about Dort being an intermediary reassured the magnus.

## Conception and interviews
According to the author in the first stream, the second book in the World of Wells will feature court affairs in Dangar: the Kaiser, the patriarch, and the chief magnus interact, and much will be said about their relationships.
