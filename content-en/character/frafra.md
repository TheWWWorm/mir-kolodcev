Franz Freiber, also known as Fra-Fra, is a fair-haired young man whom Dietrich encounters in queues for appointments at the capital's government offices. He appears in one scene in Chapter 10.

## Conversation in a Beer Hall (ch. 10)
Dietrich is looking for a position in Hofstadt, visiting office after office and being turned away everywhere: his temporary discharge and interrogation by the Kaiser’s Claws have barred his way into the army, so he is trying to enter the civil service. The fair-haired young man, whom he has seen in queues more than once, takes him to a beer hall and explains the rules: do not bother without a recommendation; the queue is only for show, and the position goes to someone who has a word put in for him; recommendations must be sought from people of standing—a governor, councilor, judge, or administrator.

When Dietrich replies that he has no influential acquaintances, the young man introduces himself: Franz Freiber, or simply Fra-Fra. He knows the lieutenant's name and offers him a temporary position that, he claims, is sheer pleasure rather than work. The novel does not state exactly what this business is: Dietrich asks him to repeat himself, Fra-Fra explains again “more clearly,” and the lieutenant flies into a rage: “I am an eagle! A combat officer! How dare you!” Freiber objects that eagles have taken such work before and calls it the most eagle-like occupation of all. Dietrich calls him disgusting and leaves. He later sees Fra-Fra in the queues again, but looks away every time.
