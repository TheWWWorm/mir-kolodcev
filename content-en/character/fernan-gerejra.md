**Fernan Gereira** is [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva's]] loyal friend, a street scribe, and a committed reformist. Because of his place in the social hierarchy, he constantly reminds the protagonist of the world's injustices; together with his wife [[lyusil|Lucille]], he represents the “ordinary person's” view of [[kataliya|Catalia]] that Alvaro argues with in almost every chapter. In the investigation, he is first a skeptic, then a secretary and scribe, and finally a participant in the fight at the castle.

## Character and Occupation
Fernan works as a scribe: he writes down the words of illiterate [[obrero|obreros]] and composes messages for postcards, greetings, and love letters. His speech is florid and grandiloquent. Work is becoming scarcer as people learn to read and write, and he must live on modest earnings of about a hundred escudos a month. He is a man of culture, avoids fights, and makes no secret of his fear: when [[karlos|Carlos el Niña]] points an unloaded pistol at him, Fernan faints. At the same time, he is stubborn and stands firmly by his views: he regards the rich as “parasites” and “bloodsuckers,” and occasionally calls Alvaro a long-eared ass.

Fernan's central idea is to reform the [[vysokij-stol|High Table]]: the number of allens representing the [[esfera|esferas]] should be proportional to the number of people in each estate. At present, he explains, every esfera has three allens, and six million peasants have no more representation than a hundred thousand people from the world of art. He prays to Clarisa, the mirna of vitality, and has a sound knowledge of the law as recorded in the [[svitok|Great Scroll]].

## Alvaro's Friend (ch. 0–7)
In ch. 0, Fernan walks with Alvaro and Lucille along Del Toro Boulevard, setting out plans for reform together with his wife. Alvaro takes his friends to a restaurant beside the Grand Opera, where he loses a dueling debate (ch. 1)—see [[duelnyj-spor-ramiresa|“The Dueling Debate with Ramires”]] for details. Fernan is pleased that Alvaro is now poorer and will “grow closer to the people,” but his friend merely dismisses his servants and waits for his next fee. In ch. 3, Fernan and Lucille recount the story of the debate to the reformists, and [[osa|Wasp]] thanks Alvaro for it.

When Alvaro receives a commission from [[filippe|Don Filippe]] to write a book about five heroes, Fernan is initially displeased: the “bloodsuckers” are a general, a pirate, a manufacturer, and “the chief snake of that judicial nest.” In ch. 5, he talks his way into becoming Alvaro's secretary to “reconnoiter” the parasitic esferas before the future reform. Alvaro agrees on two conditions: Fernan must make no reformist speeches and must not use any dirt he discovers for political purposes. That same day, the friends go to the home of the late [[khuanes|Juanes]].

In ch. 7, Fernan tries to show his friend the injustice of the world: he brings a book supposedly written by a machine, tells him about manufacturers and steam-powered machines, and invites him to a demonstration. At el Niña's fort, he records their host's tall tales and asks about the [[solantijskaya-ekspeditsiya|Solantan Expedition]], only to be brushed off with “ask Fatima.”

## Investigation and Riot (ch. 9–13)
In ch. 9, Fernan proposes building the book around a contrast between an “evil” pirate and a “good” doctor; Alvaro objects that in life it is the other way around. Fernan is charmed by Beatriz and her steam-powered Esperanza, readily agrees to travel to [[ortiyar|Ortiyar]], and pushes the carriage when it runs out of water. He is also the first to suspect that they are being followed, passing his suspicions on to Alvaro: one night at the inn, the friends mistake the chauffeur [[ugo-martines|Ugo Martinez]] for a spy and make fools of themselves (ch. 11). Remembering that he is a cultured man, Fernan steps aside at the crucial moment and leaves Alvaro to seize the “spy” by the collar.

In ch. 11, the story turns to the [[stachka-v-laerte|obrero unrest in Laerta]]. Lucille takes her husband and Alvaro to the station, where workers are building a barricade across the tracks. Fernan falls during the fighting and is struck with a club. At home, Lucille treats him while he curses [[balboa|Balboa]], Shadow, and the “snakes of Law” led by Fatima. In ch. 13, after discovering a severed finger, Alvaro falls into a creative crisis and abandons the book. Fernan visits at night and sorts the mail; when he learns that his friend has turned down seven thousand escudos, he shakes him by the shoulders. He persuades Alvaro to visit the manufacturer [[kristobal-maduro|Maduro]] for the sake of a chapter for Filippe. He and Lucille give Alvaro a guide to poisons, which will prove useful when he needs to determine what poisoned the doctor. Fernan also reports that the reformists are suing the manufacturers for using cannons against the workers, although he already knows Fatima's “snakes” will once again side with the manufacturers.

## In the Investigation's Shadow (ch. 16–24)
In ch. 16, Filippe shows Alvaro a newspaper report about the fight at the station, in which Fernan also took part. In ch. 18, Fernan and Lucille help their friend overcome his apathy; Fernan then reminds him about money. After Alvaro is wounded (ch. 20), Fernan offers to serve as his “pen” until his hand heals and urges him to help [[mor|Mor]]: the reformists want to challenge the earlier ruling on loans in the Chamber of the Word, and to do so they need Fatima to withdraw her lawyers. Alvaro sees this as an attempt to “put him in bed” with the allen and leaves in anger. In ch. 24, the butler [[emil|Emil]] remembers Alvaro “as a package deal with Fernan.”

## Fernan and the Law (ch. 20)
The conversation at the [[tri-byka|Three Bulls]] shows that Fernan knows the records of the Great Scroll well. He finds the argument on which Mor and the reformists base their plan: in Catalia, any voluntary transaction is legal, and a bank may accept anything as collateral—rumors, discoveries, seawater, a living person, a body part. As an example, he tells of a famous actress who pledged her right leg: the bank did not take the leg away, but gained the right to determine how it was used for one year. That means a factory's future profits can also serve as collateral, and the manufacturers won the credit dispute only because they hired handpicked lawyers. Fernan wants Alvaro to influence Fatima and promises to serve as his pen until his hand heals.

## Betrayal and Reconciliation (ch. 30)
In ch. 30, Alvaro brings a letter from Mor to a scribe's booth on Crafts Square—a crude, angry letter that the scribe himself wrote in his presence. The man turns out to be Fernan in disguise. The letter reveals that Mor knows Alvaro had no idea his student Beatriz was [[fatima|Fatima's]] daughter. Alvaro reasons that only one person could have told her and punches Fernan in the face with his wounded hand, hurting them both. Fernan confesses: he wanted a contact with [[fatima|Fatima]] for the reformists and material for the book; Lucille had warned him that concealing the truth was wrong. Alvaro observes that Fernan is already the third person, after Fatima and Beatriz, to argue that lying is good—and decides that, since everyone is using him, it is his turn to use everyone. He appoints Fernan his scribe for the coming journey; Fernan closes his booth, joking that once again he has not earned a single peso.

## Selezia and “Last Winter” (ch. 32–34)
In Tres Molinos, Fernan reads all thirteen of Deserey's journals and helps unravel the strange events in [[liess-van-khalm|van Halm's]] house. Fernan recalls [[khavyer-salam|Javier Salam's]] statement that van Halm had begun sending servants away before the frost, which means he did so twice. He also questions the theory of an affair between Fatima and da Vira: she has only one confirmed child, Beatriz. He proposes a theory: the philosopher's “servants” were runaway Dangarian slaves, and they themselves let van Halm out of his bedroom.

In ch. 34, Alvaro and Fernan go to [[posledniaya-zima|Last Winter]]. In van Halm's bedroom, Alvaro plays a trick on Fernan to demonstrate his point: he ties a bag of ice to the door, trapping his friend inside, to show how the jammed lock worked. During the killers' attack, Fernan is shot in the leg; afterward he advances together with Beatriz, feigning weakness, and strikes [[salvador|Salvador]] in the face with a burning lantern. Earlier, he voices the cowardly suggestion that they test the well on Beatriz, but this was part of Alvaro's script. Fernan later leaves for Laerta with Beatriz in the Esperanza.

## Relationships (ch. 34)
- [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]]—friend and sparring partner in argument; their friendship survives quarrels and betrayals.
- [[lyusil|Lucille]]—the wife who restored his common sense and put his household in order.
- [[beatris|Beatriz]]—an ally in the castle, although initially he is charmed by her.
- [[mor|Mor]] and [[osa|Wasp]]—reformists with whom he is connected.

## At the End of the Novel (ch. 34)
Fernan is alive, wounded in the leg, and remains active in the party of the left. The reader also knows him as a man who is cautious in words but decisive in action when an enemy must be struck.
