**Lucille Gereira** has “only recently” become [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan Gereira's]] wife, as Alvaro puts it in the preface, and is [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva's]] closest female friend. She works as a hired nanny. In the novel, she is the most sensible of the friends: she feeds the hungry writer, calms her husband, and answers Alvaro without ceremony when he becomes too pleased with himself.

## Character and Biography
Lucille knows how to make herself understood and is not afraid to disagree: in ch. 1, she calls Alvaro unbearable for forgetting how far below him his friends stand in rank. She does “the work of two,” sometimes at night, earning enough for the couple to rent their own room, eat in restaurants, and go to the theater with standing-room tickets.

In ch. 7, the narrator recounts her life in detail as an example of the principle “skill is the key.” Lucille is the second of a poor cleaner's five daughters. As a girl, she ate a handful of rice and slept on a mat by the door; she left home with one dress and a pair of shoes and arrived in the capital with nothing, but her skill at caring for children has made her highly sought after, with many masters among her clients. Alvaro cites her as an argument against her husband's gloomy talk of injustice.

## Friend and “Housekeeper” (ch. 5–13)
In ch. 5, Lucille offers to feed the impoverished Alvaro so he need not sell his conscience. In ch. 9, she wakes the friends, who have spent the night at his home, and brings cofenado made to the Gereira family recipe—coffee, pepper, and orange juice: she has a lesson at eleven with a professor's grandson. The silk scarf around her neck is a gift from Alvaro. Through her, he receives a letter from General [[dezerej|Deserey]], which the messenger mistook for a message from the writer's mistress.

In ch. 11, Lucille enthusiastically supports the strikers: she explains to her husband and Alvaro that the [[obrero|obreros]] dismissed because of steam-powered machines lost their debate with the manufacturers, and takes them to the station. During the clash, she hides behind a locomotive and is the first to realize the cannon volleys are blanks. Later, she treats Fernan. In ch. 13, she and her husband send Alvaro a guide to poisons to help establish what poisoned Dr. [[khuanes|Juanes]]. In ch. 18, she and Fernan try to shake their friend out of his apathy; in ch. 20, she brings paella to the wounded Alvaro and takes charge of changing his clothes.

## Three Bulls and Selezia (ch. 30)
In ch. 30, at the [[tri-byka|Three Bulls]] tavern, Lucille helps in the infirmary where beaten workers are treated. She is delighted by Alvaro and Fernan's reconciliation, says she warned her husband about the harm of secrecy, and sends him off to pack for Selezia without objection. Her view of that region is gloomy: the only thing sadder than a three-legged dog is a Selezian girl.

## Relationships
- [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]]—her husband; she leads him by the hand in politics.
- [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]]—a friend she cares for.
- [[mor|Mor]] and the reformists—allies with whom Lucille works in the infirmary.
