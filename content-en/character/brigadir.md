In the novel, “foreman” is a position rather than a name: it is the title of the senior slave in a dugout. One such man is depicted in detail—the foreman of the dugout to which [[red|Red]] is assigned in ch. 4. Other foremen are mentioned only in passing.

## The Position and Other Foremen
In the [[kamni|slave]] settlement, each dugout is run by a foreman. He occupies the best bunk beside the stove, receives a triple portion, assigns duties, and controls the distribution of food, but does not work as his subordinates do. A foreman remains a slave and “Guild property,” as the sentence in ch. 12 emphasizes. Foremen are mentioned in the plural in ch. 27, when the baron sends not only soldiers but also “trusted” slaves, foremen, and scribes to search for the fugitive.

In ch. 15, Red is given a separate dugout, and the scribes register him as a “foreman” (in the sense of the holder of the dwelling and its ration). This is merely an entry in the ledger: he has no subordinates.

## The Foreman of Red's Dugout (ch. 4–8)
In ch. 4, he is a ruddy, shaven brute trying on the boots taken from Red; Red strikes him in the face with his heel. The man orders four subordinates to beat the newcomer with shovels. Red defeats them all and presses a shovel blade to the foreman's throat. The conflict is laid to rest for the night.

But the foreman uses the rations to take revenge: Red receives less and less food. The other stones worship him, although he is as much a slave as they are. After the church service in ch. 8, he strikes Red with a stone, and his subordinates finish the beating. Red apologizes, but the next morning, when the foreman spits into his bowl, Red plunges the foreman’s face into boiling water and porridge, deals with the others, and institutes an equal division of food. The foreman is badly burned.

## Complaint and Fear (ch. 12)
The foreman complains to the fischmeister about the fight. Using a dictionary, [[joriksen|Joriksen]] pronounces Red's sentence for damaging Guild property: twenty lashes without salt. The foreman forbids anyone to feed the beaten man—only those who work are fed. Later, he himself uses gestures to ask whether Red really is dying and, on learning that he has about two months left, begins to fear him: the man has nothing to lose. From then on, Red eats at the common table.

## Enemy and Envious Rival (ch. 15–23)
When Red returns from his escape, the foreman sits in the dugout relishing thoughts of Red’s punishment. Red is then housed separately, and the foreman comes with [[siplyj|Hoarse]] to look for the mark of a noose on his neck. Red shows him his unscarred back: “You are a stone; I am a Catalian.” The foreman leaves empty-handed, then visits [[greta|Greta]] as a customer and stands on the porch while Red kisses her.

In ch. 19, he whispers something to Baron [[landau|Landau]] and asks him to crush the Catalian. When Red reaches water, the foreman strikes him on the head with a shovel and fills the pit with earth. In ch. 23, Lieutenant [[klyajn|Klein]] calls him “the foreman of the twenty-ninth dugout,” who hated Red, whispered with the baron, and returned on the morning after the collapse with a dirty shovel; the guards had been warned in advance to leave their posts. For a long time, Red does not know what became of him.

In ch. 27, Klein adds that Joriksen deliberately used Greta to convince the foreman that Red was insane, and he immediately informed the baron.

## Death (ch. 33)
In ch. 33, Landau declares the foreman the “beast's” first victim: he is guilty of attempting to destroy a schönbrun and kill a Guild slave. The foreman lunges at Red, knocks him down, and starts choking him, but Red stuns him with a chain and beats him to death. The novel does not explicitly establish that this is the same foreman, but the charge matches the actions of the foreman in ch. 19, and the text specifically refers to “the foreman.”
