Collected here are short lines that make the novel’s characters memorable. The source article reproduces each one verbatim, following the book’s spelling; they are presented here in English translation, with chapter numbers. Lines from the middle and end of the book appear in separate sections marked by chapter, to avoid revealing the course of events to readers who have not yet reached those passages.

## Alvaro da Silva
- “Don Alvaro da Silva—that sounds damned good!” (preface). The writer dreams of exchanging his silver maestro’s bracelet for a gold one, and imagines his future in those terms. See [[alvaro-da-silva]].
- “We do not need a well-fed, purring tomcat. We need a hungry wolf, ready for the hunt.” (ch. 3). Alvaro explains to his students why a novel’s protagonist should be poor and in need.

## Vincente Ramires
- “Me… no one but the cat, I’m afraid.” (ch. 1). In the dueling debate, the master of Law answers the question of who loves him, thereby proving that people are unequal even in love: the lonely are loved less than the famous. See [[vinsente-ramires]], [[duelnyj-spor-ramiresa]].

## Dietrich von Dort
- “You won’t have time to let me rot, Herr Staff Captain. I’m afraid you’ll be dead in seven seconds.” (ch. 2). Dietrich whispers this to the battery commander Zeidvist after seeing the future in the well. See [[ditrikh]], [[tri-zalpa]].

## Salvation
- “The water will save us! The Sower has already saved us, and the dear water will save us yet!” (ch. 2). The simple-minded stone slave explains his strange hope. See [[spasenie]].

## Red
- “I do not have three years. Not even a year. I am mortally ill.” (ch. 4). Red tells the master fisherman that he cannot wait years for a schönbrun to be found. See [[red]], [[joriksen]].
- “From now on, we eat equal shares. If you object, I’ll finish you off.” (ch. 8). Red restores order in the slaves’ dugout, where the foreman has been taking the best food for himself.
- “I believe in brains, fists, and the mirna—in precisely that order!” (ch. 12). Red tells Greta what he believes in; the mirna here is [[eliga|Eliga]]. See [[greta]].

## Olkha Lenz
- “You look troubled, Mr. Occupier.” (ch. 6). This is how the Ollandian woman opens a conversation with the Dangarian officer in an outdoor restaurant in Latsen. See [[olkha]].

## Wolfgang von Klein
- “Consider your discharge a reward for your heroic deed.” (ch. 6). The major of the Kaiser’s Claws removes Dietrich from service after questioning him. See [[klyajn]].

## Gabriel Deserey
- “Maestro, make your readers afraid!” (ch. 9). The general asks Alvaro to make fear of Dangar the book’s principal theme. See [[dezerej]].

## The stone acolyte
- “God’s tears have dried; ours will dry too.” (ch. 10). This is how a slave in a grey cassock asks the Sower’s churchgoers for alms. See [[seyatel]].

## Lines from the middle of the book (ch. 13–24)

### Diego Ingres
- “…there is no law against bringing severed fingers into someone else’s home.” (ch. 13). The alguacil explains to Alvaro why the threat sent to him is not considered a crime. See [[diego-ingres]].

### Fritjof Joriksen
- “…a schönbrun is not a shop, and I am not a merchant!” (ch. 14). The master turns down a petitioner who wants to order a miracle worth millions. See [[joriksen]], [[kolodets|schönbrun]].
- “Pah, what a tiny sprat you are.” (ch. 21). Joriksen comments on Dietrich’s requests, which amount to nothing more than a rank and exoneration.
- “Everything has its price. A miracle, all the more so.” (ch. 21). The master insists on collateral.

### Filippe di Berro
- “The highest power is power over oneself.” (ch. 16). The prim-allen of Art repeats to Alvaro the caption of a scandalous painting. See [[filippe]].
- “Then write the damned book!” (ch. 16). Filippe cuts short his argument with the writer.

### Carlos el Niña
- “The worst thing cannot happen twice.” (ch. 18). The pirate explains why he cannot shoot himself. See [[karlos]].

### Alvaro da Silva
- “Courage, señorita. A soldier has the right to be afraid; a writer does not.” (ch. 20). Alvaro identifies a writer’s most important quality.

### Beatriz
- “So I am your only friend?” (ch. 20). The student picks up on Alvaro’s casual remark. See [[beatris]].
- “An antagonist is a narrative role, not a moral judgment.” (ch. 22). Beatriz corrects her teacher as they analyze the case as a plot.

### Baron von Landau
- “A fischmeister knows how to fish. An oberfischmeister can take the catch with him.” (ch. 23). The baron instructs his subordinate. See [[landau]], [[fishmajster]].

## Lines from later chapters (ch. 28–31)

### The unknown woman, also known as Fatima
- “Darling, contrary to popular opinion, jealousy is not a sign of love.” (ch. 28). Fatima says this to Alvaro as he presses her about who the general is. See [[fatima]].

### Alvaro da Silva
- “I suppose it is my turn to use everyone now.” (ch. 30). Alvaro decides to stop being a pawn. See [[alvaro-da-silva]].

### Lucille Gereira
- “There is no sadder creature than a three-legged dog and a Selezian maiden.” (ch. 30). Lucille insists that her friends will find no adventures in Selezia. See [[lyusil]], [[seleziya]].

### Wolf Klein
- “A turtle glides across the surface of a lake, with a viper lying on its back.” (ch. 31). A parable about the major’s cooperation with Dietrich: if the viper bites, both will drown. See [[klyajn]].
