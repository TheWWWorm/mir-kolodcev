**The Herald** is a newspaper sold by newsboys on the streets of Laerta.

## The newspaper in the city (ch. 0, 3)
In the preface, boys shout along the boulevard: “Buy The Herald! Steam-powered machines displace obreros. Mass layoffs of workers in Laerta. A new technological marvel—Esperanza engines.” In ch. 3, the same cry announces a sensation in the [[palata-slova|Chamber of the Word]]: workers are challenging the factory owners to a debate.

## The Herald and the Reformists (ch. 20)
[[mor|Mor]] shows [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] The Herald’s leading article: the conflict is intensifying, the rebels threaten mass sabotage, the unemployed are supposedly preparing to destroy railway tracks, and readers are advised not to buy tickets. There is also an article about “a day in feudal times: how to survive without a steam engine.” According to Mor, the dons have paid the newspapermen to portray the workers as vicious idiots; the newspaper says nothing about cannons being fired at workers. Mor remarks that Alvaro is too educated and accustomed to believing newspapers, which is why he took her joke about a “plan” to destroy all the machines at face value. For more about these events, see [[stachka-v-laerte|“Obrero Unrest in Laerta”]].

## The Laerta Herald in Dangar (ch. 27)
In ch. 27, the newspaper is called the Laerta Herald; it is most likely the same newspaper, although the novel does not explicitly say so. In Dangar, Red learns that his portrait has been printed on the Laerta Herald’s front page. Lieutenant [[klyajn|Klein]], whom Red calls Wolf, shows him the issue to prove that he knows who Red really is and has told no one.

## Other newspapers in the novel
The novel also mentions other publications: “Sunday Laerta,” the weekly of the esfera of Knowledge, whose articles about creative people are approved by [[filippe|Filippe di Berro]] (ch. 16), and the Dangarian “Society Herald” (ch. 10). They are unrelated to The Herald.
