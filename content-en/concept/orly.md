Dangarian society is divided into three estates whose names come from the parable of the [[seyatel|Sower]]: eagles, hares, and [[kamni|stones]]. The parable connects the origin of each estate with its help to God in the field: the stones moved aside, the hares dug up the weeds, and the eagles exterminated the rodents. The same creatures are shown beside the Sower in icons. Stones are discussed in detail in a separate article; this one concerns the two upper estates.

## Eagles (ch. 2–33)
Eagles are Dangar’s nobility. They use the particle “von,” serve the kaiser as officers and officials, and own land together with slave stones. The storyline’s protagonist, Dietrich von Dort, is an eagle, but “landless and short of stones”—that is, poor and almost without slaves (ch. 2).

**Honor in place of a master.** Explaining to Olkha why an eagle cannot simply leave the service, Dietrich says that eagles are no freer than stones: stones are governed by their masters, and eagles by honor. Resignation would mean disgrace (ch. 6).

**Three paths.** Three possibilities are named for an eagle: living off the land while keeping stones; becoming an officer in the kaiser’s service; or entering government service. Dietrich, having no slaves, became an officer, and after his discharge (ch. 10) unsuccessfully seeks a civilian position. Serving under a hare is unthinkable to him, but most bookkeeping positions belong to hares: they own shops, pawnshops, and factories, while in the banks of noble barons, hired clerks from the hare estate run affairs. State departments hire on the recommendation of “His Nobleness.”

**Feeding.** At table, eagles observe the “feeding” ritual: after the meal, the nobles stand behind their stone servants and put the final spoonful into their mouths, saying “We care for you.” Dietrich explains to Olkha that this is a “victory of the stones,” won in the Wars of the Three Kingdoms, and that the eagles have no such custom for themselves (ch. 14).

**Greeting.** Nobles and officers greet one another with “A steady hand” (ch. 33).

## Hares (ch. 6, 10, 33)
Hares are free commoners: traders, clerks, bookkeepers, managers, and the owners of shops and factories. The author’s interview confirms that the hares are the middle class. In ch. 6, Dietrich explains that he took command in battle himself instead of handing it to the hare [[shpeer|Speer]]: in a difficult moment, the soldiers put greater trust in an eagle. By ch. 33, the former young scribe has lost his spots, filled out, and become, as Red puts it, if not an eagle, then a solid, well-fed hare.

The text uses “free person” separately from “hare,” meaning someone who is free as opposed to a slave. In the army, the only free man in Dietrich’s battery is the noncommissioned officer Gelb (ch. 2).

## Nobility and power (ch. 14, 17, 25, 27, 29)
- **Titles.** The text includes barons, counts, margraves, and princes, the last in Ollandia. The old von Eyring family is called ancient and powerful (ch. 25); Count [[lurd|Ludwig von Lurd]] is introduced as a deputy minister.
- **Eagles in the army.** The officer corps consists of eagles: they are “honorable” and embody strength and valor; the rank and file are stones (ch. 17). Major [[klyajn|Klein]] is an eagle in only the second generation (ch. 27).
- **The governorship.** According to Lurd, the governor in Ollandia will be an Ollandian noble connected by marriage to the clergy or military aristocracy; candidates close to the fischmeisters’ guild are unsuitable (ch. 25).
- **Reliability.** The empire sorts the nobility of conquered countries into the “reliable” and the “unsicher.” The reliable retain their lands and their people’s freedom; the unreliable lose their lands, and between twenty and seventy percent of their people are branded as slaves (ch. 29).

## The concept and interviews
According to the author, when conquering Ollandia, the empire arranges its population according to its own system: some peasants are enslaved as stones, the middle class becomes hares, and the nobles become eagles. Ollandia has a nobility, bourgeoisie, and peasants, but no slavery, which makes them easy to assimilate. For more on the countries, see [[dangar]], [[ollandiya]].
