The novel’s world can be called an age of steam and gunpowder. Steam engines, revolvers, and railways coexist with hussars’ sabers, slavery, and manual labor. In an interview, the author said that events take place in a notional “X century” in terms of technology: steam engines, cannons, and slavery exist at the same time. The Catalians in the novel consider themselves the most advanced, and this is partly justified.

## Steam-powered machinery and obreros (ch. 0–20)
Steam-powered machinery is at the center of social conflict in Laerta. In the preface (ch. 0), newspaper boys announce that machines are displacing [[obrero|obreros]] and that mass layoffs are taking place in the capital. According to Fernan, the workers hate the machines: they pour water on the coal and throw shoes into the gears; on one occasion, the [[reformisty|Reformists]] blocked the railway with a crowd of three thousand people (ch. 13). The factory owners, particularly [[kristobal-maduro|Maduro]], respond with cannons: blanks are fired at workers during a demonstration, and Fernan and [[lyusil|Lucille]] complain that Fatima’s lawyers will justify even this (ch. 11, 13).

[[mor|Mor]] shows that technology has another side: she herself invented the pressure condenser that made a compact steam engine possible and, together with dismissed workers, built the Esperanza factory in [[brega|Brega]], a center of heavy industry with coal mines, the River Monho, and black rain (ch. 20). Mor declares that her “plan”—to close ten or so factories, destroy steam-powered machinery, and ban steam mechanisms by law, leaving only the railways—was a joke aimed at Alvaro’s habit of believing the newspapers (ch. 20).

## Steam transport (ch. 0–34)
- **Steam “rattletraps” and carriages.** Smoking self-propelled vehicles already travel along Laerta’s boulevards, and ladies wrinkle their noses at the fumes (ch. 0). Horse-drawn carriages, light carts, and stagecoaches still share the streets with them.
- **Esperanza.** The compact steam engine from Mor’s factory powers [[beatris|Beatriz’s]] red lacquered car. More details are in [[esperantsa|“Esperanza”]].
- **Cherrato 3-5.** In ch. 11, Beatriz notices a green car of this make in a hotel courtyard. [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] decides that someone is spying on them and later suspects the car’s owner, a grey-haired sixty-year-old man six and a quarter feet tall. In ch. 20, Beatriz uses the number plate to establish that the Cherrato belongs to an ordinary hired driver, and the spy proves imaginary.
- **Steam locomotives.** Iturbe-Clemento is the company whose powerful locomotives pull the express train northwest from Catalia’s capital (ch. 4). Catalia also sells them to Muaris, where the sultan is building railways across the desert (ch. 17). The Laerta–Seleza line passes through the small town of [[tres-molinos|Tres Molinos]] (ch. 32).

## Dangar’s railways (ch. 2–29)
[[dangar|Dangar]] also has railways: a line with a small station, coal stores, and water tanks runs near [[ditrikh|Dietrich’s]] position on the Ollandian hill (ch. 2); Red travels to penal servitude “by ship and railway carriage” (ch. 4); and Dietrich and Joriksen ride the same train, which has compartments, a dining car, and a conductor (ch. 29). At Baroness Walbrecht’s fishing session in ch. 14, a man talking to Prince [[lents|Lenz]] asks how many marks he would charge per verst of track across his land. In ch. 28, General [[dezerej|Deserey]] warns about the double-track railway Dangar is building into [[ollandiya|Ollandia]]: once it opens, another three fully supplied armies can be moved there.

## Firearms
- **Catalia’s revolvers.** Alvaro carries a six-shot revolver. During his dispute with Ramires, he challenges his opponent to a revolver duel, but Ramires refuses: “true-born Catalians are people of the word” (ch. 1). In ch. 9, he shows General [[dezerej|Deserey]] a repeating revolver with a rifled barrel and self-contained cartridges: six shots in a few seconds, with at least two hitting a target the size of an orange. The “stones” in Dangarian infantry carry smoothbore muskets that fire one and a half times a minute. The guard and the alguacil carry carbines and revolvers (ch. 13).
- **Mardur.** A compact Catalian pistol of 0.29 caliber, suitable for concealed carry (ch. 15). The [[klyajn|Black Falcon]] carries one, and its very appearance makes the dying Red nostalgic: he calls the Mardur a piece of perfection made only in Catalia. Red had also handled a Mardur in the past (ch. 12). Later, the same pistol is described in Lieutenant [[klyajn|Klein’s]] possession, with a word engraved on its grip; Red asks for it as his last wish, but is refused because the pistol was a gift (ch. 23, 27).
- **A bolt-action rifle.** In ch. 34, the second killer has a rifle that Alvaro calls “a marvel of military engineering.” An ordinary person could not buy such a new weapon. From this, and from Salvador’s use of the word “schönbrun,” Alvaro concludes that the killer is a Dangarian spy, a “Kaiser’s Claw.”
- **Dangarian muskets and rifles.** Stone soldiers carry smoothbore muskets (ch. 2, 9). Contrary to custom, the Ollandian hussars take rifles into battle slung over their shoulders (ch. 2).
- **Flintlock guns.** The hunter [[yakka|Yakka’s]] double-barreled flintlock with silver decoration is old but beautiful; it has a name of its own, [[ejkha|Eikha]]. [[karlos|Carlos el Niña]] has a double-barreled flintlock pistol, which he aims at his guests, inviting them to choose a barrel (ch. 7).

## Cannons and grenades
- **Artillery.** The Dangarian battery in ch. 2 fires six-pound cannonballs and canister shot. At the firing range in ch. 9, the Catalian battery loads shrapnel shells, gunners turn handles on the carriages, and an observer uses an optical instrument to adjust fire. The observation post is equipped with binoculars whose lenses have graduations for estimating distance.
- **Hand grenades.** A sentry in Solanta’s coastal fort throws a grenade, and the navigator Rodrigo da Vira covers it with his body (ch. 22). In ch. 27, Red makes an improvised grenade and throws it into a cart carrying soldiers. In ch. 34, a killer throws a grenade into the castle cellar, and the explosion stuns the protagonists.

## Flying ships (ch. 10, 17)
Dangar has “[[samolet|airships]],” flying ships. Mari tells how, on the first day of the gehai celebration, the patriarch’s airship flew over their house, covered in gold and banners, with bells and a choir, while little Heinz pointed at it. Another, a three-masted vessel as enormous as a ship of the line, without sails or lights but carrying the Life Guards’ standard with the Sower’s face and with its gunports open, glides over the city at night. Mari tells her husband about the first flight; Dietrich sees the second himself.

According to Count [[lurd|Ludwig von Lurd]], Catalia will also be able to build several airships thanks to the flying brante trees (ch. 17). They grow on Solanta. Airships also occur in Alvaro’s joke (ch. 13): he will not visit Maduro on foot, on horseback, in a steam carriage, by airship, or by ship.

## Smaller devices
- **A listening tube.** Baron [[landau|Landau]] listens to Red’s chest with such a tube: “in distant lands,” a doctor used such a device to discover that Red was dying (ch. 15).
- **Water supply.** Tres Molinos has piped water, windmills, and a water tower (ch. 32).
- **An ice store.** The finger sent to Alvaro is kept in the ice store in his cellar (ch. 18).
- **Sewing machines.** Catalia exports them; Muaris supplies silk for them (ch. 17).
- **Gas lighting.** A gas floor lamp stands in Fatima’s bedroom (ch. 28).
- **A steam engine in a painting.** At the “Philosophy of Horror” exhibition, the mirn [[darredzho|Darreggio]] inserts a miniature steam engine into his own body. The serpent Ikhtrodan is made from steamship hulls (ch. 16).
