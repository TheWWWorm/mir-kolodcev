The First Judge is the figure on whom Catalia’s organization rests. The time before him was the age of ancient gods and noble hidalgos; after him came the age of law, debates, and the High Table. His name is constantly heard in the novel: debates follow his code, esferas live by his principles, and his statue greets visitors to the Great Scroll. Yet the reader does not see the First Judge himself: knowledge of him is assembled from the traces he left behind.

## What is known (ch. 1–32)
- **Founder of Catalia.** Before him, the country consisted of two kingdoms, Catilez and Laertar. They united, “recognizing the wisdom of the First Judge,” and became Upper and Lower Catalia, later Beautiful and Mighty Catalia (ch. 9).
- **Creator of the order.** The [[vysokij-stol|High Table]] has governed the country since the First Judge’s time (ch. 1). The First Judge’s code determines who must take part in a [[duelnyj-spor|dueling debate]]: the arbiter places a hand on it when taking the oath. In his debate with Ramires, Alvaro invokes this principle with the words “Every person has an equal opportunity to defend their rights through a dueling debate” (ch. 1).
- **Creator of the Scroll.** The results of all debates since the First Judge’s time are recorded on the [[svitok|Great Scroll]]. In the Hall of the Scroll, the First Judge’s principles are carved into marble slabs: “Skill is the key,” “What belongs to everyone belongs to no one,” “Honor matters more than morality,” and “One equals seven” (ch. 22). The last formula costs Alvaro dearly: Ramires wins the debate by defending this very phrase.
- **Taboo.** Murder on [[belaya-zemlya|white land]] is considered the gravest violation of what is sacred: the killer “spits in the First Judge’s face” (ch. 9).
- **Limits of authority.** According to Catalian tradition, the First Judge’s laws apply only on land; at sea, the Ancient Lords still rule (ch. 15).
- **The end of paganism.** After the First Judge’s arrival, the temple of the Ancient Lords near Tres Molinos was closed; the locals still use this temple as the reference point for the cardinal directions (ch. 32).
- **Belief.** Red believes “in the First Judge and the mirna Eliga,” in his own mind and fists (ch. 8): a formula that places law and chance side by side.

## The concept and interviews
According to the author, the First Judge founded Catalia’s modern system, created dueling debates, and made the Great Scroll. Before him, the Catalians had never had monotheism: there had been “creepy Lovecraftian paganism” with sea monsters, which the First Judge abolished. The author says that the First Judge’s influence on the Catalian worldview is greater than that of the ancient gods; more will be revealed about him in the future (interview iv1).
