**The Author's Words** collects Roman Surzhikov's statements about his novel [[sledy-na-vode|Traces on the Water]] and the World of Wells from two reader Q&A livestreams. These are the author's comments, not the text of the book: sometimes he expands on things the novel mentions in passing, and sometimes he explicitly refuses to reveal anything. Every statement below conveys the author's words (“according to the author”). Remarks and guesses by the host and viewers are either excluded or identified as such; jokes are marked separately. Anything that touches on the plot is hidden beneath headings with chapter numbers.

## Sources
Both recordings survive only as machine-generated transcripts (tactiq), so names and some words are distorted; doubtful passages are qualified below.

- **“Livestream with Writer Roman Surzhikov”** ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch/qXDc_0Hh2U4)). Hosted by Vasil and broadcast simultaneously on Twitch and YouTube. The date, **7 January 2026**, has been reconstructed from context: host and author wish one another a merry Christmas, and the host says the stream was arranged for the author's birthday. It lasts about two hours and twenty minutes. There is little discussion of Traces on the Water: the author names it among his current projects, explains how he constructs characters, and discusses his plans for 2026.
- **“Discussing the Novel Traces on the Water, the Polari Series, and Much More with Writer Roman Surzhikov”** ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch/fWYbDxa7AMc)). Again hosted by Vasil. About two hours and nine minutes long. The author does not give an exact date; he says publication of the novel will finish “by Lenin's birthday,” around 22 April, and refers to the January stream as the previous one. The recording is therefore dated to spring 2026, approximately March–April. This is the main source: almost the entire conversation concerns Traces on the Water and the World of Wells.

In both cases, questions came from the author's Telegram channel, the chat, and viewer donations. Below, the sources are distinguished as the “January stream” and the “spring stream.”

## The Novel's Conception
- According to the author, his ambitious goal is to create **at least two fantasy worlds**: most writers, he says, are remembered as the creators of one world. Polari was finished about two years earlier, and he called Traces on the Water the first novel of his second world—the World of Wells (spring stream).
- The author will not return to the Polari series until he feels he has created another world; a return is “possible, but not in the next few years” (January stream). He describes the World of Wells as taking priority over Polari.
- He deliberately chose creativity as a theme: creative protagonists are rare in fantasy, and he wanted to explore the subject through a novel (spring stream). Of the three main protagonists, one is an artist and the other two are not.
- His principle for introducing the characters: according to the author, each main protagonist of Traces on the Water is presented in a different form. One is introduced through a denunciation written against him, another through his own “portrait” in memoirs he supposedly writes afterward, and the third in yet another way. The author says he enjoys playing with form, but his priorities are a plot the reader can understand first, then the expressive force of feelings and ideas, and originality only afterward (January stream). He did not specify which character is presented in which way.
- All three protagonists are men. In January, the author admitted feeling “a little uneasy” and regretting that he had not made one of them a woman: he usually wants at least one of three protagonists to be female (January stream).
- When a viewer complained that everyone in his books has bad relationships, the author replied that he likes depicting good, genuine love, and added that Traces on the Water “even has a married couple who, in my view, are very good together.” He did not name the couple; the novel's principal married couple is [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]] and [[lyusil|Lucille Gereira]] (January stream).
- As for readers, the author advises them not to suffer through a book or finish it if they dislike its opening pages; he himself follows a “read fifty pages” rule.

## The World and Its Structure
- **Technology and period.** Events take place in a notional “X century”: steam engines, cannons, and slavery coexist in this world (spring stream). See [[tekhnika|Technology]].
- **Catalia** is “the triumph of materialism.” For most of its people, wells are more of a curiosity. According to the author, Catalians are individualists, each concerned with personal success, and the esferas are more a federation than parts of a state as we know it. He promised to discuss the economic interaction between esferas “separately.” He compared the Catalian dream to the American dream: rising from obrero to maestro and don through one's own work (the transcript renders the word as “arera”). See [[kataliya|Catalia]], [[esfera|Esferas]].
- **Catalia's religion.** The Catalians never had monotheism. There was “rather creepy paganism of a Lovecraftian kind,” with sea monsters; then the First Judge came and abolished it. Today, people believe in gods much as we believe in muses: there are no icons or prayers. The ancient gods live in the sea and do not intervene in life; they are unimportant to the plot and provide atmosphere. According to the author, the First Judge's influence on the Catalian worldview is far stronger. See [[drevnie|Ancients]], [[pervyj-sudya|First Judge]].
- **Mirnas.** They are patrons of every field of activity, not only art: engineering and agriculture have their own. They may be male or female; people appeal to them for inspiration, as to muses. They are not gods: there is no evidence that mirnas existed, and they are not worshipped. The author called them “muses or angels” and allowed that they might turn out to be nothing but myth. See [[mirny|Mirnas]].
- **Rituals.** Catalians rejoice at weddings, divorces, and, in his words, “in a good way,” even funerals: their philosophy is to find something positive in every event. Divorce leads to something good, or it would not happen. At funerals, people set up a small tree and tie ribbons to it: light or blue ribbons signify a fond memory—a “beautiful, bright trace”—while red or black ones signify dislike. The author clarified that he wanted to portray this philosophy, not ridicule the custom. He added that blue ribbons do not mean the deceased never did anything wrong. See [[sinkho|Sinkho]].
- **Dangar** is a slave-owning empire, multinational and constantly expanding. According to the author, monotheistic faith and wells are the two foundations of Dangarian philosophy and mysticism, and Dangar's philosophy contains “more profound ideas.” The host described the service in which stones are fed by the hands of free people as a privilege won by the stones; the author confirmed that they “fought for and won” this privilege, but deferred the history of the civil war to later books. Dangar, he added, is a tightly interconnected system: the upper estates care for the stones, while almost all goods are produced by the stones' hands, and “free the stones, and everything will fall apart.” See [[dangar|Dangar]], [[kamni|Stones]], [[seyatel|Sower]].
- **Assimilation.** The empire assimilates conquered peoples. Catalia would be difficult to digest, which makes Dangarian generals pessimistic about war with it, while the rulers of both sides fear such a war. Ollandia, spelled “Olandia” in the transcript, is easy to assimilate: it has classes—nobility, bourgeoisie, peasantry—but no slavery, so some peasants are turned into stones, the middle class into hares, and the nobles into eagles. See [[ollandiya|Ollandia]], [[orly|Eagles and Hares]].
- **Map.** Alexey Safin drew a large map of the World of Wells' three continents. By the spring stream, only the fragment containing the first book's setting had been published; the author promised to expand the map.

## Countries and Their Models
For details, see [[prototipy|Models and Influences]].

- According to the author, Catalian is Spanish and Dangarian is German. Catalia's style, naming system, and clothing are “copied” from Spain, while Dangar's ranks, clothing, and Kaiser come from Germany. But this “copying” is superficial. The author says one cannot claim “Catalia is Spain and Dangar is Prussia”; both countries embody ideas and principles of statehood. Catalia's system is “completely unlike” Spain's; he describes a conception of a state over which he “laid a skin” of Spanish style (spring stream).
- The host observed that Ollandia resembles Poland and the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth; the author agreed: “in places, yes, there are similarities,” and its fate is sad too. Asked whether Ollandian is Polish, he answered “more or less,” adding that he had not thought about it: he had never needed to write anything in Ollandian. Ollandian names are Slavic; in general, he selects names so that they sound different from one another. Prince Wladyslaw's name is Slavic partly so that he can easily be distinguished from Dangarian nobles in Dangar's capital. This does not mean the character was modeled on a real prince. See [[yazyki|Languages]].
- On Spanish: the author began learning it while conceiving this world; he reads at the level of Wikipedia articles, mainly studies through dictionaries, and focuses more on reading. He studied German systematically at university to Mittelstufe II level; readers sometimes point out mistakes in his Dangarian words.

## Characters
- **Alvaro da Silva.** The author acknowledges that Alvaro is to some extent a projection of himself, and that he agrees with most of what Alvaro says—for example, that people who can write rarely teach others. At the same time, he warns beginning writers against making a protagonist resemble themselves: it is a trap involving “Mary Sue” characters and poor self-knowledge. He explains his choice of a writer protagonist through his interest in creativity and his desire for a challenge. See [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]].
- **Beatriz.** The author called her a clever straight-A student like Hermione Granger: she knows the theory but has not yet written a single book. As for how she will fare in practice, “read the next novels of the World of Wells.” “Golden Quill” is Beatriz's nickname in the novel; the host also calls her that in the stream. See [[beatris|Beatriz]].
- **Deserey.** The model for the general's behavior is a military acquaintance of the author: he would send five pages of criticism about Polari—the battles were described incorrectly, the swords were the wrong length—and finish by saying it was “really great.” The author says he thinks of him when writing the general. He presented this as a humorous anecdote. See [[dezerej|Gabriel Deserey]].
- **Dangarians.** The author does not consider it necessary to “root for” a state: his sympathies go to people. Among the Dangarians, he likes Master Joriksen, rendered as “Yurikson” in the transcript; a count, identified by the host as Count von Lurd, though the transcript badly distorts the word; Katrina; and Dietrich “to a lesser degree, but him too.” The transcript is inaccurate here, so the count's surname is not certain. See [[joriksen|Joriksen]], [[ditrikh|Dietrich]].
- **Yako**, according to the author, is a northern hunter, the fischmeister's right hand, and “not quite an overseer.” He was conceived as a representative of another people; the author later “grew fond of” him and developed the character. See [[yakka|Yakka]].
- **Red** is a slave, and the author compared his position in Dangar to that of a concentration-camp prisoner. This explains readers' negative attitude toward Dangar.
- **The Kaiser, patriarch, and chief magnus** interact with one another, but court affairs are absent from Traces: the author deferred them to the second book. See [[kajzer|Kaiser]], [[fishmajster|Fischmeister]].

### Storylines and Chronology (ch. 4)
The author confirmed that Red's storyline takes place before Dietrich's and Alvaro's, and that the storylines, offset in time, will “merge in a certain way.” He called the details a spoiler. He neither confirmed nor denied the host's time-travel hypothesis, saying only that it would become clear “as new chapters come out.”

### Red's Name (ch. 34)
The host ventured a guess at the protagonist's real name; the author only confirmed that it could be guessed by someone who knows Spanish, and that it “definitely must not” be revealed. In the book, the name is connected to [[red|Rodrigo da Vira's]] storyline: see [[red|Red]].

### A Book Within a Book (ch. 34)
In response to a viewer's question, the author confirmed that Alvaro really is writing a book called Traces on the Water, “but why do you ask?” (January stream). The host called the device “a book within a book”; that is his definition. See also [[roman-surzhikov|Roman Surzhikov]].

## What the Author Does Not Reveal
- Red's real name (see above), the mechanism by which the storylines “merge,” and the reason for slavery in Dangar: asked whether slavery had existed in Dangar from the beginning, the author declined to answer because it would be a spoiler.
- The history of how Dangar's and Catalia's systems developed, including the stones' civil war, is promised later, in subsequent books.
- There will be “more” about the Great Scroll and the First Judge later.
- Beatriz, Dangarian court affairs, and the relationships among the Kaiser, patriarch, and magnus are reserved for the second book.
- The author gave neither a schedule nor a title for the second book.

## The Continuation and the Spiral of Worlds
- After Traces, the author plans to return to the World of Wells and “begin the next book” after working on the novel about Unlucky Chris (spring stream). In January, he was writing Traces and Chris in parallel: he began Chris in early 2025, switched to the World of Wells, grew a little tired, and returned to Chris.
- He set himself a challenge for 2026: write 12 pages a week, around 600 a year; by the spring stream, he had not missed a single week.
- **The spiral.** The spiral is Polari's sacred symbol. The author describes his books as taking place in one metaverse, on different turns of a spiral: Polari occupies one turn, the world of Unlucky Chris another, and the World of Wells another turn of that same spiral. Physical passage from one world to another is impossible. A “world of bards” was named as a possible future world. See [[otsylki-k-polari|References to Polari]], [[mir-kolodtsev|World of Wells]].
- The final parts of Polari are still being written: the author is working on the concluding book Fantasies and an interlude called The Superfluous Ones in the transcript.

## How the Author Wrote the Novel
- The author does not write drafts: he first thinks through the plot in his head, writes a “final version” immediately, reads it aloud once—to Dara, someone close to him, according to the transcript—and revises it. He writes for no more than five hours a day (January stream).
- Preparation proceeds from an image—a world, person, or situation—to development, discussion, conflict, and plot; he begins writing when he can see the entire conflict from beginning to resolution.
- He writes to undemanding music without words, naming Enigma, Dagda, and Dead Can Dance, listening to one album ten times over (spring stream). This applies to his work in general, not specifically to Traces.
- He discussed the scene in which Alvaro attends an autopsy with AI as a “doctor friend” to check its realism. He says he writes the text and the details of the world himself; AI helps with illustrations for posts, translations, and background information on professions.
- He sends the text to a proofreader only before a print edition; for websites, he proofreads it himself.
- After finishing Traces, he wanted to continue writing about the same characters, and he spent about a week “in a daze” while switching projects.

## Publication and Audiobook
- The novel is finished; chapters were released by subscription. Publication ends around 22 April; releases are on Fridays and holidays, usually in the morning, with two chapters a week over the coming month. Readers who “feared an ongoing work,” unfinished in the manner of Martin's, can read without concern. The author accidentally failed to post one chapter on Litspace—a joke about forgetfulness.
- Platforms: Litspace, Author.Today, rendered as “After Today” in the transcript, and Litnet, where the author had recently opened a page. Typos remain in the text, and he welcomes corrections.
- Kirill Golovin is narrating the book; the audio edition is expected “before the end of spring.”
- According to the author, the print edition will not appear soon: work on Fantasies must finish first, followed by the typesetting and publication of Traces.
- The author judged Traces more dynamic and concentrated than Arrow, Coin, Spark. His advice to Polari readers is: “read Traces on the Water.”
