A fischmeister, literally “fish-master,” is a Dangarian master who “angles” in Beautiful Wells ([[kolodets|schönbruns]]). These are key figures in the novel: Fischmeister [[joriksen|Fritjof Joriksen]] devises the experiment involving the Catalian [[red|Red]], [[landau|Baron Landau]] runs the settlement of slave diggers, and Magnus [[amir-sedoj|Amir the Grey]] investigates the settlement’s destruction. The profession is organized into the Fishermen’s Guild ([[gildiya-rybakov]]); Count [[lurd|Ludwig von Lurd]] calls it one of Dangar’s three centers of influence, alongside the emperor and the patriarch (ch. 17).

## Appearance and habits (ch. 4, 14)
Joriksen first appears in the manor’s study wearing a purple cloak of rich fabric, fastened with a silver catfish-shaped brooch, and a square cap on his head. His clock’s pendulum is a little fish. At the fishing session in Hofstadt, he wears a white cloak and a white tricorn edged in gold; beneath the cloak is a foreign naval uniform with numerous pockets. His assistant [[viglaf|Wiglaf]] has an anchor tattoo. The craft itself is emphatically nautical: the master checks the date, time, and weather and counts the people “aboard,” as on a ship.

## What a fischmeister does (ch. 14, 21, 25)
- Conducts fishing sessions for well owners; admission may be charged, and the clients may be noble guests.
- Determines a well’s temperament: each has its own whims, there are no formulas, and intuition and experience are required. Getting to know a documented well takes one to five days; a new one takes months.
- Charges a fee: Joriksen quotes 500 silver marks for a day of getting to know a well, and the same for a day of fishing. He works only with well owners, and poor people do not own wells (ch. 14, 21).
- Maintains an atlas of all known wells and their landowners; Otto must know it by heart (ch. 21).
- Helps people rid themselves of “heartache”: at a fishing session, guests write down a wish, and the master warns that they might mistakenly lose a love they need.
- Knows the dangers of dark wells (see [[temnyj-shyonbrun]]); in ch. 31, Otto takes a silver flask so that he can look safely into a well.
- Supervises digging, selecting Zustands for the slaves ([[tsushtand]]).

## Ranks (ch. 14–33)
The text suggests the following hierarchy:

1. **Apprentice.** Begins with menial work; some serve for seven years as scribes, porters, and diggers (ch. 29). Otto has been Joriksen’s apprentice “since last winter.”
2. **Fischmeister (master fisherman).** A master of the first rank, such as Joriksen: “the best fisherman of his generation,” he studied under the Grey, but is not yet an oberfischmeister (ch. 21).
3. **Oberfischmeister.** There are few of these. As Landau explains, a fischmeister can fish, while an oberfischmeister can “take the catch with him”—that is, direct a well’s effects onto people (ch. 23). Landau and [[flammel|Flammel]] are named in the book.
4. **Magnus.** Heads the Order of Fishermen and is considered the “third man” in Dangar; Amir the Grey wears an azure frock coat with gold stars (ch. 29).

The Dangarian greeting is “A steady hand” (ch. 33); a magnus is addressed as “Your Wisdom.”

## Dunkwald (ch. 27, 33)
For half a century, the guild tried to create a schönbrun through slave labor in the settlement of [[dunkvald|Dunkwald]]. In earlier days, its best masters and the kaiser’s agents went there; later, it began sending the unlucky and low-born for ten-year terms. That is how the young Joriksen ended up in the settlement; according to him, the lower-ranking fishermen sent there serve for ten years (ch. 33).

## Relations with the state and church (ch. 14–31)
In ch. 17, Count von Lurd names three centers of influence: the emperor; the patriarch, who influences the stones through the church and the Sower; and the guild’s magnus, whose fishermen “sell miracles” to the new, factory-owning nobility. The kaiser’s secret service, the [[kogti-kajzera|Claws]], watches them: the agent [[klyajn|Klein]] imposes tasks of his own on the fishermen, including a search for a “schönbrun of victory” for the army (ch. 31). On learning of Dunkwald’s destruction, Magnus the Grey takes charge of the case (ch. 29).

## The concept
In interviews, the author said that Dangar is an empire where wells, alongside monotheism, form the foundation of philosophy and mysticism. The fishermen are the people who turn this mysticism into a craft.
