“World of Wells” is the name the author [[roman-surzhikov|Roman Surzhikov]] gives the world in which his novel [[sledy-na-vode|Traces on the Water]] takes place. It is his second universe after the Polari series: its technology is steam-powered, power rests with the word and slavery rather than kings, and hidden in the earth are wells from which a miracle can be “fished.” According to the author, the world has three continents, but a detailed map has so far been drawn only for the region in which the first book takes place.

The novel has three main protagonists, each living in his own country: the writer [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]] in Catalian Laerta, the Dangarian artilleryman [[ditrikh|Dietrich von Dort]], and the Catalian [[red|Red]], who has fallen into Dangarian bondage. This article is a guide to the world in which they meet. Definitions of individual terms are collected in the [[glossarij|glossary]], and the characters' words on the [[tsitaty|quotations]] page.

## Map of the World
The author mentioned three continents in conversation without naming them, while the map, drawn by Alexey Safin, currently shows only part of the world. The principal lands in the book are:
- **Catalia**—Alvaro's and Red's homeland; its capital is Laerta.
- **Dangar**—an empire far to the west, weeks of travel from Laerta; its capital is Hofstadt.
- **Ollandia** lies between them: the Grand Principality of Ollandia. Farther along the Khursla River lies Mergana, beyond which begin Dangar's shores on the Kobold Sea.
- **The Muari Emirates (Muaris)**—a continent under one god, divided into emirates; to the north it is washed by the Miriam Sea, in which the island of **Solanta** lies.

The route from Laerta to Dangar is described in Chapter 4 as a journey for the reader: the railway crosses the Dariney Range and White Bone Pass, Selezia, industrial Brega, the steppes of Kabarda, and the border fortress of Indra. Ollandia begins beyond the marshes, and there the rails end at Ginarva. Travelers then take a steamship along the Khursla through Mergana and across the Kobold Sea to a Dangarian port. The train travels west for more than five weeks; in winter, trains do not run at all because the water in their tanks freezes. At the final station, the rails end, and the journey continues along a forest clearing. See [[brega]], [[seleziya]], [[indra]], [[ginarva]], [[khursla]].

## Catalia
[[kataliya|Catalia]] is the country of “people of the word.” Its native inhabitants take pride in resolving conflicts with arguments rather than bullets. Officially, their society is founded on the idea of the First Judge, who, according to tradition, abolished the old paganism and gave the country its law ([[pervyj-sudya]]). The author compares Catalia to “the triumph of materialism”: people believe in gods much as we believe in muses, while personal success matters more. Catalia has a Spanish stylistic flavor, but, as the author emphasized, “Catalia is not Spain.”

A mountain range divides the country in two: Mighty Catalia in the northeast, with mines and factories, and Beautiful Catalia in the south, with the capital, coast, and theaters. The kingdoms of Catilez and Laertar once existed here; the Valentian dynasty that ruled Laertar fell long ago.

### Esferas and the High Table
Catalia is not a state “as we understand it,” but more a federation of [[esfera|esferas]]: eleven professional estates. The three largest are Craft (later Production), Agriculture, and Service; the eight smaller ones comprise Art, Law, Knowledge, Shadow, Medicine, Finance, War, and Transport. Each esfera governs itself and is represented by exactly three [[alleny|allens]] at the [[vysokij-stol|High Table]], regardless of its size. Consequently, the voice of a peasant, whose esfera numbers in the millions, carries dozens of times less weight than that of an artist, whose esfera has around a hundred thousand members. This inequality gave rise to the [[reformisty|reformist]] movement. The supremo presides over the Table. Membership in an esfera and rank within it are shown by a [[braslet|bracelet]], awarded after a dueling debate in the [[palata-slova|Chamber of the Word]]. The lowest rank is [[obrero|obrero]], a maestro has a silver bracelet, and a gold one is the dream of people like Alvaro.

### Law and Land
The main means of resolving disputes is the [[duelnyj-spor|dueling debate]]: opponents compete in argument before an arbiter and a jury. The result is entered in the [[svitok|Great Scroll]], which preserves “Truth.” Catalia has no taxes, trade is duty-free, any voluntary transaction is legal, and slavery is forbidden. Crimes are governed by the “color of the land”: on [[belaya-zemlya|white land]], there are no crimes and no locks; on gray land, fighting and theft are allowed but killing is not; on black land, everything is permitted. Order is maintained by alguacils of the esfera of Law and guards from the esfera of [[teni|Shadow]].

### The Capital and the Country
[[laerta|Laerta]] is divided into eleven districts, one for each esfera. It contains the Chamber of the Word, the [[grand-opera|Grand Opera]], the [[galereya-eliste|Eliste Gallery]], the [[akademiya-iskusstv|Academy of Arts]], the pirate district known as the [[staryj-port|Old Port]], and the [[tri-byka|Three Bulls]] tavern, where discontented obreros gather. Thirty miles from the capital stands the fortress and transport hub of [[ortiyar|Ortiyar]], “Laerta's shield”; industrial [[brega|Brega]] lies to the north and rice-growing [[seleziya|Selezia]] to the west.

## Dangar
[[dangar|Dangar]] is a slave-owning empire. The author describes it as a state set in a notional “X century,” where steam engines, cannons, and slavery coexist; its style is German and Prussian, but “Dangar is not Prussia.” The Kaiser stands at its head, with two other centers of power beside him: the patriarch of the Sower's church and the magnus of the Fishermen’s Guild. The author said he would describe the relationships among these three in greater detail in the next book set in this world.

Society has three layers: [[orly|eagles]]—nobles who serve out of honor—hares, comprising traders and people in service, and [[kamni|stones]], or Steins—slaves. Stones wear a stone on a chain around their necks as a sign of the Sower's love, do almost all the menial labor, and form the army's rank and file. In the church's parable, each estate has its own role in the Sower's planting: the stones moved aside, the hares dug up the weeds, and the eagles exterminated the rodents. Free people feed slaves porridge in church with the formula “Sorge um dich” (“I care for you”), and this is considered a privilege of the stones. The capital is [[khofshtadt|Hofstadt]] on the Wunde River, where the palace and ministries stand in the Schlissel district. The [[kogti-kajzera|Kaiser’s Claws]] gather the information the empire needs. Dangar wages war: it has conquered Chinkhar and is now invading Ollandia, building railways behind its advancing army. See [[olandskaya-vojna]].

## Ollandia and Its Neighbors
[[ollandiya|Ollandia]] is a country of proud princes, hussars, and orchestras. The author said its style and fate resemble Poland's; its names are Slavic. It has no industry of its own—all its locomotives come from Catalia. There is no slavery in Ollandia, and the country is divided into a northern region of shepherds and a southern region of wealthy principalities. Its main cities are [[plyutva|Plyutva]], [[latsen|Latsen]], [[mershants|Mershants]], and [[bargot|Bargot]].

[[mergana|Mergana]] is famous for its university and white chocolate; the [[muaris|Muari Emirates]] for their one god and their power, but not their unity. In recent years, the island of [[solanta|Solanta]] has become a base for the Catalians, who concluded a treaty with it and gained the ability to purchase a raw material that provides lift—brante trees. See [[solantijskaya-ekspeditsiya]].

## Wells
A well containing a miracle is the world's central mystery. In Dangarian, it is called a [[kolodets|schönbrun]], a “Beautiful Well.” Inside is white mist, beneath which another world of monsters can be seen. Ordinary wells are simply water-producing brunnens. For most Catalians, whom the author called materialists, wells are an amusing curiosity; for Dangarians, together with the one god, they form the foundation of religion and philosophy. All known schönbruns are entered in the [[gildiya-rybakov|Fishermen’s Guild]] atlas, and only [[fishmajster|fischmeisters]] know how to “fish” in them. Fishing requires an exact date, time, and number of people; its result is a “miracle,” most commonly relief from heartache. There are light wells that grant miracles, and dangerous [[temnyj-shyonbrun|dark]] ones. Slaves sent to dig are tormented with special conditions called [[tsushtand|Zustands]], in the hope of discovering a way to create a schönbrun. See [[kolodets]], [[rybalka-valbrekht]].

## Beliefs
- **Dangar:** monotheism. The [[seyatel|Sower]] is the god who sowed humanity and digs a well; churches stand even in remote places, with a silver bird above the entrance and the Sower as their principal icon. The patriarch heads the church; the clergy come from the stones.
- **Catalia:** there was once “Lovecraftian” paganism here, with sea monsters—the [[drevnie|Ancients]] and Lords of the Abyss. Now, as the author put it, they “do not intervene in life” and merely provide atmosphere. The [[mirny|mirnas]] are regarded as patrons of crafts and arts, while the afterlife is divided between the blue beyond and the abyss.
- **Muaris:** one god, while the Dangarians attribute worship of the Black Maiden to the Muari.

## Technology and Money
Steam carriages—“rattlers”—and trains run through Catalian cities, people fire rifled revolvers and carbines, and steam-powered machines displace workers. These machines manufacture [[esperantsa|Esperanza]] engines. Dangarian technology lags behind: heavy locomotives and smoothbore fusils, although sailing ships fly above the capital. Catalia's currencies are escudos, pesetas, and pesos; Dangar's are marks and pfennigs. See [[tekhnika]], [[dengi]].

## History
The book reveals relatively little. Before the First Judge's arrival, hidalgos ruled the country; knightly heroes such as [[lid-anteodor|Lid Anteodor]] fought the sorcerer Faid-Musa and his Black Maiden. In Dangar, under Empress Augusta, about seventy years ago, the Chinkhar Wars took place; afterward, an entire district was built for officers' widows. Catalia's recent past includes the [[solantijskaya-ekspeditsiya|Solantan Expedition]], described by the characters as an event roughly fifteen years earlier—different remarks place it thirteen and sixteen years ago. The novel's three storylines occupy different points in time: according to the author, Red's comes before Dietrich's and Alvaro's, but later they will “merge in a certain way.”

## The Novel's Three Storylines
- **Alvaro (Catalia):** a writer loses a dueling debate, is left penniless, and accepts a commission to write a book about five famous people (ch. 1–3).
- **Dietrich (Dangar and Ollandia):** a young artilleryman in the invading army sees the future in a well and changes the course of a battle (ch. 2).
- **Red (Dangar):** a terminally ill Catalian is enslaved and digs a well in exchange for the promise of freedom (ch. 4).

For more on each storyline, see [[alvaro-da-silva]], [[ditrikh]], [[red]].

## The Spiral of Worlds
The author said Polari is one turn of a spiral, the World of Wells another, and the world of Unlucky Chris a third; they form a single metaverse, but physical passage from one world to another is impossible. The World of Wells is conceived as a setting for several books: according to the author, the second will feature the Kaiser, the patriarch, and the chief magnus. See [[roman-surzhikov]].
