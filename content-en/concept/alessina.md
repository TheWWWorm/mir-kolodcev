**Alessina** is the [[mirny|mirna]] of art. Members of the esferas associated with creativity invoke her at every opportunity: [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]] calls upon her in moments of inspiration and swears by her. She does not act in person in the novel: people speak of her, refer to her, and paint her.

## Symbols and invocations
- Her symbol is a theatrical mask. In a painting at the “Philosophy of Horror” exhibition, Alessina wears a mask, while Iolanta has a feather in her hair (ch. 16).
- A greeting at the faculty of literature: Dean [[farmiga|Farmiga]] wishes that “Alessina may light his way” when addressing Alvaro (ch. 3). Alvaro also uses this formula himself.
- Oaths: Fernan, threatened with execution by firing squad in [[karlos|Carlos el Niña’s]] den, swears by Alessina that he has not yet written anything (ch. 7). Alvaro swears by her at the autopsy (ch. 24).
- The narrator calls Alvaro “Alessina’s favorite” and a “servant” of [[iolanta|Iolanta]] as he walks in the company of an alguacil (ch. 13). The expression is also used more generally: “the servants of Iolanta and Alessina” are the esferas of Law and Art (ch. 26).

## Alessina Bay (ch. 7)
The first section of Laerta’s waterfront is called Alessina Bay. Dons’ yachts, passenger ships, and fast clippers carrying coffee, tea, and spices moor here. White pillars mark the shore. It has leafy squares, grand hotels and villas, cafés, and restaurants. Farther along the waterfront lies Christopher’s cargo harbor, and beyond a black pillar begins the [[staryj-port|Old Port]]. [[karlos|Carlos el Niña]] does not live here.

## Alessina in paintings and prayers (ch. 15–34)
- In her parables, Red’s mother names Alessina as one of the four mirnas (ch. 15).
- In a painting in the [[galereya-eliste|Eliste Gallery]], Iolanta persuades Alessina that she understands painting better than Alessina does. Alessina admits defeat, and Iolanta is declared a brilliant debater and made mirna of Law (ch. 16).
- A drunken Alvaro asks Alessina to give him the strength to “transgress morality in the name of art” and pry secrets out of a grief-stricken Carlos. Instead, Iolanta appears in the form of a drowned woman and shakes her fist at him, and Alvaro abandons the idea (ch. 18). Later, after escaping the pirate’s den, he says that “Alessina herself” is calling him to his writing desk. In the Old Port, a revolver is pointed at his face, and he silently prays to Alessina.
- During the fight in the castle cellar, Alvaro believes that his shot succeeded thanks to Eliga’s and Alessina’s intervention (ch. 34). There, he also describes Alessina as the “good” principle in a pairing with [[eliga|Eliga]].

## The concept and interviews
According to the author, the mirnas are not gods at all, but something closer to muses. As the mirna of art, Alessina comes closest to that function: people turn to her for inspiration. For more, see [[mirny|“Mirnas”]].
