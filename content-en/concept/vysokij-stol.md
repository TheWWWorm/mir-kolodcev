The High Table is the supreme body of [[kataliya|Catalia]]. It is not a royal court, and the supreme allen is not an emperor. The Table has governed the country since the [[pervyj-sudya|First Judge’s]] time, as the reformer [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan Gereira]] explains in ch. 1.

## Organization (ch. 1–24)
The Table seats [[alleny|allens]]: three from each of the eleven [[esfera|esferas]], no more and no less, for a total of thirty-three seats. The three large esferas, the “Three Bulls” ([[tri-byka]]), therefore occupy nine seats, while the eight smaller ones occupy twenty-four. The Reformists consider this unjust, because the esfera of Art numbers about a hundred thousand people, while the agricultural esfera has at least six million (ch. 1, 7). They want reform based on proportional representation.

The Table’s authority is limited. In accordance with the proverb “A tree grows on its own roots,” it handles only the foreign policy of Catalia as a whole and relations between esferas; the dons settle their esferas’ internal affairs. Decisions are made by voting. The Table is headed by the supreme allen, or supremo; in ch. 22 and 24, an election scheduled for roughly six months later is discussed, but the novel does not describe the electoral procedure.

## Purges (ch. 3, 28)
Every year, the Table has the right to expel the worst of its allens. Chapter 3 states that [[filippe|Filippe di Berro]], prim-allen of Art, has held his seat for six years, although he is considered vindictive and scandalous. In ch. 28, [[fatima|Fatima]] fears that she will be expelled at the first purge if Dangar’s kaiser changes his mind about attacking after she has supported mobilization.

## The High Table in the story (ch. 22–30)
- General [[dezerej|Deserey]] asks Fatima to raise mobilization: bringing at least two hundred thousand people into the esfera of War, at a cost of 300 million escudos in the first month and 100 million in each subsequent month. Field Marshal Cortago himself does not dare propose it: the Table interprets military requests as a hunger for money (ch. 28).
- In ch. 26, an unknown lady tells [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] about two parties: the war party—generals, members of Shadow, and some allens of Medicine, Service, and Transport—and the peace party—merchants, scholars, metal producers, and mine owners. The latter lacks a charismatic leader.
- Obrero unrest forces the Table to convene ahead of schedule and demand that the allens of Production restore order; they ask for soldiers or members of Shadow, and the right to sell cannons abroad (ch. 28, 30; see [[stachka-v-laerte]]).
- The industrialist [[kristobal-maduro|Maduro]] aspires to become an allen (ch. 34). In ch. 13, Alvaro demands that only one of Craft’s three allens should be a factory owner.

## The concept
In interviews, the author calls Catalia a federation of esferas rather than a state “in our sense”; the High Table was not mentioned in the interviews.
