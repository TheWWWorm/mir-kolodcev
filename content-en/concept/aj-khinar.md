**Ai-Khinar** is one of the [[drevnie|Ancients]], described in the novel as the most fearsome. He figures in the story as a city symbol and a detail in an exhibition, rather than as a character.

## The statue in the Old Port (ch. 7, 18)
The statue of Ai-Khinar is the symbol of Laerta’s [[staryj-port|Old Port]] district. It depicts a gigantic serpent rising out of the waves and spreading its wings, ready to soar into the air. Its jaws are open, and venom drips from its enormous fangs. Some joker has placed a real human skull between its jaws (ch. 7). In chapter 18, when Alvaro visits Carlos el Niña, the skull has been replaced by an empty bottle.

The district beyond the black pillar where Ai-Khinar stands belongs to pirates and smugglers: here, anyone wearing a pirate’s or assassin’s bracelet may kill anyone without punishment (ch. 7).

## Ai-Khinar in painting (ch. 16, 32)
At the “Philosophy of Horror” exhibition, Ai-Khinar is depicted as a madman. [[filippe|Filippe di Berro]] mentions him as they move between paintings, then immediately turns to a story about [[liess-van-khalm|van Halm]] (ch. 16). In van Halm’s study in the castle above [[tres-molinos|Tres Molinos]], a portrait of Ai-Khinar hangs alongside the Shipbreaker and the Mother-and-Father (ch. 32).
