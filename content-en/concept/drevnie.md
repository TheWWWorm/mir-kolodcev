The **Ancients**, also called the **Lords of the Abyss**, are the gods of Catalia’s old religion. They live in the sea, and Catalians invoke them at every surprise or misfortune. In the novel, they are part of language and culture rather than active characters: “Oh, Ancients!”, “for the abyss’s sake,” “may the Crab devour me.” Humanity is called “the proud creation of the Ancients” in the very first scene, when [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] debates human equality in the [[palata-slova|Chamber of the Word]] (ch. 1).

## How Catalians speak of the Ancients
- **Oaths and curses.** “In the name of the Ancients,” “Ancients save us,” “glory to the Ancients,” “lords of the abyss,” “for the abyss’s sake,” and “may the Crab devour me” occur in the speech of Alvaro, Fernan, Lucille, Red, and other characters (ch. 4, 8, 11, 13, 15).
- **Earlier names.** The audience at the Grand Opera argues about a new production of “Señorita Violet”: supposedly, in that period the Ancients were called the Winged Ones or the Lords of the Abyss, rather than the Ancients (ch. 3).
- **Parting curses.** Carlos el Niña’s quartermaster, [[zolotoj-dukat|Golden Ducat]], passes on the captain’s words: if the writer dies before finishing the book, Carlos will “make an arrangement with the Ancients,” drag him out of the abyss, and shoot him himself (ch. 13).

## The Lords of the Abyss and the First Judge’s laws (ch. 4–16)
Red has listened to stories about the seas since childhood. Their lesson is this: the human laws of the [[pervyj-sudya|First Judge]] apply only on land, while the Ancients rule the seas. They crush ships with their tentacles, swallow them like grapes, carry them away in currents and dash them against rocks, trigger eruptions in underwater volcanoes, or part the water beneath a keel. In all such stories, people encounter a power or mystery greater than themselves. In parables about the [[mirny|mirnas]], by contrast, the heroines find a way out of every impossible situation. Red regards the legends of the knight [[lid-anteodor|Lid Anteodor]] as a blend of the mirnas’ optimism and the Ancients’ grim lessons (ch. 15).

In Carlos el Niña’s tale about the explosion of a Muari galley, the blast exposes the seabed, where an ancient temple stands with an idol of the King of the Abyss (ch. 7). Carlos tells this as a yarn for the future book.

At the “Philosophy of Horror” exhibition in the [[galereya-eliste|Eliste Gallery]], the Ancients are depicted as follows (ch. 16):
- [[aj-khinar|Ai-Khinar]] is a winged serpent, depicted in the painting as a madman;
- Ikhtrodan, the Shipbreaker, is a colossal serpent coiled into a wheel and crushing frigates; its body is made of ships’ hulls, with steamship bottoms for scales and propellers for warts;
- the triptych “Mother-and-Father” depicts the Most Ancient of the Ancients, the sole parent of all the Lords of the Abyss: a spiral shell from which a pearl containing a newborn god rolls out, flanked by a woman with an oyster and a man with a mollusk in place of his mouth.

## The Ancients and the Wells (ch. 15)
Red is certain that the ancient gods created the Wells and that no mortal can dig one: “You’ve lost your mind, fischmeister.” [[joriksen|Joriksen]] replies in Dangarian that the schönbruns were created by the [[seyatel|Sower]], not the Ancients. More details: [[kolodets|Wells]], [[kolodets|Schönbrun]].

## Temples (ch. 32)
Near [[tres-molinos|Tres Molinos]] stands a temple of the Ancient Lords, closed shortly after the First Judge. After that, the locals say, the region’s development came to a halt. Portraits of the Lords of the Abyss hang on the wall of [[liess-van-khalm|van Halm’s]] study: Ai-Khinar, the Shipbreaker, and the Mother-and-Father. A statue of Ai-Khinar stands at the entrance to Laerta’s [[staryj-port|Old Port]]. Beside the general staff headquarters is an avenue of old cannons shaped like sea monsters (ch. 30).

## The concept and interviews
According to the author, the Catalians have never had monotheism. There was a “creepy,” Lovecraftian pagan faith in which sea monsters played the leading role; then the [[pervyj-sudya|First Judge]] came and abolished paganism. Today, belief in the Ancients is rather like our belief in muses: there are no prayers or icons. The author said that the Ancients live in the sea and do not interfere in human affairs. They are not important to the plot, but provide “color” and atmosphere; the exhibition of paintings about them is one example of that color. In his view, the First Judge’s influence on the Catalian worldview is greater than that of the ancient gods.
