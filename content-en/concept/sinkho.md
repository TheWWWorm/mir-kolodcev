**Sinkho** is a Catalian mourning custom. When someone in a household dies, a small dry sinkho tree in a pot is placed on the porch. The narrator calls it “a symbol of the recent death that has visited the house.”

## Ribbons (ch. 5)
Traditionally, anyone may tie a blue, red, or black ribbon to a branch as a sign of their attitude toward the deceased. The novel explicitly says only that the colors convey that attitude. Context makes clear that blue ribbons express kind words about the dead: on Dr. [[khuanes|Garcia Juanes’s]] tree, all the ribbons are blue, and [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] and [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]] have never seen such a thing before. No one wished to say a single bad word about the doctor. [[khuanes|Juanes’s]] widow says that her husband “went into the blue beyond,” an expression for death. The meanings of the red and black ribbons are not explained in the text.

This observation about the ribbons plays a part in Alvaro’s first theory: if the doctor had no enemies, why kill him?
