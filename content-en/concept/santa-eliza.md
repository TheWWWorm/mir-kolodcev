**Santa Eliza** is the vessel that sailed to [[solanta|Solanta]] on the expedition that made five Catalians famous. The novel does not specify the ship’s type; it is known to have been commanded by [[karlos|Carlos el Niña]].

## The glorious expedition (ch. 5)
The butler Emil explains to [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] and [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]] that the Solantan expedition brought fame to all of Santa Eliza’s officers, including the ship’s doctor. Garcia Juanes set sail as an ordinary doctor, but returned a hero and immediately opened a practice (ch. 5). See [[solantijskaya-ekspeditsiya|“Solantan Expedition”]].

## The navigator and the capture of the fort (ch. 22)
From the Great Scroll’s records, Alvaro learns about a court case heard three months after the expedition. Captain el Niña brought a claim against the High Table: the allens had decreed that Santa Eliza’s entire crew should be rewarded except for the dead navigator. Fatima, representing [[red|da Vira’s]] interests, gave a speech about his heroic deed. At night, a scouting party from Santa Eliza seized the coastal fort; a sentry managed to throw a grenade, and da Vira covered it with his body. The explosion would have killed at least five men and raised the alarm.

## The ship in conversation (ch. 28, 34)
- On discovering who his lover really is, Alvaro throws the name “Santa Eliza’s navigator, Rodrigo da Vira” in her face (ch. 28).
- In ch. 34, the mercenary [[salvador|Salvador]] presses Alvaro about where to find da Vira. Alvaro suggests looking for Santa Eliza’s sailors—not its officers, but ordinary seamen who know the sailors’ brothel and the girl Carmensita, who bore the navigator’s child.
