Two things bear the title “Philosophy of Horror”: a book by [[liess-van-khalm|Liess van Halm]] and an exhibition of paintings inspired by it, created by an unnamed artist. In the novel, the book and exhibition are linked by [[filippe|Filippe di Berro]] and the bookseller [[khose-flarbo|Jose Flarbo]]: according to Flarbo, the exhibition conveys “both the atmosphere of the book and its main ideas.”

## The exhibition at the Eliste Gallery (ch. 16)
The exhibition is titled “Philosophy of Horror. An Artist’s View of the Myths of the Ancients.” It has few visitors: it is noon on a working day. [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro]] comes at Filippe di Berro’s invitation, and Filippe leads him from painting to painting:
- [[iolanta|Iolanta]] and [[alessina|Alessina]] are two dead girls at an easel. One has rotted to the bone; the other has swollen in water;
- [[darredzho|Darreggio]] inserts a small steam engine into his own belly, with the caption “The highest power is power over oneself”;
- the dead Christopher, with his little donkey Toppo, drags stones: the painting is called “Strength of Spirit”;
- Ikhtrodan, the Shipbreaker, is a serpent made from steamship hulls;
- [[eliga|Eliga]] appears above a gaming table with a bullet hole in her temple;
- [[aj-khinar|Ai-Khinar]] appears as a madman;
- the triptych “Mother-and-Father” depicts the Most Ancient of the Ancients.
Filippe uses the paintings as an opportunity to persuade Alvaro to write the commissioned book. His voice drops an octave as he lists possible answers to the question of who Alvaro is: “Are you a coward? A talentless hack? A stupid brawler? A rebel? A sniveling woman? A creative genius?” The result, he says, is “barren ugliness,” and the only thing that matters is whether the book has been written.

## The book (ch. 16)
“Philosophy of Horror” is a sober-looking edition without illustrations. The first page carries a warning that the book is being published unfinished. It ranks second in sales after the memoirs of Colonel el Cameda, who died in a duel: Flarbo jokes that the only way to outsell these authors is to go mad or die.

According to Flarbo, van Halm came to Catalia twenty years earlier at the invitation of the Academy of Sciences and settled on an estate in [[seleziya|Selezia]]. He wrote that horror means stepping beyond the edge of the ordered world into chaos, and that progress is the same kind of step. Then he dismissed his servants and shut himself away. An influential person and a doctor broke down the door and found him skeletally thin: he had been eating curtains, book spines, and shoe soles.

Alvaro asks for the book to be put on his account: it costs two escudos (ch. 16). In Flarbo’s shop, he meets a mysterious female customer whose name he learns only later.

## The book and exhibition in the investigation (ch. 18–34)
- In ch. 18, [[karlos|Carlos el Niña’s]] wife [[izabel|Isabel]] asks her husband to take her to the “Philosophy of Horror” exhibition, described as “a new view of the mirnas and the Ancients.” After the exhibition, the couple were walking when an unknown hooligan distracted Carlos, while his wife was bundled into a black carriage and driven away (ch. 22).
- Alvaro calls [[fatima|Fatima]] “Iolanta from ‘Philosophy of Horror’” and a “cold, soulless corpse of a woman,” referring to the painting in the exhibition (ch. 20).
- In ch. 22, Alvaro sets out the facts for Beatriz: Isabel disappeared immediately after this exhibition, on white land, where, according to his theory, a Dangarian spy has a motive to blackmail Carlos.
- “Philosophy of Horror” is among the ten or so books on the shelf in Fatima’s bedroom (ch. 28).
- The circumstances of the author’s madness emerge at the clinic (ch. 26) and at van Halm’s house (ch. 32). The manuscript in van Halm’s study stops at the same point as the published text, and the walls are hung with portraits of the Lords of the Abyss. When told about the house’s curse, the philosopher replied that this was his chance to “look beyond the edge of the world” (ch. 32).
- In ch. 34, Alvaro explains van Halm’s madness: the cold thickened the grease and the lock jammed, although the door was not locked; for a month the philosopher believed he had been left to die. When the grease thawed, the door opened with a single turn, but by then he had already lost his mind. Alvaro also suspects that Last Winter castle is connected with a [[temnyj-shyonbrun|dark schönbrun]]. More details: [[liess-van-khalm|Liess van Halm]], [[posledniaya-zima|Last Winter]].
