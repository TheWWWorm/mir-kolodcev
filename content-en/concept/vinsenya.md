**Vinsenya** is the [[mirny|mirna]] of fertility. She appears in the novel as a detail of Laerta’s urban life, rather than as a character.

## The clock on the academy tower (ch. 3, 5)
Figurines of mirnas move around a circular balcony on the tower of the [[akademiya-iskusstv|Academy of Arts]]. A white enameled sun is painted above the balcony. Vinsenya’s figurine passes beneath it at about two in the afternoon, marking the time. At three, [[darredzho|Darreggio]] replaces her (ch. 5); the narrator ironically calls him a lazy fellow. [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro’s]] lecture begins around then.

## The church of Vinsenya (ch. 11)
The road from central Laerta to the Service district, where [[fernan-gerejra|Fernan]] lives, runs along the Northern Garden, past the church of Vinsenya, and across an arched bridge. This is the novel’s name for a Laertan church dedicated to the mirna.

## Vinsenya on the walls (ch. 13)
In the factory district, colorful grotesque pictures are daubed on blank brick walls: a bull embracing a bullfighter, a fat woman chasing a frail little man, drunken mirnas, and Vinsenya “fast asleep, naked to the waist.” The narrator observes that this would look amusing on the white walls of seaside villas, but in a working-class neighborhood it only deepens the gloom.
