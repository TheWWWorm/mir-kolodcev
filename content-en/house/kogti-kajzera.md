The Kaiser’s Claws are a special service of the Dangarian Empire whose officers are known as “claws.” They watch over state security, investigate suspicious incidents, and also certify promotions: no advancement in the army occurs without their signature. In the novel, the service is represented almost entirely by one man—Major, formerly Lieutenant, Wolf Klein—but through him we see the Claws involved both in war and in the politics of the Fishermen’s Guild.

## Organization and distinguishing features (ch. 4–23)
The service’s distinguishing marks are conspicuous. In chapter four, Red notices a man beside Joriksen dressed in black, with an iron falcon badge on his sleeve. In the camp he is called the [[klyajn|“Black Falcon”]], and only later is he revealed to be Lieutenant Wolf Klein. In Ollandia, Major Klein receives Dietrich in a black undress uniform with silver falcons on the sleeves. Klein himself dreams of a red phoenix patch, the highest distinction, almost unattainable in peacetime (ch. 31).

Klein himself gives the clearest account of the service’s tasks. Arguing with Baron Landau in chapter twenty-three, he explains that he is an officer of the special-purpose division: “We are the sword in the Kaiser’s hand and the armor on his body.” The Claws receive orders only from Schlissel, the Hofstadt district containing the ministries and palace, and no will except the emperor’s means anything to them. According to the narrator, every Claw has a specialty: Klein’s is Catalia. He knows everything about the neighboring country that can be learned from newspapers, books, and reports, and has several experienced spies in Laerta.

The service inspires fear: its name serves both as a byword and as a threat. In an argument with his batman Milosh in chapter ten, Dietrich replies that he is not a Kaiser’s Claw and cannot know how the investigation will end; when he hears the word “them” in chapter twenty-one, his first thought is of gendarmes or “Claws.”

## Watching over Dunkwald (ch. 4–33)
Fifty years ago, when Dunkwald was founded, the Kaiser sent his best agents to monitor the Fishermen’s Guild’s experiments: if the fishermen learned to create schönbruns, their influence would grow, and the sovereign needed to know first (ch. 27). Interest later waned, and unlucky Claws were posted to Dunkwald. One was Lieutenant Wolf, only a second-generation eagle, whose grandfather had been a “hare.” On the train, he befriended the young Fischmeister Joriksen, who was supposed to keep secrets from him, and they agreed to help one another escape the backwoods.

Wolf chose a modest path: he learned Catalian, expecting that one day the Kaiser would need officers familiar with the enemy’s country. When [[red|Red]] arrives at the camp, Klein is present at his first conversation with Joriksen and, during the “feeding” ceremony, offers him porridge with a black-gloved hand. He speaks broken Catalian to Red; in chapter twenty-three, he introduces himself as Wolf (meaning “wolf”). In the same chapter, when Red digs his “schönbrun,” Klein prepares to send the Kaiser a dispatch and reminds Baron Landau that he answers only to Schlissel. The baron forbids him to report until the well is restored, then demonstrates an oberfischmeister’s power in response: the lieutenant is temporarily blinded.

In chapter twenty-seven, it is Klein who reveals to Red that the “schönbrun” was a deception for the sake of science, and tells him that he recognized him as a bandit from the Laerta Herald but told no one. By the end of their second year of service, Joriksen keeps his promise and secures Wolf’s transfer to [[khofshtadt|Hofstadt]]. Several years later, a recommendation from the Claws leads General Brauschwitz to take Red into the army (ch. 33).

## The investigation of Dietrich and the schönbrun of victory (ch. 6–31)
In chapter six, Major Klein summons [[ditrikh|Dietrich]] to the town hall of occupied [[latsen|Latsen]], ostensibly to certify his recommendation for a decoration for the [[tri-zalpa|battle at the well]]. He begins as a genial conversationalist, calling the lieutenant a hero, but then lays five bullets on the table for the battle’s five “peculiarities”—the stones’ flight, Salvation’s disappearance, the accurate shot that killed Zeidvist, and the faultless calculation of positions—and sets out a theory in which Dietrich is a traitor. Dietrich can refute it only by pointing out that he carried no weapon. The interview ends with his suspension and return to Hofstadt “until the investigation is complete”: “Consider your discharge a reward for your heroic deed.”

The Claws reappear in chapter twenty-nine: Count von Lurd warns Prince Lenz that they have found no proof of his guilt in the attack on the station, but suspicion remains. Together with the duel and the “fishing,” this may be enough for him to be classified “unsicher.” In chapter thirty-one, Klein, now a major, goes beyond simple surveillance: learning that Dietrich consulted a fischmeister about his mysterious victory, he finds the [[kolodets|schönbrun]] near the railway halt, brings Joriksen to it, and orders him to study it within two weeks. General [[khofmajer|Hofmeyer]] needs a “schönbrun of victory” so that his army can reach the Catalian border. Klein, dreaming of his own red phoenix patch, also hints that Hofmeyer should delay the courier carrying Field Marshal [[rajniger|Reiniger’s]] order, forcing the general to take Mershants. This is described in the article on the [[olandskaya-vojna|Ollandian War]]. Klein has Dietrich arrested by gendarmes, but releases him under pressure from Olkha and Katrina von Lurd, backed by influential families. He compares his alliance with Dietrich to a parable: a snake rides on a tortoise, and both die if they quarrel.

## The Catalian connection (ch. 32–34)
Because Klein knows Catalia better than the others and maintains spies there, the Claws are discussed in the Catalian chapters as one possible explanation. In chapter thirty-two, Fernan observes that the Dangarians seen in Selezia are more likely deserters than Claw agents: they were enslaved and driven to the front. In chapter thirty-four, Alvaro suggests to Salvador that he is a Dangarian spy serving a Kaiser’s Claw: his accomplice has a bolt-action rifle that cannot be bought; the mercenaries kill on grey land too readily; and they know the word “schönbrun.” Salvador responds only with a threat. The service is not explicitly named in these chapters, and Salvador’s death leaves the question open.
