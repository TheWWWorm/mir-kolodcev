The Fishermen’s Guild is a community of Dangarian masters skilled in the art of “fishing” in magical wells. Dangarians call such a well a [[kolodets|schönbrun]] (“beautiful well”), its owner a landowner, and a master capable of drawing a miracle from it a [[fishmajster|fischmeister]]—literally, “master fisherman.” The guild appears in the novel almost exclusively through its members: Joriksen, Landau, and the Grey. To the reader, it remains an organization whose internal workings are little known but whose power is beyond doubt.

## What “fishing” means and why the guild is needed (ch. 4–14)
The first guild representative to appear in the novel is [[joriksen|Fritjof Joriksen]]: in chapter four, he receives the chained [[red|Red]] while wearing a purple cloak with a silver catfish-shaped brooch, and calls himself a “master fisherman.” The term’s meaning becomes clear later. “Fishing” is a session a master conducts at a schönbrun. He first checks the date, time, weather, and number of people “aboard” according to the “sea reckoning,” collects the participants’ wishes, burns their papers with powder and a potion, and then removes the lid. The spectators look into the well, usually through mirrors, and see an image of another world at its bottom. Euphoria and the “catch” follow—a miracle, such as relief from heartache. Such a session is shown in the [[rybalka-valbrekht|fishing event at Baroness Walbrecht’s]].

The guild does not, however, sell miracles wholesale. In chapter fourteen, Joriksen says that he works only with schönbrun owners and charges very high prices. The guild maintains an atlas of lands that records every known schönbrun and, as chapter twenty-one reveals, every landowner as well. According to Joriksen, each schönbrun has its own temperament: one likes spiders on the walls, another grows angry at redheads, and the baroness’s well likes prime numbers. There are therefore no formulas, and a master must spend days or months studying a new well. Joriksen’s study contains volumes describing the temperaments of all the Wells known to the guild.

The guild has found no new schönbruns in the past half-century: this is precisely why Dietrich is not believed when he says he has discovered an unknown well (ch. 14).

## Dunkwald and the enslaved diggers (ch. 8–33)
The guild’s other face is that of a slaveholder. The settlement of [[dunkvald|Dunkwald]], in Dangar’s forest wilderness, was founded about fifty years ago as a site for scientific experiments. Hundreds of the guild’s enslaved “stones” dig wells under widely varying, sometimes lethal conditions—the [[tsushtand|Zustand]], a condition selected for the digger—while apprentices record everything, a fischmeister examines the water, and the pit is filled in. The aim is to achieve what only the [[seyatel|Sower]] could do: create a schönbrun dug by human hands. In half a century, not one pit has yielded magical water.

At first, Dunkwald was considered an important undertaking: the finest masters visited, and the Kaiser sent his best agents to oversee the experiments (ch. 27). Interest later waned, and the unlucky and low-born began to be sent there for ten-year postings. This was how the young Fischmeister Joriksen, who has some Chinkharian ancestry, and the “Kaiser’s Claw” Wolf Klein ended up in the settlement. Dunkwald’s slaves are guild property: when Red scalds the dugout’s [[brigadir|foreman]] in chapter twelve, Joriksen’s verdict treats it as damage to the guild’s property. Like every slaveholder in the empire, the guild pays the state a head tax in people: each spring, army collectors arrive in Dunkwald and take slaves into military service (ch. 33). Red himself leaves for the army under just such an order near the end of the novel.

## Ranks and organization (ch. 4–29)
The guild’s hierarchy emerges from scattered references.
- **Apprentices** begin with menial work and keep records. Otto, Major Klein’s son, has been Joriksen’s apprentice since the previous winter; Wiglaf assists the master during fishing.
- **Fischmeister** means master fisherman. Joriksen holds first-class rank (ch. 14), but is not yet an oberfischmeister; in chapter thirty-one, Klein tempts him with the chance to become one. Fishermen study medicine: Baron Landau, for example, is a doctor and uses a listening tube (ch. 15).
- **Oberfischmeister** is the highest rank, held by very few. Landau demonstrates its essence in chapter twenty-three: a fischmeister knows how to fish, but an oberfischmeister can “take the catch with him”—inflict a schönbrun’s effects on a person, such as blindness, petrification, or the inability to close the mouth. According to Joriksen, Oberfischmeister Flammel, who leads the Dunkwald investigation, does not fully understand his own power.
- **Magnus** is the head of the guild. Amir the Grey, Joriksen’s teacher, wears an azure frock coat with golden stars and uses a golden clepsydra that slows time around him and muffles sound.

Clothing is another mark of rank: Joriksen wears a purple cloak with a silver catfish brooch; Landau appears in an azure cloak with a golden catfish brooch. The novel does not set out a strict color system.

## The guild among the empire’s powers (ch. 17–31)
Speaking with Dietrich, Count [[lurd|von Lurd]] identifies three centers of influence in Dangar: the Kaiser with the army, the patriarch with the clergy, and the magnus with the guild. The first is supreme but needs the others as a counterbalance (ch. 17, 25). According to him, the secular nobility is fairly close to the fischmeisters, but the guild shows less zeal in serving the empire than the Kaiser would like; a bridegroom from the clergy is therefore preferable for [[olkha|Olkha]]. Priests, he adds, come from former stones. At their meeting on the train, the magnus says that the Kaiser is under the patriarch’s influence, dines with him on Tuesdays and Saturdays, and listens to newspaper reports; the guild’s misfortunes please the patriarch.

Relations with the Kaiser are strained: when supply steamships for Kurtzleben’s army were held up by Mergana’s protest, the furious Kaiser criticized the fischmeisters, although they had nothing to do with supplies (ch. 31). The guild knows more about the “Kaiser’s Claws” than it would like: one of them, Klein, is Joriksen’s friend and uses him for his own ends.

## The destruction of Dunkwald (ch. 14–31)
The guild’s gravest blow is the [[gibel-dunkvalda|destruction of Dunkwald]]. The first rumors reach the capital’s nobility as early as the baroness’s fishing event: guests discuss the “terrible incident,” and in the gazebo Joriksen says that the guild is bewildered and the magnus furious. The magnus gives the full picture in chapter twenty-nine: a postman found the settlement full of corpses, about four hundred people, including three platoons of soldiers; there were many killers, and not all were human. The magnus assigns the investigation to Oberfischmeister Flammel, personally questions Joriksen, and insists that every pit has been examined and there is not a single schönbrun in Dunkwald. Of Landau’s loss, he says that it is harder on the guild than losing another three Dunkwalds would be.

## Relationships
- [[amir-sedoj|Amir the Grey]]—magnus, Joriksen’s mentor, and Flammel’s superior.
- [[joriksen|Fritjof Joriksen]]—a young fischmeister, formerly assistant to the senior master in Dunkwald; deceived Landau for the sake of the experiment with Red.
- [[landau|Landau]]—oberfischmeister and head of the Dunkwald experiments.
- [[kogti-kajzera|Kaiser’s Claws]]—the secret service that monitored the experiments in Dunkwald.
- [[red|Red]]—a guild slave, digger, and Dunkwald’s “beast.”
