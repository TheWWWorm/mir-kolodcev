Mergana is a country on the River [[khursla|Khursla]] that scarcely appears directly in the novel but is constantly mentioned in conversation. In chapter 4, the narrator calls it the home of white chocolate and “the great astronomer who explained the sun’s movement across the sky.” The capital is guarded by a gunboat that meets foreign steamships; the traveler goes ashore at the famous Professors’ Bridge, which leads from the Fishermen’s District to the University’s fairy-tale towers. For a native of Laerta, the narrator says, this marks the western limit of the world they know well.

## Role in the story
- The River Khursla, running through Mergana, links Ollandia to the Kobold Sea. This is why, in chapter 31, Dangarian steamships carrying supplies for [[kurtsleben|Kurtzleben’s]] army become stranded: Mergana has lodged a protest and refused them passage. Dangar’s foreign ministry settles the matter, but the steamships have already moved to other routes, leaving the cargo to rot in port warehouses.
- Mergana produces valisyan, a strong drink that Count [[lurd|Ludwig von Lurd]] keeps on a separate shelf in his continent-shaped bar (ch. 17).
- The philosopher [[liess-van-khalm|Liess van Halm]], author of [[filosofiya-uzhasa|Philosophy of Horror]], is Merganan by birth; he has lived in Catalia for twenty years after being invited by the Academy of Sciences (ch. 16, 26, 32, 34).
- A Catalian teaching hospital is named after Infanta Teresa of Mergana (ch. 24).
- Dangarian characters consider the possibility that runaway stones fled from Ollandia into Mergana, from which they cannot be brought back (ch. 10).
- Beatriz, [[fatima|Fatima’s]] daughter, speaks Merganan (ch. 24, 26).

## On the author’s map
Mergana appears as an elongated region along the lower Khursla, between Dangar and Ollandia; an unlabeled city stands at its edge. Its color is brownish-grey, distinct from Dangarian orange and Ollandian green.
