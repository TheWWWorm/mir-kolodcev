The Muari Emirates are a country viewed in the novel through other people’s eyes: Dangarians call the Muari enemies of the Sower, while Catalians see them as merchants, smugglers, and maritime rivals. The Muari themselves are dark-complexioned people—[[filippe|Don Filippe’s]] servant is “a swarthy Muari in a white tailcoat”—who speak with an accent and believe in one god. [[dangar|Dangar]] considers them worshippers of the “Black Maiden” (ch. 2).

## Organization and position
In chapter 17, Count [[lurd|Ludwig von Lurd]] explains that Muaris is an entire continent “under the rule of one god,” divided into emirates that often quarrel with one another but unite in times of need. Given their territory and population, he says, the Muari are a formidable power. The map in his study cannot fit the whole continent. Above the emirates stands a sultan with a harem; slavery is forbidden, as it is in Catalia, and a wife stolen from the sultan’s harem is returned to her owner (ch. 32).

## Sea and trade (ch. 17, 20)
The Muari formerly dominated the Miriam Sea along the continent’s northern coast and used the reef-ringed island of [[solanta|Solanta]] as a base against Catalian pirates of the Shadow esfera. Thirteen years ago, Catalia signed a treaty with Solanta, and since then the sea has ceased to be safe for the Muari. Their trade with Dangar declined, while trade with Laerta flourished: Catalia receives silk for sewing machines and saltpeter for Brega’s cannon, and supplies “Iturbe-Clemento” locomotives for the sultan’s railways. [[esperantsa|Esperanza]] steam-powered vehicles are also sold in the emirates.
