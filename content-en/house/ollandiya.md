Ollandia is the country where much of the fighting in the novel’s first half takes place. In chapter 4, it is described through a traveler’s eyes as a land of princely palaces in landscaped gardens, cathedrals with stained-glass windows, brightly dressed hussars in helmets with long feathers, orchestras, and haughty, rosy-cheeked ladies. Pride is written on every face, from the stationmaster to the woman selling pretzels, yet it has no industry of its own: we are told that if the Ollandians had factories, they would manufacture arrogance, and every locomotive in the country is Catalian. At the beginning of the novel, Ollandia is already losing its war with [[dangar|Dangar]].

## Land and cities
The country lies between Dangar and Catalia. Travelers enter from Catalia through the border fortress of [[indra|Indra]] and a belt of marshes. The railway ends at [[ginarva|Ginarva]]; beyond, the route follows the River [[khursla|Khursla]] by steamship, past landings and border bastions as far as [[mergana|Mergana]]. On the author’s map, Ollandia is the large central region, with mountains to the north and east and a lake east of [[latsen|Latsen]]. [[plyutva|Plyutva]] is marked farther west beside the Dangarian frontier; [[mershants|Mershants]] stands at the very eastern edge of the map, with Ginarva and [[bargot|Bargot]] above the river to the south. Arrows show the advances of the three Dangarian armies: [[kurtsleben|Kurtzleben]] southward, [[brojenshtoff|Brojenstoff]] northward, and [[khofmajer|Hofmeyer]] east toward Mershants.

By chapter 31, the country is described as follows, with [[bargot|Bargot]] as its capital: mountains and shepherds in the north, wealthy principalities in the south, and three voivodeships—Plyutva, Latsen, and Mershants. Latsen is called the country’s geographical center, whose capture split it into north and south.

## Society and language
The author said in an interview that Ollandia has social classes—nobility, bourgeoisie, and peasantry—but no slavery, making its people easy to incorporate into Dangar’s system. In style and fate, the country resembles the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth; when asked by the host, the author answered, “In places, yes, there are similarities.” Ollandian names in the novel are Slavic: Prince [[lents|Wladyslaw Lenz]] and Princess [[olkha|Olkha]]. The author said that he chooses names so that they sound different from one another and an Ollandian immediately stands out in Dangarian company. The Ollandian language does not appear in the text, and the author admitted that he “had not thought about” what it resembles—possibly Polish.

## War and occupation (ch. 2–6, 14, 28, 31)
The Ollandian forces failed to unite. Even before the war, Prince [[lents|Lenz]] understood that the fragmentation of the noble retinues would doom the country (ch. 17), and tried to persuade the nobles to join forces. In Hofstadt, he established connections among the nobility, including [[varkhajt|von Wahrheit]] and Baroness [[elza|Walbrecht]]. A hussar squadron concealed in Mitnaya Oakwood attacked Lieutenant [[ditrikh|von Dort’s]] Dangarian battery near a railway halt on the Plyutva–Latsen line ([[tri-zalpa|The Battle at the Well on the Hill]]) and was defeated.

According to the characters, Plyutva was almost razed to the ground, and ten thousand Ollandians were branded as stones (ch. 10). Latsen was captured and became Field Marshal [[rajniger|Reiniger’s]] headquarters; a clean square, an orchestra, and a line of women at the fountain form the picture of occupation (ch. 6). Later, in chapter 28, Catalia learns that six months after Ollandia’s capitulation, the Dangarian armies have not withdrawn and are continuing to build the Plyutva–Latsen–Mershants railway. Yet in Dangarian reports (ch. 31), the war is not over: in the north, [[brojenshtoff|Brojenstoff’s]] army faces ambushes and guerrilla warfare; Kurtzleben advances on Bargot, the capital; and Hofmeyer besieges Mershants, defended by two Catalian howitzers. See [[olandskaya-vojna|The Ollandian War]].

## Dangarian policy (ch. 25, 29)
The empire “sorts” the Ollandian nobility: the reliable retain their lands and their people’s freedom, while the unreliable lose their land and have between twenty and seventy percent of their people branded as slaves. Status also depends on personal connections: Prince Lenz, whose lands lay at the center of the battle, nearly becomes “unsicher,” and Count [[lurd|Lurd]] explicitly threatens him with this. The author confirmed that Ollandian nobles become eagles in Dangar and some peasants become stones. According to the characters, an Ollandian noble related by marriage to the clergy or military nobility could become the Kaiser’s governor in Ollandia.

## By the end of the novel (ch. 31)
By the conclusion, the country is divided: central Ollandia is under Dangarian occupation, the north resists, and the south along the Khursla and Mershants in the east face decisive battles. Dangarian Field Marshal Reiniger has given Kurtzleben sixty days to cross the river and besiege Bargot, while ordering Hofmeyer to contain Mershants and send some divisions on a flanking attack. Major [[klyajn|Klein]] hints to Hofmeyer that the courier carrying the order might “never arrive,” so the divisions would not march in time.
