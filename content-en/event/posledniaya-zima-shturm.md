Chapter thirty-four brings together everything scattered through the preceding chapters: the history of the Solantan expedition, Juanes’s death, the “curse” of the House Above, and the theory of a Dangarian spy. It begins with an argument between two friends and ends in gunfire. It is the most eventful chapter of the Catalian storyline.

## The climb to the castle (ch. 34)
In the evening, Alvaro and Fernan climb the road above Tres Molinos to van Halm’s castle. That morning, Fernan argued that the “Dangarian gang” that appeared here could not be so bad, and that they had consciences; now Alvaro wants to prove that the Dangarians came with malicious intentions. He reminds his friend of two important facts they overlooked: the water tower stands far below the estate, and it was very cold on the mountain on the night the Solantan heroes visited. He now calls the castle “Last Winter” and connects its curse with Eliga, goddess of chance. On the way, he recounts the fates of those affected by the curse: Juanes drank the wrong medicine and strong bonk; Carlos el Niña’s suffocating care drove his wife Isabel into a lover’s arms; and van Halm lost his mind.

## Da Vira’s story and the door (ch. 34)
In the northern residential wing, Alvaro tells the story of the navigator Rodrigo da Vira, a hot-tempered brawler whom Captain Carlos outmaneuvered in a debate. Carlos bought a chart of the approach to Solanta and planned the Solantan expedition with Maduro, while Dr. Juanes diagnosed da Vira with incurable lung sarcoma and blabbed to the captain. For details, see [[solantijskaya-ekspeditsiya|The Solantan Expedition]].

He then demonstrates the mystery of the “cursed” door. In van Halm’s bedroom, he ties a bag of ice to the handle, and Fernan, alone on the other side, panics when the door will not open. Alvaro explains: it was cold in the castle that night, the rusty lock’s grease thickened, the latch jammed, and the handle stopped turning. The door was not locked. Believing himself imprisoned, van Halm spent a month without paper or pen; when the thaw came and the grease softened, a single turn would have opened the door, as Maduro and Juanes eventually did. The philosopher lost his reason completely upon learning that he could have left.

## The attack (ch. 34)
The second important fact is that the water in the castle’s washbasin does not come from the water tower, so the castle must have its own supply. Then a disturbance breaks out in the courtyard: Deserey’s agents have brought in a “spy”—Beatriz, who came to Tres Molinos on business of her own. Alvaro releases her, and the agents enter the house to find warm clothes. Suddenly, shots ring out from the tower; both agents are killed, and Fernan is wounded in the leg. Alvaro fires toward the tower, but the second killer has already seized Beatriz and pressed a pistol to her temple, so Alvaro drops his revolver.

In the castle hall, Maduro’s mercenary Salvador, who previously shot Alvaro in the Old Port, interrogates him. The second killer stands nearby with a bolt-action rifle. Salvador asks about Rodrigo da Vira. A blow to the ribs makes an idea “flash” through Alvaro’s mind: what if da Vira is alive and carrying out his own revenge? He sets out the chain of events: the doge sold da Vira into Dangarian slavery; the navigator was held captive by the fishermen, then entered the army, escaped with other stones during the Ollandian War, and returned to Catalia with a brand burned into his chest. Hearing about the cursed house, he realized a well lay nearby; he seized the castle, imprisoned van Halm, and forced him to write five invitations to the heroes of Solanta, who then drank the water of misfortune. Fernan proposes luring da Vira out with an article about children drinking water from the schönbrun. Alvaro also deduces that Salvador is a Dangarian spy serving a Kaiser’s Claw. Salvador does not deny it, merely ordering him to be quiet.

## The cellar (ch. 34)
Salvador agrees to the bait but demands that they find the schönbrun of misfortune. Alvaro works out that the well is in the boarded-up southern wing: pipes lead there from a tank with a pump, while a wind-powered drive stands in the attic. Armored knights stand in the dining hall; a pipe leads to a small door, beyond which stairs descend into the cellar. Fernan and Beatriz go down first, feigning weakness. Dozens of mirrors on the cellar ceiling allow people to look into the well; when Salvador tilts his head back, Fernan strikes him in the face with a burning lantern. Alvaro grabs Salvador’s revolver and fires: the mercenary burns like a living torch, and Alvaro finishes him with a merciful shot. The second killer throws a grenade down the stairs; after the explosion, Alvaro fires at a silhouette, and the bullet wounds the killer’s arm and leg.

## Fatima (ch. 34)
Frey and Fatima enter the cellar, and Fatima asks Alvaro not to look into the well. She wants to “start playing honestly” and explains that she had no affair with da Vira: she despised him and later helped Carlos have him declared dead in court. The five heroes received van Halm’s invitations, looked into the well, and saw beings there that were not human. A couple of days later, Carlos received a letter from the living da Vira marked “the two-faced one’s bullet.” Fatima herself sent Juanes the anonymous note. She came incognito to Tres Molinos with Frey and worked out how the well operates. Her three laws of the dark schönbrun of misfortune are: those who look into the water, including its reflection, at a particular time become victims; disaster strikes by chance; and misfortune attacks what is most precious. That is why Fatima asks Alvaro and Beatriz not to become too precious to her.

## Outcomes (ch. 34)
Fatima has brought ten armed alguacils and five cars. The wounded agent is sent to hospital, and the bodies and bound killer are taken away. Beatriz rides in the Esperanza with Fernan, while Alvaro returns to Laerta with Fatima. Fatima is glad they are both alive, but anxiety colors her voice. Asked what happens next, she answers: “That is tomorrow’s Fatima’s problem.”

Da Vira himself does not appear in the chapter. The reader knows him as [[red|Red]]: he is the leader of the six Dangarians who lived in the castle before the heroes’ visit, returned from slavery with “THOU SHALT NOT KILL” branded on his chest. The novel does not say where he is now.
