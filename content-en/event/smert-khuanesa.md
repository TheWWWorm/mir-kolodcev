The death of the physician Garcia Juanes is the first in the series of mysterious events that opens Alvaro’s book about the heroes of Solanta. At first it appears natural, then a murder, then an absurd domestic mishap, and finally part of someone else’s revenge. In the plot, it initiates the detective storyline of the Catalian chapters.

## The doctor’s last day (ch. 5)
Juanes lived in a white district of Laerta beside the Valentian Gardens, in a house with a withered sinkho sapling by the porch: ribbons are tied to these trees in memory of the dead. He had been the Santa Eliza’s ship’s physician, returned a hero, quickly built a large practice, and was known as a man who could listen. He suffered from hypertension and took drops of a balsam he prepared himself.

The butler Emil tells Alvaro and Fernan everything that happened on the day of the death when they visit the house. Juanes saw his regular patients; after lunch, an important artillery officer he had known for years arrived. Juanes prescribed medicine for the officer’s low blood pressure and ordered tea. The officer spoke intently about Dangar: the Kaiser’s troops had crushed the Ollandian princes, threatening dire consequences. Juanes burned his mouth on the tea, broke his cup, and grabbed a glass of water from the table. By evening, he had taken to bed, taken his balsam, and been unable to sleep. Complaining of stuffiness and heart pain, he took a second dose, went up to the roof for air, and fell dead on the upper flight of stairs. Doctors diagnosed a rupture of the heart muscle.

## Initial suspicions (ch. 5–22)
Alvaro begins by asking who served the water and whether the officer was a friend. Those present are horrified: he suspects murder, yet they stand on white land, where murders do not occur—there are no locks, guards, or even window bars. Nevertheless, he inspects the house. Emil shows him a cabinet of patients’ gifts, including a bottle of ten-year-old Almagarian bonk from the officer. Juanes was not allowed strong drink, and Emil had already tried the bonk on the day of the funeral without being poisoned. Alvaro takes away an anonymous note assembled from words cut from a newspaper: “I know everything. You will not escape retribution.”

Beatriz, having read the newspaper, objects: murder is impossible in a white district, so this must be a medical error. In chapter eighteen, Carlos el Niña adds to the mystery: Juanes was an ordinary doctor but talked too much, which displeased both Carlos and Allen Fatima. “It began with da Vira and ended with this.” In chapter twenty-two, Alvaro and Beatriz develop theories involving two antagonists: a Dangarian spy who might, for example, have been Juanes’s patient and suffered from his loose tongue; and Fatima’s son by da Vira, avenging himself on the doctor. The theory about Fatima’s son collapses: Beatriz turns out to be “Fatima’s daughter,” and according to Beatriz, Fatima has no son.

## Alvaro’s solution (ch. 24)
The solution begins in the anatomical theater: fleeing the smell of an autopsy, Alvaro hears “rupture of the heart muscle” and “tamponade” and connects them with Juanes’s death. The next day, he visits the dead man’s house with Beatriz and reconstructs Deserey’s visit from the ledger. The officer was General Gabriel Deserey, who had come with low blood pressure. Juanes prescribed drops from a red vial, but after burning himself on the tea, absentmindedly drank from the general’s glass, into which the drops had already been measured. In a man with hypertension, medicine for low blood pressure triggered an attack. Juanes realized what had happened and sent his wife for his own “antidote,” the balsam in the blue vial.

The butler played the part of fate. Left in the study, he decided to sample the expensive bonk, poured it into an empty vial, failed to put it away, and left when the mistress entered. She took that very vial from the cabinet. Juanes drank the bonk without tasting it because he had burned his tongue with boiling water. Thus Emil killed him without knowing he was killing, so the white land’s taboo did not take effect. He also removed black ribbons from the sapling so that no one would suspect murder. The butler can expect forgiveness if he helps with another investigation: Beatriz asks him to tell them about van Halm. His account reveals that three months before his death, Juanes visited a strange philosopher, after which Maduro’s chauffeur drove him away with an unknown third person. On returning, the doctor recorded that van Halm was ill and had been transferred to [[klinika-miloserdiya|Mercy Clinic]]; his blood pressure had risen frequently ever since.

## What lay behind the solution (ch. 26–34)
By chapter thirty, Alvaro regards the death as an accident and abandons the murder theory, but continues looking for a connection to the heroes of Solanta. In chapter twenty-eight, he overhears Deserey tell Fatima that “after what happened to the doctor,” Catalia cannot go to war; Fatima replies that it is Deserey himself who cannot command an army. This shows that the general feels implicated in the chain of accidents.

Chapter thirty-four supplies the full picture. Juanes came with the other heroes of Solanta to van Halm’s castle, Last Winter, and drank water from the dark schönbrun of misfortune. Each of the five loses what they value most “through some wild accident.” In Alvaro’s formulation, Juanes died from an elixir of his own making. Fatima reveals that she sent the anonymous note herself: she went to Juanes to learn how the “dead” navigator could threaten them years later, but the physician kept insisting that his diagnosis had been correct. In anger, she cobbled together the anonymous message to rub his nose in his mistake. Moreover, by the time of Juanes’s death, she already knew what to look for—a chain of improbable accidents—and found the final link during her meeting with Alvaro in the anatomical theater.

## Relationships and role in the plot
- Juanes’s death connects Alvaro’s storyline with the history of [[red|Rodrigo da Vira]]: the physician’s diagnosis and loose tongue set the revenge in motion.
- The investigation brings Alvaro closer to [[beatris|Beatriz]], who searches the dead man’s house with him and obtains information about van Halm from the butler.
- The death demonstrates how a dark [[temnyj-shyonbrun|schönbrun]] works: it cannot kill directly, but it controls chance.
