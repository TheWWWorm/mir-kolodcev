The unrest in Laerta forms the backdrop to the Catalian storyline, but in several chapters it intrudes into Alvaro’s life: he finds himself on the tracks between a barricade and guards, meets Mor, has to explain himself to Don Maduro’s guards, and finally leaves the city in the middle of a strike. The novel gives no exact chronology; events unfold over several weeks, interspersed with the investigation of Juanes’s death.

## Causes and the Chamber debate (ch. 7–11)
In Catalia, steam-powered machinery displaces workers in the Craft esfera. In chapter seven, Fernan explains that the previous day, obreros challenged the factory owners’ right to dismiss them without cause in the [[palata-slova|Chamber of the Word]], but the owners fielded hired representatives from the Law esfera and won. Fernan invites Alvaro to a demonstration, but Alvaro considers his visit to Carlos el Niña more important.

Lucille supplies the details in chapter eleven: two days earlier, dismissed obreros, backed by bankers from the Finance esfera, challenged the factory-owning dons to a debate. The dons did not appear, sending legal specialists led by Allen Fatima instead, and the obreros lost. The workers marched to Don Balboa’s arms factory, where guards and thugs from the Shadow esfera beat them, while newspapers called them “madmen with torches.” This lie outraged the Craft and Service esferas: “The Three Bulls stand together.” Mor later tells the same story from another angle (ch. 20): the dons challenged the bankers to a debate in the Chamber of the Word and secured a ruling that preferential loans to workers constituted interference in the Production esfera’s affairs. The novel does not explain the discrepancy between these accounts.

## The railway barricade (ch. 11)
Returning from Ortiyar, Alvaro and Beatriz see crowds of gloomy people in grey factory jackets and torn shoes. Lucille leads her husband and Alvaro to the station; masters wearing bracelets already march at the head of the procession, and many people have yellow ribbons in their buttonholes. The crowd’s weapons are hammers and wrenches. Workers build a barricade with yellow flags across the tracks to stop a train from Brega carrying steam-powered machines for Don Balboa. Guards in helmets and masks, along with “Shadows,” gather on the other side. This is grey land, where shooting is forbidden.

The train stops almost at Alvaro’s feet. The bandits’ leader shouts threats from a twisted mouth, while the workers demand that they remove their masks. In the crush, Fernan is pushed toward the bandits and beaten. Alvaro draws his revolver and breaks a thug’s nose with the grip, taking blows from a gunstock to his elbow and stomach. The workers join the fight and push back the guards; an enormous man with a mechanical arm, whom Alvaro takes for Mor but who is later revealed to be Little One, lifts Fernan with one hand. Then two cannon roll out from behind the bandits and fire. Everyone thinks the guns are firing canister, and the obreros flee; Lucille, sheltering behind the locomotive, realizes the charges are blanks. A second volley follows, turning the retreat into a general rout. At home, Alvaro feels euphoric, then despondent: he thinks about money and the fact that he can afford neither wine nor a gift for [[malena|Malena]].

## Consequences for Alvaro (ch. 13–20)
In chapter thirteen, Wasp gives Alvaro Mor’s invitation to a general meeting at the Three Bulls, and he weakly promises to try to attend. A few days later, visiting Don Maduro’s mansion on business concerning the book, he ends up in the guardroom. There, the guard Salvador, whose nose is broken—one of those who fought for Balboa on the tracks—interrogates him: what do the Yellow Ribbons want? Alvaro improvises, demanding that only one of Craft’s three allens be a factory owner, and leaves with the declaration that a writer is society’s conscience.

In chapter twenty, wounded, he reaches the Three Bulls, where his friends help him. There Mor explains that the workers do not intend to destroy the machines: that is what newspapers bribed by the dons claim. Her real aim is to win a new debate in the Chamber of the Word, which requires taking the Law esfera out of the contest. For more, see [[reformisty|Reformists]].

## Political background (ch. 26–28)
In chapter twenty-six, the mysterious lady, later revealed to be Fatima, explains to Alvaro that the supremo elections are approaching. Some expect a “war party,” others a “peace party”; on the left stand the Three Bulls, torn apart by the obreros’ revolt against the factory owners. She suggests that the owners need mass dismissals to create a crisis, so the High Table will turn to them before the election. The Law esfera has unexpectedly backed the factory owners, although Mor’s Esperanza has introduced competition and become useful to many esferas.

In chapter twenty-eight, Alvaro scans the newspapers: the Reformists have organized strikes at three factories at once, and the High Table has asked Production’s allens to take action.

## The strike (ch. 30)
By chapter thirty, the unrest in Laerta is gathering force. The cabman taking Alvaro and Fernan curses the Yellow Ribbons and explains that the High Table met ahead of schedule and demanded that Production’s allens restore order; they are asking for soldiers or “Shadows” and the right to sell cannon abroad. People wearing yellow ribbons crowd around the Three Bulls tavern. The main hall has become an improvised headquarters, with a map of factories that have joined the strike, while the inn upstairs serves as an infirmary for people beaten by Maduro’s men. Lucille sends her husband off to Selezia with Alvaro without objection.

## By the end of the novel (ch. 34)
The strike has not ended by the conclusion. In Last Winter Castle, Alvaro reminds Maduro’s mercenary that one of his captives is Mor’s assistant, and that if he is harmed, Fatima will join Mor against his employer. The novel does not reveal how it all ends.
