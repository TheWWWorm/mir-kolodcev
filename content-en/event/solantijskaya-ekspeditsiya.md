The Solantan expedition is an event from sixteen years earlier, as well known in Catalia as the exploits of Lid Anteodor. Five of its participants are entered in the [[svitok|Great Scroll]], with ballads composed and books written about them. That is why [[filippe|Don Filippe di Berro]] commissions [[alvaro-da-silva|Alvaro da Silva]] to write a book about how the heroes live now. Almost the entire Catalian storyline grows out of this commission.

## The official account (ch. 3–22)
In chapter three, Filippe gives Alvaro a list of five names: the corsair and smuggler Carlos el Niña, General Gabriel Deserey, the factory owner Maduro, the prim-allen of Law Fatima Ir-Reya, and the Santa Eliza’s ship’s physician Garcia Juanes. The doctor is already dead when the novel begins. All five received fame and rewards. Fernan Gereira, a committed Reformist, calls the four surviving heroes “bloodsuckers” in chapter five, excluding the late doctor, but does not question their exploit.

Count von Lurd outlines the feat to Dietrich in chapter seventeen. Solanta is a small island surrounded by reefs and currents. It was an ally of the [[muaris|Muari]], who had been enemies of Catalia since ancient times. No one could approach without a knowledgeable pilot, making it an impregnable base for Muari traders and warships. But one day, a pirate schooner found a passage through the reefs and captured the coastal fort under cover of night. The pirates turned its cannon toward the doge’s palace and offered negotiations. Afraid the entire Catalian fleet would follow, the doge signed an alliance treaty. Under its terms, Catalia gained the right to sell the “flying trees,” brante, that grow on the island, and its fleet was allowed to enter Solantan ports. Lurd regards the treaty as a turning point: Solanta became a base for Catalians as well as Muari, and trade brought Laerta and Muaris closer despite their centuries of hostility.

Alvaro finds the second part of the official account in the Scroll’s archives (ch. 22). Three months after the voyage, the High Table intended to reward every sailor aboard the Santa Eliza except the navigator Rodrigo da Vira. Carlos sued the High Table itself, and Fatima came to defend the fallen man’s memory. According to her speech, a scouting party took the coastal fort at night without firing a shot, but one sentry managed to light and throw a grenade. Da Vira covered it with his body; the blast killed only him and raised no alarm, enabling the expedition to succeed. Fatima won the case: da Vira was posthumously awarded the Order of Courage. Later, [[karmensita-estevos|Carmensita Estevos]], a prostitute from a port brothel, sought recognition as the navigator’s widow and of her infant as his son; the court refused.

## What the heroes say (ch. 5–18)
The heroes avoid the subject. In chapter seven, Carlos brushes it aside: it was all “talk” on Solanta; ask Fatima. He says Juanes was no hero. In chapter nine, the general’s face darkens at the word “Solanta.” The late Juanes’s butler Emil says that the doctor sailed as an ordinary physician and returned a hero, quickly building a practice. In chapter eighteen, Carlos says of Juanes’s death, “It began with da Vira and ended with this.” Of da Vira himself, he says only that he was a sailor but is “gone.”

Alvaro develops a theory: Fatima had an affair with da Vira and bore his child, and the doctor blabbed the secret to her husband. This theory proves incorrect (ch. 28–34).

## What is revealed (ch. 24–34)
In chapter twenty-four, Beatriz reports that the philosopher Liess van Halm claimed to have seen all five heroes in a Well. Further details then emerge. Deserey sends Alvaro his diaries, but not the last one; the heroes’ names disappear from the entries sent for the latest year, while the first diary provided begins a year after Solanta. Alvaro concludes that the general concealed the very first notebook, containing the expedition’s account (ch. 30–32). In chapter twenty-eight, Fatima admits that she is half-Solantan: her mother comes from the island, she grew up there herself and knows the language, and that is why she joined the voyage.

Alvaro sets out the full account in chapter thirty-four, and Fatima confirms it, adding what he did not know. Carlos bought a chart of the approach to Solanta from a Black sailor, showed it to Maduro, and together they devised a way to displace the Muari from the island. The War esfera supplied a platoon of marines “to make it official”; they found an eloquent negotiator who had grown up on Solanta and took a ship’s doctor, although pirate schooners usually managed without one. They invited Rodrigo da Vira as navigator, a master of navigation and a hot-tempered brawler whom Carlos had once outmaneuvered in a debate and against whom the captain held a grudge. During the voyage, da Vira developed a cough; Juanes diagnosed incurable lung sarcoma and told the captain.

Da Vira did not, in fact, die from a grenade: he was alive and well in the fort when the doge arrived to negotiate. Everything went brilliantly and Fatima played her part perfectly, but the doge, stung by humiliation, demanded the man who knew the secret channel. Carlos, who told his wife that “Eliga herself came to my aid,” handed over the navigator. The doge did not execute him but sold him into slavery. Later, in court, Carlos and Fatima constructed the story under which da Vira was declared dead; no one tried to ransom him because they believed him terminally ill. Fatima denies an affair: she cannot stand aggressive brutes and gloated when da Vira landed in a Solantan prison.

Many years later, at da Vira’s demand, van Halm invited the heroes to Last Winter Castle, where they drank the water of misfortune. What followed is described in the articles on [[smert-khuanesa|Juanes’s death]] and [[posledniaya-zima-shturm|the night at Last Winter]].

## What remains unsaid (ch. 34)
The novel does not explicitly describe Deserey’s role in the expedition itself or his involvement in the conspiracy: we know only that he is a “hero of Solanta” and that his diary omits the event. What happened to da Vira after his escape also remains a question. Alvaro deduces that he passed through slavery under the fishermen, then the army, escaped during the Ollandian War, returned to Catalia with a brand on his chest, and seized the castle together with Dangarian deserters.

Da Vira is [[red|Red]], the protagonist of the Dangarian storyline. Everything fits: Red is a Catalian falsely diagnosed with “lung sarcoma” in Laerta; he left behind a child he has never seen; he knows Solantan; and “THOU SHALT NOT KILL” is branded across his chest. At the end of his storyline, Red enters the army. There he becomes the rifleman Killnot in Dietrich’s battery (ch. 2), and later arrives in Tres Molinos as the leader of the six Dangarians who served van Halm. The novel does not state this outright: Alvaro does not know the Dangarian chapters, and the reader pieces the picture together. The author said that Red’s real name could be guessed by someone who knows Spanish.
